# Vrykul Grammar

## Phonology

### Consonant Inventory

### Notable Phonological Features

- **Gj → /ʃ/**: The digraph "Gj" is pronounced as /ʃ/ (sh). Canon: Captain Adams notes "Gj" in "Gjalerbron" is pronounced "Sh" (quest: Dealing With Gjalerbron). This parallels the Norwegian/Gj sound shift.
- **Double consonants**: Attested in names like Skarvald, Sjonnir, suggesting gemination or pre-aspiration as in Old Norse.
- **R sounds**: The language likely uses an alveolar tap or trill [ɾ/r] as in Old Norse, given the frequency of rhotic-heavy names (Ymiron, Hrothgar, Valhalas).
- **Vowel length**: Possibly phonemic, as in Old Norse. Attested in double vowels: "oo" in "Ugglin oo bjorr", "uul" in "Mjor na uul kval!"
- **Consonant clusters**: Heavy initial clusters are common: Vr- (Vrykul), Sk- (Skovald, Skadi, Skarvald), Hl- (Helya), Gj- (Gjalerbron), Mj- (Mjordin), Ym- (Ymiron, Ymirheim).

### Vowel System

Based on attested forms, likely similar to Old Norse:

| | Front | Back |
|---|---|---|
| High | i, y | u |
| Mid | e | o |
| Low | æ | ɑ |

- **Diphthongs**: Attested in "au" (Haustvald), "ou" (possible in "oo"), "ei" (Geirrivif), "ja" (Jotunheim, Mjordin).

### Stress Pattern

- Primary stress appears to fall on the first syllable in most words, consistent with Old Norse/Germanic patterns: **Y**miron, **Ut**garde, **Val**halas, **Sko**vald.

### Phonological Patterns

1. **Gender-correlated word-final vowel**: Feminine names overwhelmingly end in /a/ (~13 of 17 female names: Svala, Helya, Hyrja, Fjola, Hildana, Danica, Agatha, Arthura, Brynja, Daschla, Kyra, Signe, Skyja, Skolla, Valkyra). Masculine names overwhelmingly end in consonants (~10 of 12 male names: Skovald, Hrothgar, Ymiron, Bjorn, Halof, Kreug, Algar, Ingvar). This parallels Old Norse and Germanic naming conventions and may reflect a productive phonological gender marker. **Confidence: High**

2. **Consonant-initial exclusivity**: Every attested Vrykul proper name begins with a consonant or consonant cluster. Not a single name begins with a vowel. Even names like *Eyir* and *Eirich* begin with /ɛɪ/, which may be analyzed as /j/-initial. This is consistent with Old Norse phonotactic preference but appears to be categorical in the Vrykul corpus. **Confidence: High**

3. **/r/ near-universality**: /r/ appears in approximately 75%+ of all attested proper names, in onset (Ragnar, Rodin), medial (Ymiron, Skovald, Hrothgar, Bjarngrim, Ingvar), and coda (Algar, Hrothgar, Skarvald, Varkul) positions. No other consonant approaches this frequency. /r/ is the single most defining consonant of the Vrykul phonological identity. **Confidence: High**

4. **Initial /s/ and /h/ dominance**: /s/-initial and /h/-initial names together account for nearly half of all attested proper names (Sk-, Sv-, St-, Si-, Sj- + Hr-, Ha-, He-, Hy-, Hu-). This creates a distinctive auditory profile: the language "sounds" sibilant and breathy at its onset, consistent with Old Norse phonotactics. **Confidence: Medium–High**

5. **Sonorant-containing medial clusters**: Nearly all attested medial consonant clusters in Vrykul contain a sonorant (/l/ or /r/): -lg- (Valgarde), -ld- (Skarvald, Skovald), -rn- (Bjarngrim), -rv- (Skarvald), -lv- (Nifflevar). Clusters of two obstruents without an intervening sonorant are rare or unattested, suggesting a phonotactic constraint requiring sonorant "bridges" in medial clusters. **Confidence: Medium**

## Morphology

### Nouns

#### The -heim Suffix
- **Meaning**: "home" or "realm" (cognate with Old Norse heimr)
- **Attested in**: Jotunheim, Ymirheim, Stormheim, Helheim
- **Function**: Forms place names indicating a homeland or realm

#### The -jar Suffix
- **Meaning**: "army" or "host" (cognate with Old Norse -jar/jarlar)
- **Attested in**: Ymirjar, Valarjar, Helarjar, Drekirjar
- **Function**: Denotes a collective group or army associated with the root

#### The -bron Suffix
- **Meaning**: Possibly "hold" or "fortress" (uncertain)
- **Attested in**: Gjalerbron
- **Function**: Forms fortress/structure names

#### The -garde Suffix
- **Meaning**: "enclosure" or "keep" (cognate with Old Norse garðr)
- **Attested in**: Utgarde

#### The -korn Element
- **Meaning**: Possibly "chosen" or "kin" (uncertain; cognate with Old Norse -korn as "grain/seed")
- **Attested in**: Winterskorn, Tideskorn, Felskorn

#### Compound Personal Names
- **Bjarngrim**: bjarn (bear) + grímur (mask/helmet) → "Bear-helm" (Icelandic: Bjarngrímur)
- **Geirrivif**: geirr (spear) + vif (wife) → "Spear-wife" (canon confirmed)
- **Bjornharta**: bjorn (bear) + harta (heart) → "Bear-heart" (canon confirmed: "bear heart")
- **Mordvigbjorn**: mord (murder/death) + vig (battle) + bjorn (bear) → compound proper name

### Titles

- **Drottinn**: "Lord" or "leader of a band" (cognate with Old Norse dróttinn)
  - Attested in: Drottinn Hrothgar
- **Thane**: A title of nobility (cognate with Old Norse þegn)
  - Attested in: Thane Banahogg, Thane Ufrang the Mighty
- **Overthane**: Superior thane title
  - Attested in: Overthane Balargarde

### Verbs/Morphology

- Too few verb forms are attested to determine conjugation patterns.
- Imperative forms may be present in battle cries: "Rualg nja gaborr!" (Stand and die!), "Var-jogg gramalch!" (Kill them all!)

### The Val- / Val'- Prefix

- **Val-** appears in multiple terms related to chosen/fallen warriors:
  - **Val'kyr**: "chosen of the spear" or "slaughter-chooser" (cognate with Old Norse valkyrja, from valr "slaughter" + kjósa "to choose")
  - **Valarjar**: "army of the chosen/slain" (valr + ar + jar)
  - **Valhalas**: "hall of the slain" (cognate with Valhǫll/Valhalla)
  - **Valgarde**: "enclosure of the slain/chosen"
  - **Valkyrion**: Place associated with the Val'kyr
  - **Valkyra**: The order of shieldmaidens who aspire to become Val'kyr

### The Ym- / Ymir- Root

- Associated with King Ymiron and the primordial:
  - **Ymiron**: King's name (cognate with Ymir, the primordial giant in Norse myth)
  - **Ymirheim**: "Home of Ymir" — the Ymirjar city in Icecrown
  - **Ymirjar**: "Ymir's army/host"
  - The root likely carries connotations of primordial origin or kingship

### Possessive/Genitive Patterns

- "Bjorns mottar" = "Bjorn's mother" (speculative; parallels Icelandic genitive)
- The -s possessive suffix parallels Old Norse and modern Scandinavian patterns

## Syntax

### Word Order

Very limited evidence. The attested phrases suggest possible VSO or imperative-first patterns:

- "Rualg nja gaborr!" = "Stand and die!" — possibly [verb] [conjunction] [verb]
- "Ugglin oo bjorr!" = "Speak or perish!" — possibly [verb] [conjunction] [verb]
- "Var-jogg gramalch!" = "Kill them all!" — possibly [verb-imperative] [object/pronoun]

### Conjunctions

- **nja**: "and" (in "Rualg nja gaborr!" = "Stand and die!")
- **oo**: "or" (in "Ugglin oo bjorr!" = "Speak or perish!")

These are consistent with Old Norse en/ok (and) and eða (or), though the forms differ.

### Imperative Constructions

Battle cries follow a pattern of imperative + conjunction + imperative:
- "Rualg nja gaborr!" → Stand and die!
- "Ugglin oo bjorr!" → Speak or perish!

Or imperative + object:
- "Var-jogg gramalch!" → Kill them all!
- "Wyrgn ak!" → Sniveling pig! (insult rather than imperative)

## Limitations

- No prose texts exist; only battle cries, proper names, and isolated phrases are attested.
- Verb conjugation, noun declension, and grammatical gender cannot be determined from available evidence.
- The Old Norse parallels provide strong analogical evidence but should not be treated as canon.
- The "slightly different dialect" of Stormheim vrykul is noted in canon but no specific dialectal features are attested.
- Several untranslated phrases (Bolvin umyol marnjar, Folvynn buul hrom onn, etc.) lack any translation or morphological analysis.
