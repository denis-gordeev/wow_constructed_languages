# Vrykul Grammar

## Phonology

### Consonant Inventory

The Vrykul phonological system closely parallels Old Norse/Icelandic:

| | Bilabial | Labiodental | Dental | Alveolar | Palatal | Velar | Glottal |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stops | p b | | t d | | | k g | |
| Fricatives | | f v | θ ð | s | | | h |
| Nasals | m | | | n | | | |
| Approximants | | | | ɾ | j | | |
| Laterals | | | | l | | | |

### Notable Phonological Features

- **Gj → /ʃ/**: The digraph "Gj" is pronounced as /ʃ/ (sh). Canon: Captain Adams notes "Gj" in "Gjalerbron" is pronounced "Sh" (quest: Dealing With Gjalerbron). This parallels the Norwegian/Gj sound shift.
- **Double consonants**: Attested in names like Skarvald, Sjonnir, suggesting gemination or pre-aspiration as in Old Norse.
- **R sounds**: The language likely uses an alveolar tap or trill [ɾ/r] as in Old Norse, given the frequency of rhotic-heavy names (Ymiron, Hrothgar, Valhalas).
- **Vowel length**: Possibly phonemic, as in Old Norse. Attested in double vowels: "oo" in "Ugglin oo bjorr", "uul" in "Mjor na uul kval!"
- **Consonant clusters**: Heavy initial clusters are common: Vr- (Vrykul), Sk- (Skovald, Skadi, Skarvald), Hl- (Helya), Gj- (Gjalerbron), Mj- (Mjordin), Ym- (Ymiron, Ymirheim).

### Vowel System

Based on attested forms, likely similar to Old Norse:

| | Front | Back |
|---|---|---|
| High | i, y | u |
| Mid | e | o |
| Low | æ | ɑ |

- **Diphthongs**: Attested in "au" (Haustvald), "ou" (possible in "oo"), "ei" (Geirrivif), "ja" (Jotunheim, Mjordin).

### Stress Pattern

- Primary stress appears to fall on the first syllable in most words, consistent with Old Norse/Germanic patterns: **Y**miron, **Ut**garde, **Val**halas, **Sko**vald.

### Phonological Patterns

1. **Gender-correlated word-final vowel**: Feminine names overwhelmingly end in /a/ (~13 of 17 female names: Svala, Helya, Hyrja, Fjola, Hildana, Danica, Agatha, Arthura, Brynja, Daschla, Kyra, Signe, Skyja, Skolla, Valkyra). Masculine names overwhelmingly end in consonants (~10 of 12 male names: Skovald, Hrothgar, Ymiron, Bjorn, Halof, Kreug, Algar, Ingvar). This parallels Old Norse and Germanic naming conventions and may reflect a productive phonological gender marker. **Confidence: High**

2. **Consonant-initial exclusivity**: Every attested Vrykul proper name begins with a consonant or consonant cluster. Not a single name begins with a vowel. Even names like *Eyir* and *Eirich* begin with /ɛɪ/, which may be analyzed as /j/-initial. This is consistent with Old Norse phonotactic preference but appears to be categorical in the Vrykul corpus. **Confidence: High**

3. **/r/ near-universality**: /r/ appears in approximately 75%+ of all attested proper names, in onset (Ragnar, Rodin), medial (Ymiron, Skovald, Hrothgar, Bjarngrim, Ingvar), and coda (Algar, Hrothgar, Skarvald, Varkul) positions. No other consonant approaches this frequency. /r/ is the single most defining consonant of the Vrykul phonological identity. **Confidence: High**

4. **Initial /s/ and /h/ dominance**: /s/-initial and /h/-initial names together account for nearly half of all attested proper names (Sk-, Sv-, St-, Si-, Sj- + Hr-, Ha-, He-, Hy-, Hu-). This creates a distinctive auditory profile: the language "sounds" sibilant and breathy at its onset, consistent with Old Norse phonotactics. **Confidence: Medium–High**

5. **Sonorant-containing medial clusters**: Nearly all attested medial consonant clusters in Vrykul contain a sonorant (/l/ or /r/): -lg- (Valgarde), -ld- (Skarvald, Skovald), -rn- (Bjarngrim), -rv- (Skarvald), -lv- (Nifflevar). Clusters of two obstruents without an intervening sonorant are rare or unattested, suggesting a phonotactic constraint requiring sonorant "bridges" in medial clusters. **Confidence: Medium**

## Morphology

### Nouns

#### The -heim Suffix
- **Meaning**: "home" or "realm" (cognate with Old Norse heimr)
- **Attested in**: Jotunheim, Ymirheim, Stormheim, Helheim
- **Function**: Forms place names indicating a homeland or realm

#### The -jar Suffix
- **Meaning**: "army" or "host" (cognate with Old Norse -jar/jarlar)
- **Attested in**: Ymirjar, Valarjar, Helarjar, Drekirjar
- **Function**: Denotes a collective group or army associated with the root

#### The -bron Suffix
- **Meaning**: Possibly "hold" or "fortress" (uncertain)
- **Attested in**: Gjalerbron
- **Function**: Forms fortress/structure names

#### The -garde Suffix
- **Meaning**: "enclosure" or "keep" (cognate with Old Norse garðr)
- **Attested in**: Utgarde

#### The -korn Element
- **Meaning**: Possibly "chosen" or "kin" (uncertain; cognate with Old Norse -korn as "grain/seed")
- **Attested in**: Winterskorn, Tideskorn, Felskorn

#### Compound Personal Names
- **Bjarngrim**: bjarn (bear) + grímur (mask/helmet) → "Bear-helm" (Icelandic: Bjarngrímur)
- **Geirrivif**: geirr (spear) + vif (wife) → "Spear-wife" (canon confirmed)
- **Bjornharta**: bjorn (bear) + harta (heart) → "Bear-heart" (canon confirmed: "bear heart")
- **Mordvigbjorn**: mord (murder/death) + vig (battle) + bjorn (bear) → compound proper name

### Titles

- **Drottinn**: "Lord" or "leader of a band" (cognate with Old Norse dróttinn)
  - Attested in: Drottinn Hrothgar
- **Thane**: A title of nobility (cognate with Old Norse þegn)
  - Attested in: Thane Banahogg, Thane Ufrang the Mighty
- **Overthane**: Superior thane title
  - Attested in: Overthane Balargarde

### Verbs/Morphology

- Too few verb forms are attested to determine conjugation patterns.
- Imperative forms may be present in battle cries: "Rualg nja gaborr!" (Stand and die!), "Var-jogg gramalch!" (Kill them all!)

### The Val- / Val'- Prefix

- **Val-** appears in multiple terms related to chosen/fallen warriors:
  - **Val'kyr**: "chosen of the spear" or "slaughter-chooser" (cognate with Old Norse valkyrja, from valr "slaughter" + kjósa "to choose")
  - **Valarjar**: "army of the chosen/slain" (valr + ar + jar)
  - **Valhalas**: "hall of the slain" (cognate with Valhǫll/Valhalla)
  - **Valgarde**: "enclosure of the slain/chosen"
  - **Valkyrion**: Place associated with the Val'kyr
  - **Valkyra**: The order of shieldmaidens who aspire to become Val'kyr

### The Ym- / Ymir- Root

- Associated with King Ymiron and the primordial:
  - **Ymiron**: King's name (cognate with Ymir, the primordial giant in Norse myth)
  - **Ymirheim**: "Home of Ymir" — the Ymirjar city in Icecrown
  - **Ymirjar**: "Ymir's army/host"
  - The root likely carries connotations of primordial origin or kingship

### Possessive/Genitive Patterns

- "Bjorns mottar" = "Bjorn's mother" (speculative; parallels Icelandic genitive)
- The -s possessive suffix parallels Old Norse and modern Scandinavian patterns

## Syntax

### Word Order

Very limited evidence. The attested phrases suggest possible VSO or imperative-first patterns:

- "Rualg nja gaborr!" = "Stand and die!" — possibly [verb] [conjunction] [verb]
- "Ugglin oo bjorr!" = "Speak or perish!" — possibly [verb] [conjunction] [verb]
- "Var-jogg gramalch!" = "Kill them all!" — possibly [verb-imperative] [object/pronoun]

### Conjunctions

- **nja**: "and" (in "Rualg nja gaborr!" = "Stand and die!")
- **oo**: "or" (in "Ugglin oo bjorr!" = "Speak or perish!")

These are consistent with Old Norse en/ok (and) and eða (or), though the forms differ.

### Imperative Constructions

Battle cries follow a pattern of imperative + conjunction + imperative:
- "Rualg nja gaborr!" → Stand and die!
- "Ugglin oo bjorr!" → Speak or perish!

Or imperative + object:
- "Var-jogg gramalch!" → Kill them all!
- "Wyrgn ak!" → Sniveling pig! (insult rather than imperative)

## Investigations

### Investigation 1: Untranslated Combat Phrases — Morphological Analysis

Six untranslated combat phrases spoken by named Vrykul NPCs permit morphological analysis via Old Norse cognates and cross-phrase comparison.

#### Confirmed Baseline (from officially translated phrases)

| Phrase | Translation | Structure |
|--------|-------------|-----------|
| Rualg nja gaborr! | Stand and die! | V conj V |
| Ugglin oo bjorr! | Speak or perish! | V conj V |
| Var-jogg gramalch! | Kill them all! | V Obj Adj |
| Wyrgn ak! | Sniveling pig! | Adj N (exclamatory) |

Known conjunctions: **nja** = "and", **oo** = "or".

#### Phrase-by-Phrase Analysis

**1. "Haraak foln!"** (Thane Banahogg) — **Medium confidence**

| Word | Proposed Decomposition | Proposed Meaning | ON Cognate |
|------|----------------------|-----------------|------------|
| Haraak | haraak | "Raid! / Attack!" (imperative) | herja (to raid, harry) |
| foln | fol + -n | "the folk / the people" | fólk (folk) + -inn (def. art.) |

The two-word imperative structure parallels *Wyrgn ak!* (Adj N). The -n suffix in *foln* matches the canon-confirmed -inn in *Drottinn*. The same *fol-* root recurs in *Folvynn* (phrase 2), providing independent cross-phrase confirmation.

**2. "Folvynn buul hrom onn!"** (Sjonnir the Ironshaper) — **Low confidence**

| Word | Proposed Decomposition | Proposed Meaning | ON Cognate |
|------|----------------------|-----------------|------------|
| Folvynn | fol + vynn | "Fight/conquer the folk" | fólk (folk) + vinna (to work/fight) |
| buul | ból → buul | "lair / dwelling" | ból (lair, dwelling) |
| hrom | hrom | "claw" | hrammr (claw, talon) |
| onn | onn | "spirit / breath" | andi (spirit) |

The *fol-* root matches phrase 1. *buul* may relate to *uul* in phrase 5 (see below). *hrom* from ON hrammr (claw) fits the combat context of Sjonnir the Ironshaper metaphorically "clawing" with iron.

**3. "Mjor na uul kval!"** (Skadi the Ruthless) — **Medium confidence**

| Word | Proposed Decomposition | Proposed Meaning | ON Cognate |
|------|----------------------|-----------------|------------|
| Mjor | mjor | "more" or imperative "bring" | meir (more) or mœra (make famous) |
| na | na | "and" (reduced nja) | cf. nja (and, confirmed) |
| uul | uul | "wolf" | úlfr (wolf) |
| kval | kval | "torment / anguish" | kvál (torment, agony) |

*kval* is the strongest new morpheme identification — ON kvál = torment/anguish is a direct phonological match fitting Skadi the *Ruthless*. *uul* from úlfr (wolf) is culturally coherent; wolves/worgs are central to vrykul culture. *na* as a reduced form of confirmed *nja* (and) is cross-linguistically common. Proposed reading: "More wolf-torment!" or "Bring wolf-anguish!"

**4. "Mjul orm agn gjor!"** (Ingvar the Plunderer) — **Medium confidence**

| Word | Proposed Decomposition | Proposed Meaning | ON Cognate |
|------|----------------------|-----------------|------------|
| Mjul | mjul | "grind / crush!" (imperative) | mjólva (to grind) |
| orm | orm | "serpent / dragon / enemy" | ormr (serpent, dragon) |
| agn | agn | "terror / fright" | ǫgn (terror, danger) |
| gjor | gjor | "do / make!" (imperative) | gjǫra/gøra (to do, make) |

*orm* is a near-certain identification — ON ormr = serpent/dragon is a direct cognate, and the morpheme appears embedded in the name *Jormungar* (jorm-ungar), providing internal confirmation. *gjor* from ON gjǫra (to do, make) is phonologically excellent but complicated by the attested Gj→/ʃ/ rule — the rule may apply only to proper names or only in certain positions. Proposed reading: "Grind the serpent, make terror!" — a serial verb construction fitting Ingvar the Plunderer's destructive nature.

**5. "Bolvin umyol marnjar"** (General Bjarngrim) — **Low confidence**

| Word | Proposed Decomposition | Proposed Meaning | ON Cognate |
|------|----------------------|-----------------|------------|
| Bolvin | bolv + -in(n) | "the cursed / the curser" | bǫl (evil/curse) + -inn (def. art.) |
| umyol | um + yol | "overwhelm / crush" | um (over/around) + mylja (to crush) |
| marnjar | marn + -jar | "sea-army" | marr (sea) + -jar (army, confirmed) |

This is the only untranslated phrase without an exclamation mark, suggesting declarative rather than imperative mood. If so, the structure may be **SVO** (subject-verb-object), consistent with the only other declarative (*Bjorns mottar haelgond sygar*). The -jar suffix in *marnjar* is canon-confirmed.

**6. "Jarggn olkt!"** (Skarvald the Constructor) — **Low confidence**

| Word | Proposed Decomposition | Proposed Meaning | ON Cognate |
|------|----------------------|-----------------|------------|
| Jarggn | jar + gg + -n | "the [great] army" | -jar (army, confirmed) + augmentive? |
| olkt | olkt | "all" | allt (all, neut. acc.) |

The *jar* component is canon-confirmed as "army." The -n may be the definite article (as in *foln*, *Bolvin*, *Drottinn*). If *Jarggn* = "the great army," then *olkt* = "all" gives "[Rouse] all the army!" — a rallying cry. Alternative: *Jarggn* from garðr (enclosure) → "Fortify all!" — fitting Skarvald the *Constructor*.

#### Cross-Phrase Recurring Morphemes

| Morpheme | Appearances | Type | Confidence |
|----------|------------|------|------------|
| **fol-** | *foln* (#1), *Folvynn* (#2) | Root: "folk/people" | Medium |
| **-n / -inn** | *foln* (#1), *Bolvin* (#5), *Jarggn* (#6), *Drottinn* (confirmed) | Definite article | High (confirmed) |
| **-jar** | *marnjar* (#5), Ymirjar, Valarjar, etc. | "Army/host" | High (confirmed) |
| **na / nja** | *na* (#5), *nja* (confirmed) | Conjunction "and" | Medium (for *na* = reduced *nja*) |
| **uul** | *uul* (#5), possibly *buul* (#2) | "wolf" or related | Medium |
| **orm** | *orm* (#4), embedded in *Jormungar* | "serpent/dragon" | High |

#### Word Order

Imperative phrases are **100% verb-initial** across both confirmed and proposed readings (6 of 6 imperative phrases). The single declarative (*Bjorns mottar haelgond sygar*) shows SV order. The proposed declarative *Bolvin umyol marnjar* would be SVO. This is consistent with **VSO** as base order, with verb-fronting in imperatives producing V1 surface order.

#### Assessment

Two new morphemes reach **High confidence**: **orm** (serpent, ON ormr — direct cognate, internally confirmed by *Jormungar*) and **kval** (torment, ON kvál — direct cognate, semantically precise). One root reaches **Medium confidence**: **fol-** (folk/people, ON fólk — two independent phrase attestations). The word order analysis supports **V-initial imperatives** with possible **VSO** base order, upgrading from the grammar's current "VSO or imperative-first" assessment.

### Investigation 2: The *uul* / *buul* Relationship

Two words in the untranslated combat phrases share the segment /uːl/: *uul* in *Mjor na uul kval!* (Skadi the Ruthless) and *buul* in *Folvynn buul hrom onn!* (Sjonnir the Ironshaper). This analysis evaluates whether they are morphologically related.

#### Evidence Table

| Word | Phrase | Speaker | Current Analysis | ON Cognate |
|------|--------|---------|------------------|------------|
| *uul* | *Mjor na uul kval!* | Skadi the Ruthless | "wolf" (Medium confidence) | úlfr (wolf) |
| *buul* | *Folvynn buul hrom onn!* | Sjonnir the Ironshaper | "lair / dwelling" (Low confidence) | ból (lair, dwelling) |

#### Hypothesis 1: Both from ON úlfr (wolf) — *buul* is a prefixed/inflected form of *uul*

Under this analysis, *buul* = *b-* + *uul*, where *b-* is an unknown prefix. Possible functions for *b-*:
- A verbal prefix: "to be/become wolf-like" (cf. ON *verða* → *b-*?)
- A locative prefix: "place of wolves" (*b-* = dwelling, from ON *bú-* = "to dwell")
- An inflected form: ON úlfr has the plural úlfa; a Vrykul reflex *buul* would require unexplained /b/ insertion

The problem is that ON úlfr does not take a /b/ prefix in any attested Old Norse form. The /b/ onset in *buul* lacks an obvious ON source under this hypothesis.

**Confidence: Very Low.** The /b/ insertion is phonologically unmotivated.

#### Hypothesis 2: Independent words — *uul* from úlfr (wolf), *buul* from ból (lair)

Under this analysis, *uul* and *buul* derive from different ON roots that happen to produce similar Vrykul forms:

- *uul* /uːl/ ← ON úlfr /uːlvɾ/ (wolf): loss of final /vɾ/ and compensatory lengthening of /u/
- *buul* /buːl/ ← ON ból /bɔuːl/ (lair, dwelling): vowel raising /ɔ/ → /u/ and monophthongization /ɔu/ → /uː/

The sound changes are independently motivated: Vrykul frequently drops ON final consonant clusters (cf. *kval* from *kvál*, *orm* from *ormr*), and the long vowel in *buul* matches the ON long diphthong in *ból*. The identical /uːl/ rime is coincidental — two different ON words converged on the same Vrykul phonological shape through regular but different sound changes.

This is the most parsimonious analysis. The semantic fit is also strong: *buul* = "lair/dwelling" fits Sjonnir the Ironshaper's context (forging in a lair), while *uul* = "wolf" fits Skadi the Ruthless (wolf-torment).

**Confidence: Moderate.** The independent-derivation analysis is the simplest and requires no ad hoc sound changes.

#### Hypothesis 3: Common root meaning "predator/danger" with semantic split

Under this analysis, Proto-Vrykul *\*uul* meant "predator/danger," and the form split:
- *uul* specialized to "wolf" (the primary predator)
- *buul* specialized to "lair [of a predator]" (by adding a locative *b-*)

This is typologically unusual. Semantic splitting from a common root into such divergent meanings ("wolf" vs. "lair") would require intermediate steps not attested in the corpus. It also requires *b-* to be a productive locative prefix, which has no other attestation in Vrykul.

**Confidence: Very Low–Low.** Requires unsupported intermediate semantic stages.

#### Assessment

**Hypothesis 2 (independent derivation) is strongly preferred** at **Moderate confidence**. The identical /uːl/ rime in *uul* and *buul* is a coincidental convergence from different ON roots — a phonological accident rather than a morphological relationship. This parallels real-world examples of convergent phonological evolution (e.g., English *bare* /bɛər/ from OE *bær* vs. *bear* /bɛər/ from OE *bera* — identical modern forms from completely different Old English roots).

The key evidence against a relationship is the /b/ onset in *buul*, which has no explanation under any "shared root" hypothesis. Under the independent-derivation analysis, *b-* is simply the inherited initial consonant from ON *ból*.

**Testable prediction:** If additional Vrykul phrases containing /uːl/ are found, they should resolve to either ON úlfr-derivatives (wolf-related) or ON ból-derivatives (dwelling-related), not a single unified root.

### Investigation 3: The *Mj-* Onset — Morpheme or Phonological Pattern?

Two untranslated combat phrases begin with *Mj-*: *Mjor* in *Mjor na uul kval!* (Skadi the Ruthless) and *Mjul* in *Mjul orm agn gjor!* (Ingvar the Plunderer). Additionally, *Mjordin* (elite vrykul warriors) shares this onset. This analysis evaluates whether *Mj-* is a productive morpheme or a common phonological onset.

#### Evidence Table

| Word | Phrase/Context | Current Analysis | ON Cognate |
|------|---------------|------------------|------------|
| *Mjor* | *Mjor na uul kval!* | "more" or imperative "bring" | meir (more) or mœra (make famous) |
| *Mjul* | *Mjul orm agn gjor!* | "grind / crush!" (imperative) | mjólva (to grind) |
| *Mjordin* | Elite warrior caste | — | Possibly from Mjǫllnir (Thor's hammer) or mjór (slender) |

#### Hypothesis 1: *Mj-* is a common Vrykul phonological onset, not a morpheme

The simplest analysis: *Mj-* is simply a permitted syllable onset in Vrykul phonology, like *Sk-*, *Hr-*, *Vr-*, *Gj-*, and *Ym-*. The ON cognates for *Mjor* and *Mjul* are completely different roots:
- *Mjor* ← ON *meir* (more) or *mœra* (make famous)
- *Mjul* ← ON *mjólva* (to grind)

These have no shared semantic content. If *Mj-* were a prefix, stripping it should leave meaningful roots (*-or* and *-ul*), but neither *-or* nor *-ul* has an identifiable ON cognate or independent attestation in Vrykul.

The phonological pattern is consistent with Old Norse, where *mj-* is a common onset: *mjór* (slender), *mjólk* (milk), *Mjǫllnir* (Thor's hammer), *mjór* (narrow). Vrykul preserves this onset distribution from its Norse-inspired phonological template.

**Confidence: High.** This is the simplest and most conservative analysis, supported by the divergent ON etymologies and the broader Vrykul onset-cluster pattern.

#### Hypothesis 2: *Mj-* is a prefix meaning "great/mighty/powerful" (from ON mikill)

If *Mj-* derives from ON *mikill* (great, mighty), it would function as an intensifying prefix:
- *Mjor* = *Mj-* (great) + *-or* (unknown) = "great [or]"
- *Mjul* = *Mj-* (great) + *-ul* (unknown) = "great [ul]"
- *Mjordin* = *Mj-* (great) + *-ordin* = "great warriors"

Problems:
1. **Phonological implausibility**: ON *mikill* /mikil/ does not reduce to *Mj-*. The expected Vrykul reflex would be *Mik-* or *Mikil-* (cf. ON *Mikill* → hypothetical Vrykul *\*Mikil* or *\*Mik-*, not *\*Mj-*).
2. **Semantic problem**: Stripping *Mj-* leaves *-or* and *-ul*, which have no independent meaning. A prefix that only appears before unidentifiable roots is indistinguishable from a phonological onset.
3. **Alternative ON source**: ON *mjór* (slender, small) is phonologically a better match for *Mj-*, but semantically contradicts "great/mighty."

**Confidence: Very Low.** The phonological derivation from *mikill* is not supported.

#### Hypothesis 3: *Mj-* is a deverbal prefix marking intensive/iterative action

If *Mj-* marks intensive or iterative verbal action:
- *Mjul* = intensive "grind" → "crush!" (fits the imperative context)
- *Mjor* = intensive "bring" → "bring more!" (fits Skadi's combative context)

This is typologically plausible — many languages have intensive verb prefixes (cf. German *zer-* in *zerbrechen* = "to smash"). However:
1. No ON cognate supports a Vrykul intensive *Mj-* prefix
2. The sample size is exactly 2, both in imperative contexts — the "intensive" meaning could simply be the imperative mood itself
3. *Mjordin* (a noun, not a verb) would not fit a verbal prefix analysis

**Confidence: Very Low–Low.** Typologically interesting but unsupported by the corpus.

#### Assessment

**Hypothesis 1 (phonological onset) is strongly preferred** at **High confidence**. *Mj-* is a common Vrykul onset cluster, paralleling *Sk-*, *Hr-*, *Vr-*, *Gj-*, and *Ym-*. The divergent ON etymologies for *Mjor* (meir/mœra) and *Mjul* (mjólva) confirm that *Mj-* is not a morphological prefix but a phonological pattern inherited from the Norse-inspired phonological template.

The occurrence of *Mj-* in three independent lexical items (*Mjor*, *Mjul*, *Mjordin*) is consistent with the Vrykul phonotactic preference for onset clusters beginning with a bilabial nasal (/m/) followed by a palatal approximant (/j/) — a combination that produces a forceful, percussive sound fitting the vrykul aesthetic.

**Testable prediction:** If additional *Mj-* initial words are found, their ON etymologies should diverge, confirming phonological rather than morphological *Mj-*.

## Limitations

- No prose texts exist; only battle cries, proper names, and isolated phrases are attested.
- Verb conjugation, noun declension, and grammatical gender cannot be determined from available evidence.
- The Old Norse parallels provide strong analogical evidence but should not be treated as canon.
- The "slightly different dialect" of Stormheim vrykul is noted in canon but no specific dialectal features are attested.
- Morphological analysis of untranslated phrases depends on Old Norse cognate identification; without confirmed translations, all decompositions remain speculative.
