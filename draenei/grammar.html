Draenei Grammar

This document presents grammatical inferences drawn from attested Draenei words and phrases in World of Warcraft. Draenei is a poorly attested language; all rules below are speculative and based on limited data. No native grammar has been published by Blizzard.

Morphology

Compounding

Draenei appears to form words primarily through compounding. Place names and group designations are transparently compound:

  • Draen-ei: draen (“exile/refuge”) + ei (“ones/people”) = “Exiled Ones”
  • Draen-or: draen (“exile/refuge”) + or (“world/land”) = “Exiles' Refuge”
  • Sha'tar: sha (“light”) + tar (“born”) = “Born of Light”
  • Shattrath: shat (“dwelling”) + trath (“light”?) = “Dwelling of Light”

Compounding is right-headed: the final element typically carries the core semantic category (ei → people, or → place, tar → adjective/participle).

Suffixation

-ei (“ones” / “people”): A collective or nominalizing suffix indicating a group of people.

  • Draenei = “Exiled Ones” (draen + ei)
  • Possibly productive for ethnonym formation.

-or (“world” / “land” / “place”): A toponymic suffix indicating a geographic or spatial entity.

  • Draenor = “Exiles' Refuge” (draen + or)
  • Possibly present in Telredor (tel + red + or), but this is uncertain.

Apostrophe Usage

The apostrophe in Draenei orthography appears to mark a morpheme boundary:

  • Man'ari: man + ari — the apostrophe separates the root from a derivational suffix.
  • Sha'tar: sha + tar — the apostrophe separates two compounding roots.
  • Oshu'gun: oshu + gun — the apostrophe separates two morphemes of unknown meaning.
  • A'dal, K'ure: Naaru names; the apostrophe may reflect a phonological feature (glottal stop?) rather than a morpheme boundary. Naaru names may not follow Draenei morphological rules.

The apostrophe may also indicate a phonological process such as vowel elision or a glottal stop at morpheme junctures. Without audio or phonological description from Blizzard, this remains uncertain.

Derivational Patterns

If Man'ari = man + ari, then -ari may be an adjectival or participial suffix meaning something like “those who are [root]” or “characterized by [root].” This would make Man'ari = “those who are wrong/corrupt.”

Alternatively, -ari could be related to the -ei suffix through vowel gradation, but this is highly speculative.

Phonotactic Notes

  • Guttural or back-of-the-mouth consonants are frequent: /k/, /g/, /kh/, /x/. This gives Draenei a perceived harsh or resonant quality.
  • The sequence /ʃa/ appears in multiple words related to light (Sha'tar, Shattrath), suggesting it may be a phonologically stable root.
  • The apostrophe may represent a glottal stop /ʔ/, which would create a phonotactic boundary between vowels or vowel-initial and consonant-final morphemes.

Phonological Patterns

  1. Naaru naming sub-pattern: All five Naaru names (A'dal, K'ure, K'ara, M'uru, O'ros) share a distinctive pattern: a single consonant or vowel + apostrophe + 2-3 character element beginning with a vowel. This is a highly constrained phonological sub-pattern distinct from Draenei compound words. The apostrophe in Naaru names is likely phonological (a glottal stop or prosodic feature) rather than morphological, as the initial elements are too short to be meaningful morphemes. This supports the hypothesis that Naaru names may not follow Draenei morphological rules. Confidence: High
  2. Word-final /r/ in place names: Six place names end in /r/ (Draenor, Karabor, Telredor, Xenedar, Elodor, Aldor), far exceeding other final consonants. This correlates with the -or toponymic suffix but extends beyond it: -ar in Karabor, Xenedar, and Vindicaar suggests a broader pattern of sonorant-final toponymic suffixes. Confidence: Medium
  3. Consonant-initial preference: Of ~25+ attested words, the overwhelming majority begin with consonants (Draenei, Karabor, Shattrath, Telredor, Man'ari, etc.). Vowel-initial words are primarily Naaru names (O'ros) or possible loanwords (Argus). This may reflect a phonotactic constraint against onsetless syllables at word boundaries, consistent with the complex initial clusters the language permits. Confidence: Medium
  4. Apostrophe dual function: The apostrophe serves fundamentally different functions in Naaru names vs. Draenei compound words. In Naaru names, the apostrophe likely marks a phonological feature (glottal stop). In Draenei compounds (Man'ari, Sha'tar, Oshu'gun), it marks a morphological boundary between full morphemes. This dual function must be distinguished in any phonological analysis. Confidence: High
  5. Medial open syllable preference: Medial syllables in Draenei words are overwhelmingly open (ending in vowels): Ka-ra-bor (CV.CV.CVC), Te-lre-dor (CVC.CV.CVC), Vi-n-di-caar (CVC.CV.CVVC). Closed medial syllables occur mainly at morpheme boundaries (Tel-red-or) or in clusters (Shat-trath). This suggests a phonotactic preference for open syllables word-internally, with consonant clusters concentrated at morpheme junctures. Confidence: Medium
  6. Vowel length indication via doubling: Draenei orthography uses vowel doubling to indicate long vowels: Krokuun (double /u/), Vindicaar (double /a/). This suggests vowel length is phonemically contrastive, parallel to the Vrykul vowel length pattern. Confidence: Medium

Investigation 1: Naaru Naming — A Phonological Sub-System

Six Naaru names are attested in the Draenei vocabulary. All six share a highly constrained phonological pattern that is distinct from any Draenei compound word, suggesting that Naaru names constitute a separate phonological sub-system — possibly reflecting the Naaru's non-Draenei origin or their unique mode of communication (chimes and light patterns).

Pattern Description

  1. Initial element: A single consonant or vowel — always exactly one segment.
  2. Apostrophe: Follows the initial element, marking a boundary.
  3. Second element: 2-4 characters beginning with a vowel or approximant.

The pattern can be formalized as: (C/V)'V(C)(V) — where the initial element is maximally one segment, and the second element is vowel-initial.

Hypotheses

Hypothesis A: Naaru names are Draenei words following a special register. Under this hypothesis, the single-segment initial elements (A-, K-, M-, O-) are abbreviations or sacred shortenings of full Draenei morphemes. K-, appearing twice (K'ure, K'ara), would be a prefix or root element. However, no Draenei morphemes are short enough to be reduced to a single segment, making this unlikely.

Hypothesis B: Naaru names are not Draenei at all — they are Naaru self-designations. The Naaru communicate through chimes and light patterns, not vocal speech. Their “names” may be Draenei transliterations of non-linguistic Naaru signals. The constrained pattern (C/V + ' + VC/V) could represent a simplified phonological template that Draenei applied to render Naaru communication into spoken form. The initial single segment may represent a Naaru “chime” or “pulse” that the Draenei rendered as a consonant or vowel.

Hypothesis C: Naaru names are a vestige of the Argus-era Eredar language. Before the Draenei/Eredar split, the Eredar may have had a naming convention for powerful beings using the C'VC pattern. This pattern was preserved only for Naaru names after the split, while everyday vocabulary diverged. The pattern's constraint (single initial segment) would then be an archaic morphological rule no longer productive in modern Draenei.

Confidence: Moderate for the observation that Naaru names constitute a distinct phonological sub-system. Low–Moderate for Hypothesis B (Naaru self-designation transliterated into Draenei phonology). The key evidence is the extreme phonological constraint: all six names fit the (C/V)'V(C)(V) template, which has zero exceptions and zero parallels in Draenei compound words. This constraint is too consistent to be accidental but cannot be explained by any known Draenei morphological rule. Notably, L'ura — the only naaru attested as both Light and dark — follows the identical pattern, confirming that the naming convention is independent of naaru Light/Void state.

Investigation 2: The -ari Suffix and the ered Root Family

The suffix -ari appears in Man'ari (“wrong/corrupt/unnatural”), the Draenei term for Eredar who joined the Burning Legion. With only one attestation, -ari cannot be confirmed as a productive morpheme. However, the ered root family — appearing in Eredar, Eredath, and potentially related forms — provides a broader context for evaluating whether -ari is part of a larger derivational system.

The -ari / -ar / -ei Suffix Comparison

Three Draenei suffixes form nominals from roots:

SuffixAttestationFunctionExample
-ei1 (Draen-ei)Collective: “ones/people”Draenei = “Exiled Ones”
-ari1 (Man'ari)Derivational: “those characterized by [root]”Man'ari = “the corrupt ones”

Possible Relationships

1. -ari and -ar are variants of the same suffix:

If Eredar = ered + -ar and Man'ari = man + -ari, the difference is the final vowel -i. This could reflect:

  • Vowel harmony: -ari appears after low-vowel stems, -ar after non-low stems
  • Number marking: -ari = plural, -ar = singular or collective
  • Register: -ari = pejorative/emphatic, -ar = neutral
  • Different suffixes entirely

The vowel harmony hypothesis is testable: man ends in /n/ (consonant), while ered ends in /d/ (consonant) — no clear vowel-conditioning. The number hypothesis is plausible: Man'ari (plural “the corrupt ones”) vs. Eredar (collective “the Eredar people”), but the semantic distinction is too subtle to confirm with only two examples.

2. -ari, -ar, and -ei form a derivational paradigm:

If all three are related, they could represent vowel-grade alternation of a single nominalizing morpheme:

GradeSuffixFunctionExample
-ei/eɪ/Collective (people)Draenei
-ar/ɑːr/Ethnonym (species)Eredar
-ari/ɑːri/Derivational (characterized by)Man'ari

The semantic progression people → species → characterized-by is typologically plausible (cf. English -er → -ard → -ary). However, the phonological relationship between /eɪ/, /ɑːr/, and /ɑːri/ is not a regular vowel gradation pattern, making this speculative.

3. -ari is related to -ei through morphological derivation:

If -ari = -ar + -i (where -i is a separate morpheme), then Man'ari = man + -ar + -i, with -ar being the same suffix as in Eredar and -i being an additional marker. The final -i could be:

  • A plural marker (making Man'ari = “the [man-ar] ones”)
  • An adjectival marker (making Man'ari = “[man-ar]-like”)
  • A pejorative intensifier

This is the most parsimonious analysis but requires assuming an unattested -i suffix.

The ered Root

The root ered appears in two attested words:

If ered is a genuine root meaning something like “original,” “source,” or “first people,” then Eredar is not an ethnonym but a descriptive term: “the original people” or “the first ones.” This would parallel Draenei (“the exiled ones”) as a self-designation that encodes the speakers' identity — the uncorrupted Eredar chose Draenei to distinguish themselves, while the original name Eredar was retained by those who remained.
