Draenei Grammar

This document presents grammatical inferences drawn from attested Draenei words and phrases in World of Warcraft. Draenei is a poorly attested language; all rules below are speculative and based on limited data. No native grammar has been published by Blizzard.

Morphology

Compounding

Draenei appears to form words primarily through compounding. Place names and group designations are transparently compound:

  • Draen-ei: draen (“exile/refuge”) + ei (“ones/people”) = “Exiled Ones”
  • Draen-or: draen (“exile/refuge”) + or (“world/land”) = “Exiles' Refuge”
  • Sha'tar: sha (“light”) + tar (“born”) = “Born of Light”
  • Shattrath: shat (“dwelling”) + trath (“light”?) = “Dwelling of Light”

Compounding is right-headed: the final element typically carries the core semantic category (ei → people, or → place, tar → adjective/participle).

Suffixation

-ei (“ones” / “people”): A collective or nominalizing suffix indicating a group of people.

  • Draenei = “Exiled Ones” (draen + ei)
  • Possibly productive for ethnonym formation.

-or (“world” / “land” / “place”): A toponymic suffix indicating a geographic or spatial entity.

  • Draenor = “Exiles' Refuge” (draen + or)
  • Possibly present in Telredor (tel + red + or), but this is uncertain.

Apostrophe Usage

The apostrophe in Draenei orthography appears to mark a morpheme boundary:

  • Man'ari: man + ari — the apostrophe separates the root from a derivational suffix.
  • Sha'tar: sha + tar — the apostrophe separates two compounding roots.
  • Oshu'gun: oshu + gun — the apostrophe separates two morphemes of unknown meaning.
  • A'dal, K'ure: Naaru names; the apostrophe may reflect a phonological feature (glottal stop?) rather than a morpheme boundary. Naaru names may not follow Draenei morphological rules.

The apostrophe may also indicate a phonological process such as vowel elision or a glottal stop at morpheme junctures. Without audio or phonological description from Blizzard, this remains uncertain.

Derivational Patterns

If Man'ari = man + ari, then -ari may be an adjectival or participial suffix meaning something like “those who are [root]” or “characterized by [root].” This would make Man'ari = “those who are wrong/corrupt.”

Alternatively, -ari could be related to the -ei suffix through vowel gradation, but this is highly speculative.

Syntax

Word Order

Insufficient data. The three attested phrases are too short and poorly glossed to determine word order:

  • Kehla men'or: two words; no reliable gloss.
  • Pheta thones gamera: three words; no reliable gloss.
  • Dioniss aca: two words; no reliable gloss.

Without translations, no syntactic analysis is possible.

Definiteness, Case, and Agreement

No data available.

Phonology

Consonant Inventory

Based on attested forms, Draenei consonants include:

  • Stops: /d/, /k/, /g/, /t/, /b/, /p/
  • Fricatives: /s/, /ʃ/ (sh), /θ/ or /ð/ (uncertain which) (th), /f/, /x/? (kh)
  • Nasals: /m/, /n/
  • Liquids: /r/, /l/
  • Glottal: /ʔ/ (suggested by apostrophe usage)

Consonant Clusters

Draenei permits complex consonant clusters, particularly in medial and final positions:

  • Shattrath: /ʃtr/, /θr/
  • Auchindoun: /xtʃ/, /nd/
  • Krokul: /kr/

This suggests a syllable structure permitting onsets of up to three consonants.

Vowel Inventory

Observed vowels include /a/, /e/, /i/, /o/, /u/. Diphthongs may be present (e.g., the ai in Man'ari, the ou in Auchindoun).

Stress

No data available.

Place names with heavy final syllables (e.g., Auchindoun, Karabor) may carry stress on the penult or final syllable, but this is pure speculation.

Phonotactic Notes

  • Guttural or back-of-the-mouth consonants are frequent: /k/, /g/, /kh/, /x/. This gives Draenei a perceived harsh or resonant quality.
  • The sequence /ʃa/ appears in multiple words related to light (Sha'tar, Shattrath), suggesting it may be a phonologically stable root.
  • The apostrophe may represent a glottal stop /ʔ/, which would create a phonotactic boundary between vowels or vowel-initial and consonant-final morphemes.
