# Qiraji Grammar

Grammar rules below are **inferred from attested examples only**. The Qiraji language has no attested sentences — the entire corpus consists of proper names, place names, and construct designations from *Classic* (2006) and *Wrath of the Lich King* (2008) content. Patterns are noted where multiple examples support a rule. Speculative extensions are marked [speculative].

## Phonology

### Sound Inventory (Observed from Attested Names)

**Consonants**: b, d, h, j, k, l, m, n, r, s, t, th (/θ/), x (/ks/), z, sh (/ʃ/)

**Vowels**: a, e, i, o, u

### Phonological Patterns

1. **Apostrophe as morpheme boundary**: The *Ahn'qiraj* and *Hive'Ashi* / *Hive'Zora* names use apostrophes to mark morpheme boundaries, consistent with nerubian and Shath'Yar conventions.

2. **Egyptian-inspired phonology**: Names like *Ossirian*, *Anubisath*, and *Ahn'qiraj* evoke Egyptian phonological patterns — heavy use of /ɑ/, /æ/, /ʃ/, /θ/, and sibilant-rich sequences.

3. **Geminate consonants**: The *-naxx* / *-axx* endings in *Kurinnaxx* and *General Rajaxx* show doubled consonants. If Aqir had geminates, qiraji retained them while nerubian simplified (*-nax*, *-ax*). This is one of the few phonological differences that can be posited between the two Aqir-descended languages.

4. **No complex initial clusters**: Unlike nerubian (*Krik'thir*, *Vrrix*, *Krix'vizk*), qiraji names do not feature complex initial consonant clusters. This may reflect:
    - A qiraji sound change that simplified Aqir initial clusters
    - A nerubian innovation that created new clusters
    - Insufficient data (the qiraji corpus is very small)

5. **Sibilant frequency**: High, consistent with nerubian and the Egyptian aesthetic. *Ossirian*, *Kurinnaxx*, *Anubisath* all feature prominent sibilants.

6. **Syllable structure**: Tends toward (C)V(C), simpler than nerubian:
    - CV: *Moam*
    - CVC: *Ashi*
    - CVC.CVC: *Ossirian*
    - CVC.CVC.CVC: *Kurinnaxx*

### Comparison with Nerubian Phonology

| Feature | Qiraji | Nerubian | Inference |
|---------|--------|----------|-----------|
| Initial clusters | Absent | Present (*Krik'thir*, *Vrrix*) | Different development from Aqir |
| Geminate consonants | Present (*-naxx*) | Absent (*-nax*) | Qiraji retained Aqir geminates; nerubian simplified |
| /ks/ (x) frequency | Moderate | High | Both use /ks/, possibly from Aqir |
| Apostrophe convention | Consistent | Extremely consistent | Both inherited from Aqir orthographic tradition |

## Morphology

### Compounding with Apostrophe Boundary

The primary morphological pattern in qiraji is the apostrophe-marked compound:

| Pattern | Examples |
|---------|----------|
| *Ahn-* + suffix | *Ahn'qiraj* |
| English + qiraji element | *Hive'Ashi*, *Hive'Zora* |

The *Ahn'qiraj* compound is the only fully qiraji compound attested. The *Hive'* compounds use an English word as the first element, suggesting the apostrophe boundary convention was productive enough to be applied to mixed-language compounds.

[speculative]: If the compounding pattern is [modifier/prefix] + [head/suffix], then *Ahn'qiraj* = "sacred [place of the] Qiraj" — a modifier-head order, consistent with the right-headed compounding pattern found in nerubian and the titan-forged language family.

### The *Ahn-* Prefix

The prefix *Ahn-* appears in both qiraji and nerubian toponyms:

| Name | Species | Meaning |
|------|---------|---------|
| *Ahn'qiraj* | Qiraji | "Sacred [place of the] Qiraj" or "Ancient Temple" |
| *Ahn'kahet* | Nerubian | "Old Kingdom" (matches in-game designation) |

If *Ahn-* is a shared Aqir morpheme meaning "old/ancient/sacred," it was inherited by both descendant species. This would be the second confirmed Aqir substrate morpheme (after *Azj-* / *Nazj-*).

**Confidence: Low–Medium** for *Ahn-* as a shared Aqir morpheme.

### The *-qiraj* / *-qiraji* Element

The *-qiraj* element in *Ahn'qiraj* may be the source of the English "qiraji" (the species designation). If so:

- *-qiraj* = "the people" or "the servants" in qiraji
- *Ahn'qiraj* = "Sacred Place of the People" or "Ancient Temple of the Servants"
- English "qiraji" (with *-i* plural suffix) = "the qiraj-people"

This parallels nerubian *-nerub* in *Azjol-Nerub*, where the self-designation appears in a place name.

**Confidence: Low.** The connection between *-qiraj* and "qiraji" is plausible but unconfirmed.

### The *-naxx* / *-axx* Suffix

| Name | Suffix |
|------|--------|
| *Kurinnaxx* | *-naxx* |
| *General Rajaxx* | *-axx* |

Two qiraji names end in the *-naxx* / *-axx* pattern. If this is a suffix (meaning "warrior," "hunter," or "elite"), it parallels the nerubian *-nax* / *-ax* pattern:

- Qiraji *-naxx* → nerubian *-nax* (geminate simplification)
- Qiraji *-axx* → nerubian *-ax* (same)

**Confidence: Very Low.** Only two qiraji attestations and three nerubian attestations; the similarity could be coincidental.

## Syntax

- No sentence-level attestation exists
- Word order cannot be determined
- The compounding pattern (prefix + head, with apostrophe boundary) is the only confirmed syntactic observation
- The language appears to follow a modifier-head structure in compounds (inferred from *Ahn-* + *-qiraj*), consistent with right-headed compounding in both nerubian and titan-forged languages

## Relationship to the Aqir Substrate Hypothesis

The qiraji language, along with nerubian, is one of two attested Aqir-descended languages. The emerging Aqir substrate morphemes are:

| Aqir Morpheme | Qiraji Form | Nerubian Form | Naga Form | Meaning |
|--------------|-------------|--------------|-----------|---------|
| *\*Azj-* | — | *Azj-Kahet* | *Nazj-* | "deep/realm" |
| *\*Ahn-* | *Ahn'qiraj* | *Ahn'kahet* | — | "old/ancient/sacred" |
| *\*-naxx* (?) | *-naxx* | *-nax* | — | "warrior/hunter" (?) |
| *\*Anub-* (?) | *Anubisath* | *Anub'arak* | — | "lord/guardian" (?) |

The qiraji-nerubian comparison reveals one potential regular sound correspondence: Aqir *\*-xx* (geminate) → qiraji *-xx* (retained) vs. nerubian *-x* (simplified). If confirmed by additional data, this would be the first regular sound correspondence established between two Aqir-descended languages.

## Limitations

The qiraji corpus is extremely small:
- ~8 personal names
- ~6 place names with potential qiraji etymologies
- **0 attested phrases or sentences**

No verb forms, particles, or syntactic structures are known. The only confirmed morphological patterns are:
1. Apostrophe-marked compounding (high confidence)
2. *Ahn-* prefix (moderate confidence, shared with nerubian)
3. *-naxx* / *-axx* suffix (very low confidence)
