## Investigation: *domanaar* and the *-aar* Suffix

The Midnight expansion introduces the **domanaar**, generals of the Devouring Host. This section analyzes whether the *-aar* ending in *domanaar* reflects ethereal *-daar*, shared Void nomenclature, or coincidence.

### The Three Attestations of *-aar* / *-daar*

| Name | Language | Context | Decomposition |
|------|----------|---------|---------------|
| *Salhadaar* | Ethereal | Nexus-King, former ruler of K'aresh | *Salha-* + *-daar* |
| *domanaar* | Void/Shath'Yar? | Generals of the Devouring Host | *doman-* + *-aar* |

### Hypothesis 1: Ethereal Loanword into Void Nomenclature

If *-daar* = "king/lord/ruler" in the ethereal language, then *domanaar* could be a borrowing with simplification (*-daar* → *-aar*, loss of /d/):
- *domanaar* = *doman-* + *-aar* = "[doman]-lords" = "lords of [doman]"
- The transmission mechanism would be through the Shadowguard ethereals, who serve Xal'atath and the Devouring Host

This is plausible because:
1. The Shadowguard (led by Nexus-King Salhadaar) serve Xal'atath, creating direct ethereal-Void contact
2. If Xal'atath needed a word for "generals" or "lords" in her Devouring Host, she could have adopted the ethereal *-daar* suffix
3. The loss of /d/ (*-daar* → *-aar*) could reflect phonological adaptation from ethereal into Void/Shath'Yar phonology

### Hypothesis 2: Shared Void Nomenclature

The *-aar* ending could be a genuine Void/Shath'Yar morpheme that coincidentally resembles ethereal *-daar*:
- Shath'Yar and ethereal have no confirmed genetic relationship
- The similarity could be coincidental (*-aar* is a simple vowel-heavy suffix)
- Void/Shath'Yar nomenclature is extremely poorly attested, making comparison difficult

### Hypothesis 3: Blizzard Design Convergence

If *-daar* is an Arabic design artifact (as argued in ethereal/grammar.md), then both *-daar* and *-aar* could independently reflect Blizzard's aesthetic for "authoritative" or "commanding" name endings. The Latin-inspired names of the two named domanaar (*Decimus*, *Terminas*) suggest Blizzard drew on classical/Latin aesthetics for Void generals, which is a different design tradition from the Arabic-inspired ethereal names. This weakens the design convergence hypothesis.

### Assessment

| Analysis | Plausibility | Notes |
|----------|-------------|-------|
| Ethereal loanword (*-daar* → *-aar*) | **Low–Medium** | Plausible transmission mechanism via Shadowguard; phonological simplification /d/ loss is natural; but only two data points |
| Shared Void nomenclature (coincidence) | **Medium** | *-aar* is a common syllable; Shath'Yar corpus too small to assess |
| Blizzard design convergence | **Low** | Different design traditions (Arabic vs. Latin) for the two groups; convergence less likely than for same-aesthetic groups |
| *-aar* is the same morpheme as *-daar* with regular /d/ loss | **Low–Medium** | Requires assuming *-daar* is a genuine ethereal morpheme (not just an Arabic design artifact) and that Void nomenclature borrowed it |

**Confidence: Very Low–Low** for a genuine in-universe connection between *-aar* and *-daar*. The transmission mechanism through the Shadowguard is plausible, but the analysis is undermined by the possibility that *-daar* itself is an Arabic design artifact rather than a genuine ethereal morpheme. If *-daar* is not a genuine morpheme, then *-aar* in *domanaar* is most likely coincidental or an independent Void/Shath'Yar element.

## Naaru Names: Cross-Reference with Shath'Yar Naming

The Naaru — beings of Holy Light — consistently use names that follow the **Shath'Yar initial-apostrophe convention** (consonant-apostrophe-vowel sequence). This is notable because Naaru are the antithesis of Old Gods and the Void, yet their names are morphologically indistinguishable from Shath'Yar names. Three interpretations are possible:

1. **Naaru names are genuinely Shath'Yar**: Naaru may have originated from or been named by entities that spoke a precursor to Shath'Yar, and the Light-Void dichotomy postdates the naming tradition.
2. **Naaru names predate Shath'Yar**: The apostrophe-marked convention may be a cosmic/universal naming pattern that both Naaru and Old Gods inherited independently.
3. **Blizzard design convergence**: The apostrophe convention is simply Blizzard's aesthetic for "cosmic/otherworldly" names, applied to both Light and Void entities without in-universe significance.

### Lothraxion

**Lothraxion** is a unique case: a member of the Army of the Light who has been infused with Light energy, serving as "high commander of the Vanguard" in Midnight. His name lacks the apostrophe convention entirely and follows a Latinate morphological pattern (*Loth-* + *-raxion*), more similar to Eredun/Draenei naming (*Loth* as in *Lothar*) than to Naaru or Shath'Yar. He may not be a Naaru at all but a Light-infused being of another race.

### Analysis

| Feature | Naaru Names | Old God Names | Assessment |
|---------|------------|---------------|------------|
| Initial apostrophe | All 6 names | N'Zoth, C'Thun, Y'Shaarj | Shared pattern |
| C'V(C) structure | All 6 names | Most Old God names | Shared pattern |
| Apostrophe as morpheme boundary | Likely | Likely | Shared pattern |
| Semantic content of initial element | Unknown | Unknown (possibly entity-designator) | Cannot assess |
| Relationship to Void | Opposed | Aligned | Paradoxical if names are Shath'Yar |

**Confidence: Low–Medium** that Naaru names reflect genuine Shath'Yar naming conventions rather than Blizzard design convergence. The systematic parallel (all Naaru use the convention) is stronger than a coincidence would predict, but the Light-Void opposition makes in-universe transmission unlikely unless the naming convention predates the Light-Void split or is a universal cosmic naming pattern.
