# Kalimag Grammar

## Phonology

### General Characteristics

- Described as a primal, forceful language
- The language likely varies by elemental plane — fire, water, earth, and air elementals may have distinct phonological registers (inferred from lore)
- Apostrophes appear in some names (Al'Akir, Ko'gun, Sho'ravon) but not others (Ragnaros, Neptulon, Therazane), suggesting they mark specific morpheme boundaries rather than being a universal feature (inferred)

### Syllable Structure

- Proper names show varied phonological patterns:
  - Heavy consonant clusters: Ragnaros, Therazane
  - Apostrophe-marked compounds: Al'Akir, Ko'gun, Sho'ravon
  - Flowing syllables: Neptulon, Thunderaan
  - Short ritual words: Reth, Tas, Ko, Ma

### Phonological Patterns

1. **Dialect-specific phonotactics: Ignan CVC vs. Aquan polysyllabic**: The Molten Core runes (Ignan dialect) are uniformly CVC monosyllables: *Kress*, *Mohn*, *Blaz*, *Zeth*, *Mazj*, *Koro*, *Theri*. In contrast, the Aquan phrases show polysyllabic flowing forms: *kilagrin*, *tadrom*, *aasrugel*, *korsul*. This suggests a fundamental phonotactic split: Ignan favors harsh, compact monosyllables (consistent with the "forceful" description), while Aquan favors longer, vowel-richer forms (consistent with water's flowing quality).

2. **Reduplication as a grammatical device**: "Reth Reth Reth" = "Burn burn burn" shows complete reduplication for emphatic imperative plural. The phrase "Ma krin drinor zoln drinor" shows partial reduplication where "drinor" appears twice with "zoln" intervening, suggesting reduplication may be a productive grammatical device marking distributive, iterative, or emphatic meaning.

3. **Fricative-final word preference**: Many Kalimag words end in fricatives: -sh (*Cyruush*, *Fahss*), -s (*Ragnaros*, *Kress*, *Sulfuras*, *aasrugel*, *korsul*), -th (*Zeth*), -z (*Blaz*). This contrasts with the elven languages (where names end in vowels or sonorants) and may reflect the "primal, forceful" quality — fricatives provide a sustained, breath-heavy word ending that could mimic elemental sounds (hissing fire, rushing wind).

4. **/r/ as a cross-dialect lexical constant**: The phoneme /r/ appears in virtually every attested Kalimag word across all dialects: *Ragnaros*, *Therazane*, *Reth*, *aer'rohgmar*, *drinor*, *korsul*, *Arathar*, *Rethfuras*. While Eredun is noted for rhotic prominence, Kalimag's /r/ density is comparable but distributed differently — /r/ appears in ritual words, names, and functional vocabulary alike, suggesting /r/ is lexically pervasive across all word classes.

5. **CV functional word template**: The short functional/grammatical words follow a strict CV template: *ma* (1st person pronoun), *ko* (locative/interrogative), *tas* (demonstrative?), *tu* (particle). This contrasts with the CVC ritual words (*Reth*, *Kress*, *Blaz*) and the polysyllabic content words (*shodru'ga*, *kilagrin*). The three-way phonological split — CV grammar words, CVC ritual words, polysyllabic content words — suggests Kalimag encodes word class phonologically: shorter = more grammatical, longer = more lexical.

## Morphology

### Apostrophe Compounding

- **Al'Akir** = Al + Akir → possibly "lord" + "wind" or similar (uncertain)
- **Ko'gun** = Ko + gun → possibly "hammer" + "fire" or "firelord" (uncertain)
- **Sho'ravon** = Sho + ravon → possibly "great" + "annihilation" (uncertain)
- **Suz'ahn** = Suz + ahn → (uncertain decomposition)
- **Shodru'ga** = Shodru + ga → (uncertain decomposition; -ga may be a suffix)
- This parallels the compounding seen in Shath'Yar, Taur-ahe, and other WoW languages (inferred)

### Possible Name-Forming Suffixes

- **-os** appears in Ragnaros; (Neptulon has -on which may be related) — possibly a divine or lordly suffix (uncertain)
- **-an** appears in Thunderaan, Therazane — possibly an elemental name suffix (uncertain)
- These could also reflect different elemental phonological preferences rather than morphological markers (uncertain)

### Possible Pronoun "Ma"

- "Ma" appears in two attested phrases: "Ma reth bromo zoln kilagrin..." and "Ma krin drinor zoln drinor"
- If the Curse of Tongues translation "I am from the elemental plane!" is accurate, "ma" may be a first-person pronoun "I" (Low–Medium confidence)
- This would make Kalimag one of the few WoW constructed languages with an attested pronoun

### Possible Verb "Krin"

- "Krin" appears in multiple attested phrases: "Tas krin ko. Tas krin tu roath ko krin?" and "Ma krin drinor zoln drinor" and "Ma krin kan aasrugel korsul"
- May be a verb form (copula or auxiliary) (Low confidence)

### Possible Lexical Item "Reth"

- "Reth" appears in "Reth Reth Reth" (confirmed = "Burn burn burn") and "Rethfuras" (a fiery weapon from Ahune)
- "Reth" likely means "burn" or "fire" in the Ignan dialect (Medium confidence)
- This is one of the strongest morpheme identifications in Kalimag

### Possible Elemental Lexical Fields

The names of the Elemental Lords may encode their elemental domain (inferred):

- **Ragnaros**: "Rag-" may relate to fire/raging (uncertain)
- **Al'Akir**: "Akir" may relate to wind/air (uncertain)
- **Neptulon**: "Nept-" may relate to water (uncertain, possibly a nod to Neptune)
- **Therazane**: "Ther-" may relate to earth (uncertain)

These are highly speculative etymologies and may simply be proper names without internal morphology.

## Syntax

### Attested Sentences

1. **Reth Reth Reth** = "Burn burn burn" (imperative plural)
   - Simple repetition suggests emphatic or imperative construction
   - Triple repetition may be a ritual or invocational pattern

2. **A gi kras tor shodru'ga bromo** = "I am from the elemental plane!"
   - If "ma" ≈ "I" and this Curse of Tongues translation maps word-for-word: "a" may = "I", "gi" may = "am", "kras" may = "from", "tor" may = "the", "shodru'ga" may = "elemental", "bromo" may = "plane"
   - However, Curse of Tongues transforms language into Demonic before translation, so word boundaries may not map directly
   - Low confidence in word-by-word analysis

3. **Tas krin ko. Tas krin tu roath ko krin?** = (Asking about hearthstone binding)
   - The repetition of "Tas krin" suggests a question pattern
   - "ko" may be a question particle or locative marker (uncertain)

4. **Ma reth bromo zoln kilagrin dra ma zoern tu ko fraht ko tadrom Ma krin drinor zoln drinor Ma krin kan aasrugel korsul** = (Water elemental speech, untranslated)
   - "Ma" appears twice, possibly as first-person pronoun
   - "krin" appears twice, possibly as verb
   - "zoln" appears twice, possibly as a conjunction or adverb
   - "ko" appears twice, possibly as a particle

### Word Order

- Insufficient data to determine basic word order
- The phrases above suggest a possible SOV or SVO pattern but this is highly speculative

## Dialectal Variation

- Lore confirms four dialects of Kalimag: Ignan (fire), Aquan (water), Terran (earth), Auran (air)
- Each dialect corresponds to an elemental plane (confirmed — RPG sources, Shadows & Light pg. 143)
- Elemental conglomerates speak two or more dialects (confirmed)
- Draenor has its own variant of Kalimag, with the Burning Blade clan using a runic language similar to Ignan (confirmed — Flamebender's Tome quest)
- The dialects may differ significantly enough to be considered separate languages, with "Kalimag" as a collective term (uncertain)

## Writing System

- Kalimag can be written in runic form (confirmed — Frostmourne's pedestal runes)
- The runes on Frostmourne's dais translate to a full sentence: "Whosoever takes up this blade shall wield power eternal. Just as the blade rends flesh, so must power scar the spirit."
- Ice Stones (associated with Ahune) also bear runes that may be Kalimag
- The Tempest Caller crown has Kalimag runes of binding etched into it

## Limitations

Kalimag grammar is largely unreconstructed. While the expanded corpus provides several attested phrases (some with confirmed translations), the word-by-word analysis of most phrases remains speculative. The identification of "reth" = "burn" is the strongest morpheme-level finding. The four-dialect structure is confirmed by lore but the differences between dialects are unattested. The Frostmourne inscription proves Kalimag has a full writing system capable of expressing complex sentences, but the original Kalimag text of the inscription is not recorded — only the Common translation survives.
