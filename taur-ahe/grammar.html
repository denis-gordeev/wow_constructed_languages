Multi-Apostrophe Compounding

Some words use multiple apostrophes, suggesting recursive or tripartite compounding:

  • Chouk'taga'loush — Spirit Walker incantation (untranslated)
  • Nag'grada'baash — Spirit Walker incantation (untranslated)
  • Ish-ne-alo por-ah — full greeting phrase (two compounds joined by space)

This suggests that apostrophe-compounding is productive at multiple levels, and that phrases can consist of multiple compounded words.

Prefix "An-"

  • "An" may function as a definite article or celestial honorific prefix (inferred)
  • Appears in An'she ("the Sun") and Mashan'she ("the Loom of the Earth Mother") — note: in Mashan'she, "she" recurs but "An" does not appear as a separate prefix
  • An alternative analysis: "she" may mean "celestial body" or "eye of the Earth Mother" (inferred)
  • Too few examples to confirm (uncertain)

Suffix "-ahe"

  • Appears in "Taur-ahe" and may denote "speech," "language," or "tongue" (inferred)
  • No other attested examples to confirm (uncertain)

Suffix "-a"

  • May denote a plural or collective noun (inferred) from "Shu'halo" (the Tauren) vs "Taur-ahe" (the language)
  • Alternatively a stem form without suffix (uncertain)

Potential Morpheme "Ish-"

  • Appears in multiple words: Isha Awak ("Deep Doom"), Ishamuhale ("Speartooth"), Ish-ne-alo (greeting phrase)
  • May relate to "deep," "dark," or "shadow" (inferred from Isha Awak = "Deep Doom")
  • Low–Medium confidence

Potential Morpheme "-mani"

  • Appears in Lakota'mani ("Earthshaker") and Awak'mani (untranslated)
  • May mean "hand," "force," or "shaker" (inferred)
  • Low confidence

Word Order

  • Insufficient data to determine basic word order (SOV, SVO, etc.)
  • The three attested phrases suggest a pattern where modified nouns precede or follow their modifiers, but this is highly speculative

Writing System

  • Taur-ahe is at least partially written using pictograms (confirmed — The Writ of History)
  • The "Writ of History" is a tauren artifact with pictographic writing
  • No alphabetic or syllabic writing system is attested

Dialectal Variation

  • "There are many different forms and dialects of Taur-ahe, all differing based on tribe and location" (confirmed — Traveler, pg. 54)
  • The specific differences between dialects are not attested
  • The Grimtotem tribe may have distinct vocabulary or pronunciation (inferred from cultural separation)

Limitations

Taur-ahe grammar remains largely unreconstructed. While the expanded corpus provides 18+ confirmed translations and 3 attested phrases, no verb paradigm, case system, or detailed syntax can be established. The compounding pattern via apostrophe is the best-attested grammatical feature. All morpheme decompositions beyond confirmed translations are speculative.
