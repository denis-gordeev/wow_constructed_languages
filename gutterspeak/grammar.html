Gutterspeak Grammar

Overview

Gutterspeak is classified as a separate language in World of Warcraft game mechanics (Forsaken characters have Gutterspeak as their racial language), but in lore it is essentially a dialect or register of Common with no independently attested grammar. The Forsaken were human citizens of Lordaeron before their undeath, and they retain their native language.

Confirmed Linguistic Features

Relationship to Common

  • Gutterspeak shares its grammar entirely with Common (confirmed by lore)
  • Any grammatical rules that apply to Common apply to Gutterspeak
  • The differences are lexical, stylistic, and pragmatic rather than structural (inferred)

Dwarven and Thalassian Influence

  • Gutterspeak is described as "a lower form of Common that uses little (very little) bits of Dwarven and Thalassian" (confirmed — World of Warcraft Battle Chest Guide)
  • This suggests that the pre-Forsaken "Gutterspeak" (the black market/rogues' tongue) incorporated small amounts of Dwarven and Thalassian vocabulary
  • The specific Dwarven or Thalassian borrowings are not attested
  • This makes Gutterspeak technically a mixed register rather than a pure dialect of Common

Black Market Origins

  • Gutterspeak "evolved in the shady underground of black markets and rogues' guilds as the tongue spoken by people of ill-repute" (confirmed)
  • When the Forsaken took the Undercity, they adopted this pre-existing underground language as their official language
  • This means Gutterspeak predates the Forsaken as an organized speech variety

Phonology

Gutterspeak has no independently attested phonology. As a register of Common with minor Dwarven and Thalassian influence, its phonological features would be derivable from the source languages:

  1. Total phonological opacity: Gutterspeak is unique among WoW constructed languages in having no unique phonological material. Its existence as a “language” is entirely game-mechanical — it was created to prevent cross-faction communication, not developed as a constructed language. This makes it the only WoW language defined purely by exclusion (not-Common) rather than by positive phonological content.
  2. Confirmed mixed substrate: Common + “little bits of Dwarven and Thalassian” (confirmed — World of Warcraft Battle Chest Guide). Any phonological features would be derivable from the source languages: from Common, likely Germanic/Romance phonotactics; from Dwarven, possible guttural consonants and /θ/; from Thalassian, possible sibilant features and liquid-heavy syllables.
  3. Language comprehension loss as phonological evidence: Dan Golthas's quote (“they drove me out of the village, screaming in a language I could no longer understand”) suggests the Forsaken transformation may alter phonological perception. If Forsaken cannot understand Common they once spoke, this implies either altered auditory processing, cognitive language faculty damage, or a sociolinguistic shift where Common speakers modified their speech when addressing Forsaken.

Potential Distinguishing Features

Archaic Retention

  • The Forsaken may retain older forms of Common that have fallen out of use among the living (inferred)
  • As citizens of Lordaeron — an ancient kingdom — their speech may preserve formal or archaic constructions (inferred)

Morbid Lexical Substitution

  • Forsaken speakers may substitute morbid or death-related terms for common expressions (inferred from characterization)
  • Greetings and farewells may have uniquely Forsaken formulations (inferred, though none are specifically attested)

Register Differences

  • Forsaken speech patterns may be more terse, cynical, or darkly humorous than living Common speakers (inferred from NPC dialogue characterization)
  • This is a pragmatic difference, not a grammatical one

Language Loss

  • Dan Golthas, a Forsaken NPC, states: "When I clawed my way out of the grave, I thought my family would welcome me with open arms. Instead, they drove me out of the village, screaming in a language I could no longer understand."
  • This suggests that some Forsaken lose the ability to understand Common after their transformation
  • The RPG (non-canon) states that Forsaken "have not lost the ability to understand Common, but just never choose to speak it under normal circumstances"
  • The contradiction between these sources is unresolved in canon

Syntax

  • Identical to Common (confirmed by lore)
  • No unique syntactic structures are attested

Limitations

Gutterspeak has no independently attested grammar. It is confirmed to be a lower form of Common with traces of Dwarven and Thalassian influence. The Dwarven and Thalassian borrowings are not specifically attested. Any grammar beyond "identical to Common with minor lexical influence" is speculative.
