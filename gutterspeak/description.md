# Gutterspeak — Description

## Speakers

Gutterspeak (known in-game as "Forsaken") is the language of the Forsaken, the undead who broke free from the Lich King's control and established their own nation under Sylvanas Windrunner. It is spoken by the Forsaken of the Undercity and by undead across Azeroth. In practice, virtually all Forsaken also speak Common, and Gutterspeak is not a distinct language in any meaningful linguistic sense.

## History

The Forsaken were once human citizens of the kingdom of Lordaeron. When the Lich King's plague killed and raised them as undead, they retained their language — Common. When Sylvanas freed a portion of the Scourge and founded the Forsaken, they adopted the pre-existing underground speech known as Gutterspeak as their official language.

### Pre-Forsaken Origins

Gutterspeak predates the Forsaken. It "evolved in the shady underground of black markets and rogues' guilds as the tongue spoken by people of ill-repute" (confirmed — *World of Warcraft Battle Chest Guide*). When the Forsaken took the Undercity, they adopted this language of social rejects as their own — fitting for a people who had themselves been rejected by the living.

### The Language Barrier

In WoW lore, Forsaken can still understand and speak Common. However, they never speak it under normal circumstances. Some Forsaken, like Dan Golthas, claim to have "forgotten" Common: "When I clawed my way out of the grave, I thought my family would welcome me with open arms. Instead, they drove me out of the village, screaming in a language I could no longer understand."

The RPG (non-canon) reconciles this by stating that Forsaken "have not lost the ability to understand Common, but just never choose to speak it under normal circumstances" (*Horde Player's Guide*). The real-world explanation is that Blizzard introduced Gutterspeak to prevent cross-faction harassment, after Forsaken players in the beta could speak Common and communicate with Alliance characters.

## Dialectal Variation

Gutterspeak's variation is limited because it is fundamentally a Common dialect, but three sociolectal registers can be distinguished:

- **Pre-Forsaken Gutterspeak** — the original black market and rogues' guild register that predates the Forsaken. This was a covert speech variety used for secrecy in underground trade, characterized by Dwarven and Thalassian loanwords for illicit goods, coded references, and deliberate obfuscation. It functioned as an anti-language — a speech variety designed to exclude outsiders and resist comprehension by authority figures.
- **Forsaken Gutterspeak** — the adopted official language of the Forsaken, layered over the pre-existing underworld register. The Forsaken expanded Gutterspeak from a niche criminal jargon into a communal identity marker. New domains emerged: undeath, plaguecraft, and apothecary work. The **Royal Apothecary Society** likely developed a technical sub-register for alchemical and plague research, potentially drawing on Dwarven scientific vocabulary and Thalassian magical terminology — the two attested non-Common influences on Gutterspeak.
- **Dark Ranger sociolect** — Dark Rangers, originally high elves raised as undead, may contribute a distinct Thalassian-influenced register to Gutterspeak. Their elven linguistic background could produce heavier Thalassian borrowing than the general Forsaken population, particularly in military and archery terminology.

Beyond these registers, Gutterspeak remains structurally Common. The Forsaken's isolation from the living limits the natural drift that would produce true dialectal divergence, and the absence of any attested unique grammar or phonology confirms that Gutterspeak has not diverged structurally from its Common substrate.

### Cross-Faction Communication

Since *Legion*, cross-faction communication has become increasingly possible:
- Night elf and blood elf demon hunters both speak Demonic
- Void elves can speak Thalassian, communicating freely with blood elves
- Pandaren was unified into a single cross-faction language
- The Gutterspeak/Common barrier remains, however

## Confirmed Linguistic Character

Gutterspeak is described as "a lower form of Common that uses little (very little) bits of Dwarven and Thalassian" (confirmed — *World of Warcraft Battle Chest Guide*). This makes it technically a mixed register rather than a pure dialect of Common.

## Cultural Context

- Forsaken speech is characterized by dark humor, cynicism, and a preoccupation with death and decay (observed in NPC dialogue).
- The "Dark Lady" title for Sylvanas is the most prominent Forsaken-specific linguistic feature.
- Forsaken may use archaic or formal phrasing that reflects their origins as Lordaeron citizens (inferred).
- The Forsaken's isolation from the living may have led to some lexical drift, but this is not attested in canon.
- The Royal Apothecary Society and its work on the New Plague may have generated technical vocabulary, but none is recorded as uniquely Gutterspeak.
- The Forsaken's relationship with the Horde introduced them to Orcish and Trolls, but no borrowing is attested.

## Relationship to Other Languages

- **Common**: Gutterspeak is a register or dialect of Common with traces of Dwarven and Thalassian influence.
- **Dwarven**: "Little bits" of Dwarven vocabulary are confirmed to exist in Gutterspeak, though specific borrowings are not attested.
- **Thalassian**: "Little bits" of Thalassian vocabulary are confirmed to exist in Gutterspeak, though specific borrowings are not attested. This likely reflects the underground trade connections between rogues and elven communities.
- **Lordaeron Common**: The Forsaken's original dialect, likely a northern or formal register of Common.
- No other language relationships exist. The Forsaken are linguistically human.

## Reliability Notes

- **Gutterspeak is not a distinct language in any structural linguistic sense.** It is Common with cultural styling and trace Dwarven/Thalassian influence.
- No unique grammatical structures or phonological features are attested for Gutterspeak in canon.
- The classification of Gutterspeak as a separate language is primarily a game mechanic to allow Forsaken players to communicate privately.
- The confirmed Dwarven and Thalassian influence is an important nuance — Gutterspeak is not purely Common, but the specific non-Common elements are unattested.
- The name "Gutterspeak" itself may not be used within the lore as an in-universe term; it appears primarily in game-mechanic contexts.
- The most reliable observation about Gutterspeak is that it is predominantly Common, spoken by the dead, with minor non-Common influence from its black market origins.
