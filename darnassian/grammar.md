# Darnassian Grammar

Grammar rules below are **inferred from attested examples only**. Darnassian is not a fully constructed language; Blizzard provided limited phrases. Patterns are noted where multiple examples support a rule. Speculative extensions are marked [speculative].

## Morphology

### Compounding

Darnassian makes heavy use of compounding. Morphemes are concatenated to form complex words, particularly in names and titles.

**Pattern: Modifier + Head**

- Kal + dorei = Kaldorei ("Star" + "Children of" = "Children of the Stars")
- Quel + dorei = Quel'dorei ("Noble" + "Children of" = "Children of Noble Birth")

The `-dorei` suffix is a productive morpheme meaning "children of" or "people of," comparable to an ethnonym-forming suffix.

**Pattern: Element + Drassil (World Tree Names)**

- Tel + drassil = Teldrassil ("Earth" + "Crown" = "Crown of the Earth")
- Nor + drassil = Nordrassil ("Heavens" + "Crown" = "Crown of the Heavens")

The element `dor`/`drassil` means "crown." The variant forms may reflect phonological conditioning or an older root pattern.

**Pattern: Honorific Compounds (Title Formation)**

- Shan'do = "Shan" (honored) + "do" (teacher/elder) = "Honored Teacher"
- Thero'shan = "Thero" (beloved) + "shan" (student/learner) = "Beloved Student"

Note: The morpheme `shan` appears in both titles with apparently different meanings. This is analyzed as two distinct morphemes that happen to share a form (homophony), or as a relational term whose specific gloss depends on its position in the compound.

### Apostrophe Usage

Apostrophes mark morpheme boundaries within compound words:

- Quel'dorei = Quel + dorei
- Shan'do = Shan + do
- Thero'shan = Thero + shan
- Ru'shem = Ru + shem
- Tor ilisar'thera'nal = ilisar + thera + nal (internal boundaries in a multi-morpheme word)

Apostrophes appear primarily in:
1. Two-morpheme compounds (Quel'dorei, Shan'do)
2. Multi-morpheme verbal/nominal complexes (ilisar'thera'nal)

They do **not** appear in all compounds — `Kaldorei`, `Teldrassil`, `Nordrassil` lack apostrophes. The distribution may reflect:
- Phonological criteria (apostrophe after certain vowel-consonant patterns)
- Historical spelling conventions
- Etymological transparency (apostrophe used where morpheme boundaries are less obvious in pronunciation)

### Hyphen Usage

Hyphens connect elements in phrasal constructions:

- Elune-adore ("Elune" + "be with you" — deity name + blessing verb)
- Andu-falah-dor ("Let" + "balance" + "be restored" — three-element phrase)
- Fandu-dath-belore ("Who" + "goes" + "there" — three-element question)

Hyphens appear to mark **syntactic phrase boundaries**, while apostrophes mark **morphological word-internal boundaries**. This is a consistent distinction.

## Syntax

### Imperative Constructions

Two imperative formulas are attested:

1. **Andu + [complement]**: Andu-falah-dor ("Let balance be restored")
2. **Tor + [complement]**: Tor ilisar'thera'nal ("Let our enemies beware")

Both `Andu` and `Tor` function as jussive/imperative particles meaning "let." This suggests Darnassian may differentiate types of imperative (hortative vs. directive, or formal vs. informal), but the sample is too small to confirm.

**Direct command form**: `Ash karath` ("Do it / Make it so") — a separate imperative construction without a jussive particle, possibly a direct second-person command.

### Interrogative Formation

`Fandu-dath-belore?` ("Who goes there?") is the only attested question. `Fandu` may be an interrogative pronoun ("who"), but this is [speculative] given only one example.

### Word Order

The extremely limited sample prevents firm conclusions about word order. Observed patterns:

- **Imperative + Object/Complement**: Andu-falah-dor (Let + balance + be-restored), Tor ilisar'thera'nal (Let + enemies + beware)
- **Modifier + Head in compounds**: Kal-dorei, Quel-dorei, Tel-drassil, Nor-drassil
- **Deity name precedes blessing**: Elune-adore

The modifier-head pattern in compounds might suggest a head-final tendency, but this cannot be confirmed for full sentences.

### Possession / Genitive

`Zin-Azshari` = "The Glory of Azshara." The name "Azshara" is appended directly after "Zin" (glory) with a hyphen, suggesting a postposed genitive or appositional construction: "Glory-[of]-Azshara." This is [speculative] — the hyphen may simply join the two proper nouns.

## Phonology

### Sound Inventory (Observed)

**Consonants**: d, f, h, k, l, m, n, r, s, t, th (/θ/), v, x, z, sh (/ʃ/)

**Vowels**: a, e, i, o, u

### Phonological Patterns

- Syllable structure tends toward (C)V(C) — e.g., Kal, dor, Tel, Nor, Shan, do
- /ʃ/ is common (Shan, shem, Asha)
- /θ/ appears in `Thero`, `thera'nal` — possibly an aspirated or dental fricative
- Liquids /l/ and /r/ are frequent, especially in names (drassil, ilisar, falah)
- Vowel sequences are relatively rare; most syllables have a single vowel nucleus
- The consonant cluster /dr/ in `drassil` and /nd/ in `Andu` are the most complex onset clusters observed

### Name Phonology

Place and personal names in Darnassian show a distinct melodic quality:
- Frequent liquid + sibilant combinations (drassil, Azshari, Nazjatar)
- Alternating open and closed syllables (Da-rnas-sus, Tel-dras-sil)
- A preference for sonorant-heavy word endings (-il, -ei, -or, -an)

## Limitations

The corpus is extremely small (~25–30 items). Many "words" are isolated from single contexts, making it impossible to determine:

- Whether word order is free or fixed
- How case, number, or tense are marked (if at all)
- The full range of pronominal forms
- Whether `Andu` and `Tor` are truly synonymous or carry different nuances
- The relationship between `thera` (in ilisar'thera'nal = "enemies beware") and `Thero` (in Thero'shan = "beloved student") — whether these are homophones or a single morpheme with context-dependent meaning
