# Tol'vir Grammar

All grammatical inferences are based on the attested corpus in `vocabulary.md`. The corpus is extremely small (three phrases, one confirmed noun, three partially glossed drill commands, and approximately 25 untranslated proper names). **All grammatical claims below are highly speculative and should be treated as hypotheses, not established facts.**

## Phonology

### Consonant Inventory

Based on attested words, the following consonants are observed:

| | Bilabial | Alveolar | Palatal | Velar | Uvular/Glottal |
|---|----------|----------|---------|-------|----------------|
| Stops | p, b | t, d | | k, g | |
| Fricatives | | s, z | ʃ (sh) | | h |
| Affricates | | | tʃ (ch), dʒ (j) | | |
| Nasals | m | n | | | |
| Liquids | | l, r | | | |

### Vowel Inventory

The five cardinal vowels are all attested: /a e i o u/. The vowel /a/ is by far the most frequent, consistent with Egyptian-inspired phonology.

### Syllable Structure

Observed words tend toward (C)V(C) syllable patterns. Most roots are two or three syllables:

- CV.CVC: *Kam-ses*, *Kha-lesh*
- CVC.CV: *Mur-kash*
- V.CV.CVC: *A-khe-net*
- CV.CV: *Or-sis*, *Sa-hket*
- CV'.V(C): *Vir'naal*, *Mar'at* (apostrophe marks morpheme boundary)

### Stress

**Penultimate stress is the dominant pattern** for words of three or more syllables, confirmed by IPA transcriptions:

- /rɑmˈkɑhɛn/ (Ramkahen), /nɛˈfɜrsɛt/ (Neferset), /ɑˈmɑθɛt/ (Amathet), /feɪˈɔrɪs/ (Phaoris), /nɑˈkoʊtɛp/ (Nahkotep) — penultimate stress
- /ˈɔrsɪs/ (Orsis), /ˈkɑmsɛs/ (Kamses) — initial stress (exceptions, possibly due to morphological factors)

For two-syllable words, final stress is universal: /tɛˈkɑn/ (Tekahn), /huˈsɑm/ (Husam), /bɑˈrim/ (Barim), /kɑˈdɛʃ/ (Kadesh), /sɑˈtɛk/ (Satekh).

The unified generalization: **stress falls on the penultimate mora**, which for two-syllable words equals the final syllable, and for three+ syllable words equals the penultimate. The exceptions (Orsis, Kamses) may reflect morphological factors (monomorphemic roots or suffix-driven stress shift).

This resolves the uncertainty about stress patterns noted in earlier versions. The penultimate pattern is consistent with Egyptian-inspired phonology and distinguishes Tol'vir from Vrykul and Dwarven (which use initial stress).

### Apostrophe Usage

The apostrophe (') appears in:
- *Vir'naal*, *Vir'sar*, *Mar'at* — likely marks morpheme boundaries within place names
- *Al'burq*, *Alra'ed* — likely marks morpheme boundaries within weapon names

The apostrophe may represent:
1. A morpheme boundary (most likely)
2. A glottal stop
3. Both simultaneously

The *Vir'* element shared by *Vir'naal* and *Vir'sar* (both water features) supports the morpheme boundary interpretation.

### Phonological Patterns

1. **Penultimate stress**: For three+ syllable words, stress falls on the penultimate syllable (~78% of attested forms). For two-syllable words, stress falls on the final syllable (= penultimate mora). See Stress section above.

2. **No initial consonant clusters**: Every attested Tol'vir word begins with a single consonant or a single vowel. No word begins with a consonant cluster. This is a sharp contrast with Vrykul (Vr-, Sk-, Hl-) and Dwarven (Gr-, Br-, Sk-) and constitutes a major typological distinction among the titan-forged languages. Even "kh" in *khopesh* represents a single phoneme (/x/ or aspirated /kʰ/), not a cluster.

3. **Vowel /a/ overwhelming dominance**: /a/ is present in approximately 95%+ of Tol'vir words and is typically the most frequent vowel within any given word. No other vowel comes close. This is consistent with Egyptian-inspired phonology and is more extreme than in any other titan-forged language.

4. **Voiceless stop word-final codas (-t, -p)**: Approximately two-thirds of words ending in consonants end in /t/ or /p/ (voiceless alveolar or bilabial stops). The *-et*/*-ep* suffix pattern accounts for many of these, but even beyond the morphological explanation, the phonological tendency toward voiceless coronal stops in coda position is striking and parallels Egyptian phonotactics.

5. **Vowel-initial words are common**: Approximately 34% of Tol'vir words begin with vowels (*Amathet*, *Akhenet*, *Ankhaten*, *Orsis*, *Osadi*, *Abari*, etc.). This is dramatically different from Vrykul (~0%), Dwarven (~0%), and Draenei (~20%), and correlates with the absence of initial consonant clusters. Tol'vir permits onsetless syllables freely, while the other titan-forged languages strongly prefer consonant onsets.

6. **Post-vocalic /x/ (*kh*) pattern**: The sequence /kh/ (likely /x/ or /kʰ/) appears in approximately six Tol'vir words (*Ramkahen*, *Akhenet*, *Ankhaten*, *Nahkotep*, *khopesh*, *Sahket*), creating a distinctive "aspirated" or "breathy" phonological quality that parallels Egyptian /ḥ/ (pharyngeal fricative) and /x/ (velar fricative).

7. **Three-syllable name optimum**: Three-syllable names are the statistical mode (~60% of all names), with two-syllable names as the secondary peak (~25%) and four+ syllable names rare (~15%). This may reflect a phonological word-size constraint.

## Morphology

### Nominal Morphology

**No inflectional morphology is attested.** There are no observed case markers, number markers, or gender markers on nouns.

#### Potential Suffixes

Several recurring word-final elements suggest possible suffixes, but the corpus is too small to confirm any:

| Ending | Examples | Proposed Function | Certainty |
|--------|----------|-------------------|-----------|
| -en | Ramkahen, Ankhaten, (Akhenet?) | Place/settlement | Very Low |
| -et | Amathet, Akhenet, Sahket, Khintaset, Tahret | Locative suffix | Very Low |
| -ep | Hamatep, Nahkotep, Tebhotep | Possibly related to Egyptian *-hotep* ("pleased") or independent suffix | Very Low |
| -set | Neferset | Unknown; possibly *-et* with *s* from root | Very Low |

**The alternation between -et and -ep** is notable. If these represent the same suffix, the alternation may be phonologically conditioned (e.g., *-ep* after certain consonants). If they are different suffixes, *-ep* may be a title element (cf. *-hotep*) while *-et* may be locative. The data is insufficient to distinguish these possibilities.

#### Potential Prefixes

| Prefix | Examples | Proposed Function | Certainty |
|--------|----------|-------------------|-----------|
| Vir'- | Vir'naal, Vir'sar | Water/river-related | Low |
| Al'- | Al'burq | Definite article "the" (cf. Arabic) | Low–Medium |

### Verbal Morphology

**No verbal morphology is attested.** The three drill commands (*Kadesh*, *Osadi*, *Satekh*) are likely verb imperatives based on their combat context, but no paradigm (conjugation, aspect marking, etc.) can be inferred from three forms.

- *Kadesh* — imperative "swing/strike" (?)
- *Osadi* — imperative "swing/strike again" (?) or a different type of strike
- *Satekh* — imperative "block/parry" (?)

The difference in syllable count and structure between *Kadesh* (2 syllables), *Osadi* (3 syllables), and *Satekh* (2 syllables) may reflect:
1. Different verb roots with no morphological relationship
2. The same root with different affixes (unlikely given the phonological diversity)
3. Completely unrelated words for different combat actions

### Compounding

Several proper names appear to be compounds:

- *Vir'naal* = *Vir* + *naal* (water features)
- *Vir'sar* = *Vir* + *sar* (water features)
- *Ankhaten* = *Ankh* + *aten* (if following Egyptian pattern: "life" + "sun disk")

The apostrophe consistently marks compound boundaries in attested forms.

## Syntax

**No syntactic information is attested.** The single sentence *Achusenmen osadi djahi kahun Ammunae* is untranslated and cannot be parsed.

If the sentence follows the same word order as the drill phrase (*Kadesh Osadi Satekh* = verb-verb-verb), this might suggest verb-initial order, but three combat commands in sequence do not constitute evidence for general word order.

Possible word orders for *Achusenmen osadi djahi kahun Ammunae*:
- VSO (verb-subject-object) — common in Egyptian and Semitic languages, which inspired Tol'vir naming
- SOV — common cross-linguistically
- Any other order

The name *Ammunae* at the end of the sentence may be a proper noun (the Halls of Origination keeper), possibly serving as subject, object, or vocative.

## Writing System

The Tol'vir language is written using **hieroglyphs**. This is confirmed by multiple sources:

- Wowpedia: "The written language apparently consists of hieroglyphs."
- Warcraft Wiki: "It is written using hieroglyphs."
- The Tol'vir Hieroglyphic archaeology item confirms the existence of a hieroglyphic script.
- Hieroglyphs decorate obelisks and temples throughout Uldum.

The hieroglyphs combine **logographic and pictographic elements** (per the "Languages of World of Warcraft" overview article), meaning some symbols represent whole words while others represent concepts pictorially. No complete transcription or decipherment of the hieroglyphs has been published.

## Egyptian Comparison

The Tol'vir naming system is clearly inspired by ancient Egyptian, but the relationship is **design inspiration**, not in-universe borrowing. Key parallels:

1. **Khopesh**: A direct reference to the Egyptian *ḫpš* (sickle sword). This is the one confirmed Tol'vir word that has a confirmed real-world Egyptian cognate.
2. **Pharaonic titles**: "Pharaoh," "Sun King" echo Egyptian rulership.
3. **-hotep names**: *Tebhotep*, *Nahkotep* echo Egyptian *-hotep* names (Amenhotep, Imhotep).
4. **Place naming**: *Ankhaten* closely resembles *Akhenaten*.
5. **Amun/Ammon**: Both *Ammunae* and *Ammon* reference the Egyptian god Amun.

However, these are aesthetic choices by Blizzard's writers, not evidence that Tol'vir **is** Egyptian. The drill words (*Kadesh*, *Osadi*, *Satekh*) do not carry their Egyptian meanings in the Tol'vir context.

## Open Linguistic Investigations

### Investigation 1: The *-et*/*-ep* Suffix Alternation — Locative vs. Honorific?

The place names and personal names of Uldum show a recurring pattern of *-et* and *-ep* endings:

| Name | Ending | Category | Preceding Vowel |
|------|--------|----------|-----------------|
| Amathet | -et | Tribe name / place | e |
| Akhenet | -et | Settlement / fields | e |
| Sahket | -et | Wasteland | e |
| Khintaset | -et | Mountaintop ruin | e |
| Tahret | -et | Farm / dynasty name | e |
| Hamatep | -ep | Gate (place) | e |
| Nahkotep | -ep | Person (Sun King) | e |
| Tebhotep | -ep | Person (pharaoh) | e |
| Ammantep | -ep | Person (general) | e |

**Key observation**: *-et* appears exclusively in place/tribe names (5 attestations), while *-ep* appears in both personal names (3 attestations) and one place name (*Hamatep*, a gate). This distribution suggests a functional split.

**Four hypotheses**:

| Hypothesis | Description | Strengths | Weaknesses |
|------------|-------------|-----------|------------|
| **H1: Distinct suffixes** — *-et* = locative, *-ep* = honorific/title | *-et* marks place names and tribal designations; *-ep* (possibly reduced from *-hotep*) marks personal names with an honorific element | Clean functional split for 8/9 names; parallels Egyptian *-hotep* as a title component | *Hamatep* (a gate) is problematic — either named after a person or *-ep* is not restricted to personal names |
| **H2: Phonologically conditioned allomorphy** | *-et* and *-ep* are the same suffix with /t/ ~ /p/ alternation conditioned by the preceding consonant or morphological context | Both endings share the /e/ vowel and voiceless stop coda; phonologically similar | No clear phonological conditioning environment — all precede with /e/; the /t/ ~ /p/ alternation is not motivated by any known Tol'vir phonological rule |
| **H3: *-ep* = *-e* + *-p* (compound suffix)** | *-ep* contains a base locative *-e* plus a personal-name marker *-p*; *-et* = *-e* + *-t* (locative marker) | Would explain the /e/ vowel in both and the place/person split | Purely speculative; no independent evidence for *-p* or *-t* as personal/locative markers |
| **H4: Blizzard design convention** | *-et* and *-ep* reflect Blizzard's Egyptian naming aesthetic, not genuine Tol'vir morphology; the functional split is coincidental | Tol'vir names are heavily Egyptian-inspired; the *-hotep* connection is an acknowledged design source | The clean functional split (5/5 place = *-et*, 3/4 personal = *-ep*) is unlikely to be coincidental |

**Assessment**: H1 (distinct suffixes with functional split) is the most linguistically coherent analysis, strengthened by the Egyptian *-hotep* parallel for *-ep*. The critical counterexample is *Hamatep* — a gate, not a person. If *Hamatep* is "the Gate of Hamatep" (named after a person), H1 holds cleanly. If *Hamatep* is simply a place name ending in *-ep*, H1 is weakened.

**Confidence: Low–Moderate** for the functional split hypothesis. Nine data points with a clean 5/4 distribution is suggestive but not conclusive. The Egyptian design confound is severe — Blizzard may have independently chosen *-et* endings for places and *-hotep*-derived endings for people without creating genuine Tol'vir morphology. Testable: if new Tol'vir personal names end in *-et* (not *-ep*), H1 is falsified.

### Investigation 2: The *Vir'*- Prefix — Water/River Classifier

Two Uldum place names share the initial element *Vir'*-:

| Name | Root Element | Feature Type | Description |
|------|-------------|-------------|-------------|
| Vir'naal | *naal* | River system | Major river, lake, dam, delta — the primary water system of Uldum |
| Vir'sar | *sar* | Oasis | Oasis in northwestern Uldum |

Both are water features, supporting *Vir'*- as a water/river-related prefix. The root elements may specify the type of water feature:

| Root | Proposed Meaning | Evidence |
|------|-----------------|----------|
| *naal* | "flowing water" / "river" | Vir'naal is the major flowing river system of Uldum |
| *sar* | "still water" / "pool" / "oasis" | Vir'sar is an oasis — still water in a desert |

**Three hypotheses**:

| Hypothesis | Description | Confidence |
|------------|-------------|------------|
| **H1: *Vir'*- = "water" generic classifier** | *Vir'*- is a generic water classifier, like English "water-" in "waterfall," "waterway"; the root specifies the type | **Low–Moderate** — two attestations, both water features; semantically coherent |
| **H2: *Vir'*- = "river" specifically** | *Vir'*- means "river" specifically; *Vir'naal* = "river [naal]" (tautological or specifying), *Vir'sar* = "river [sar]" (an oasis fed by the river) | **Low** — if *Vir'* = "river," then *Vir'naal* is redundant unless *naal* specifies a quality of the river |
| **H3: *Vir'*- = proper name element** | *Vir'*- is not a classifier but part of a proper name with no independent morphemic status; the water-feature connection is coincidental | **Low** — the semantic coherence of both being water features makes coincidence unlikely |

**Cross-linguistic comparison**: No other titan-forged language has a confirmed water/river classifier prefix. Darnassian uses *Aran*/*-naar* for settlements, and Zandali uses *-watha* for settlements (with a grammaticalization chain from "cleanse" verb). If *Vir'*- is confirmed, it would be the first confirmed hydrological classifier in any WoW language.

**Confidence: Low–Moderate** for *Vir'*- as a water/river classifier. Only two attestations, but both are unambiguously water features and the semantic decomposition (*naal* = flowing, *sar* = still) is internally coherent. The Egyptian design confound applies — *Vir'naal* may simply echo Egyptian *Neilos* (Nile) phonologically. Testable: if new Tol'vir water features lack the *Vir'*- prefix, the classifier hypothesis is weakened.

### Investigation 3: The *Al'-* Element — Definite Article or Noun Phrase Marker?

The two Fists of the Heavens (legendary fist weapons crafted by the weaponsmith Irmaat) share the initial element *Al'*-:

| Name | Proposed Decomposition | Proposed Root Meaning |
|------|----------------------|----------------------|
| Al'burq | *Al'*- + *burq* | "lightning/flash" (cf. Arabic *al-barq*) |
| Alra'ed | *Al* + *ra'ed* (or *Alra* + *ed*) | "thunder" (cf. Arabic *al-ra'd*) |

**The Arabic parallel**: Both names closely parallel Arabic definite-article constructions:
- *Al'burq* ≈ Arabic *al-barq* = "the lightning/flash"
- *Alra'ed* ≈ Arabic *al-ra'd* = "the thunder"

If *Al'*- = "the" (definite article), this would be the first confirmed function word in Tol'vir, and the first confirmed definite article in any WoW language.

**Four hypotheses**:

| Hypothesis | Description | Confidence |
|------------|-------------|------------|
| **H1: *Al'*- = definite article "the"** | Genuine Tol'vir grammatical morpheme, parallel to Arabic *al-*; *Al'burq* = "the lightning," *Alra'ed* = "the thunder" | **Low–Moderate** — phonological and semantic match with Arabic is striking; would be typologically unusual for a Titan-descended language (no other titan-forged language has a confirmed definite article) |
| **H2: *Al'*- = noun phrase marker (not specifically definite)** | *Al'*- marks a noun phrase or name construction without specifically encoding definiteness; could be a naming prefix like *Uld-* in Titan | **Low** — two attestations with different apostrophe placement (*Al'*- vs. *Al-*) suggest different morphological structures |
| **H3: Blizzard Arabic borrowing** | *Al'burq* and *Alra'ed* are direct Arabic borrowings by Blizzard's writers for aesthetic purposes; *Al'*- is not a Tol'vir morpheme | **Moderate–High** — the names are transparently Arabic in origin; Blizzard has used real-world language aesthetics throughout Tol'vir naming; the Uldum zone is explicitly Egyptian/Arabic-themed |
| **H4: Grammaticalized borrowing** | *Al'*- originated as a Blizzard Arabic borrowing but has been grammaticalized as a Tol'vir definite article within the in-universe language system; this would parallel how real languages borrow articles (e.g., English borrowing Norman French article patterns) | **Very Low–Low** — requires assuming Blizzard's writers intentionally created a grammatical morpheme, which is unlikely given the aesthetic-driven naming approach for Tol'vir |

**Apostrophe distribution note**: *Al'burq* has the apostrophe after *Al'* (before *burq*), while *Alra'ed* has the apostrophe after *Alra* (before *ed*). If *Al'*- = "the" in both, the different apostrophe placement is problematic:
- *Al'burq* = *Al'* + *burq* — apostrophe marks article-noun boundary
- *Alra'ed* = *Al* + *ra'ed* — no apostrophe after *Al*; apostrophe marks internal morpheme boundary in *ra'ed*

This inconsistency weakens H1. If *Al'*- is the same morpheme in both names, the apostrophe should mark the same boundary.

**Confidence: Low–Moderate** for the definite article hypothesis. The Arabic parallel is compelling but may reflect Blizzard's design aesthetic rather than genuine Tol'vir grammar. The inconsistent apostrophe placement is a significant counterargument. The most likely explanation remains H3 (Blizzard Arabic borrowing for aesthetic purposes), with the definite article interpretation being a secondary possibility at Low–Moderate confidence.

### Investigation 4: The *Kadesh*/*Osadi*/*Satekh* Drill Commands — Morphological Analysis

The three combat drill commands from the Engraved Scimitar Hilt provide the only Tol'vir words with partial semantic content beyond proper names:

| Word | IPA | Gloss | Syllables | Structure |
|------|-----|-------|-----------|-----------|
| Kadesh | /kɑˈdɛʃ/ | "swing" (imperative) | 2 | CV.CVC |
| Osadi | /oˈsɑdi/ | "swing again" (imperative) | 3 | V.CV.CV |
| Satekh | /sɑˈtɛk/ | "block" (imperative) | 2 | CV.CVC |

**Phonological comparison**: *Kadesh* and *Satekh* share the same syllable structure (CV.CVC) and both contain the voiceless palato-alveolar fricative /ʃ/ or velar stop /k/ in coda position. *Osadi* is structurally different — three syllables, vowel-initial, no coda fricatives.

**Four morphological hypotheses**:

| Hypothesis | Description | Evidence For | Evidence Against |
|------------|-------------|-------------|-----------------|
| **H1: Unrelated verb roots** | Each word is an independent, monomorphemic verb root with no morphological relationship; the different structures reflect different verb classes | No shared morphemes are identifiable; the three words have different syllable structures | *Kadesh* and *Satekh* share the /-Vdɛʃ/ vs. /-Vtɛk/ pattern (penultimate /ɛ/ + voiceless coda), which could reflect a verb template |
| **H2: *-esh*/*-ekh* verb suffix** | *Kadesh* = *Kad-* + *-esh* and *Satekh* = *Sat-* + *-ekh*; *-esh* and *-ekh* are verb endings (perhaps imperative mood markers), while *Kad-* and *Sat-* are the verb roots | Both disyllabic commands have a CV root + VCVC suffix structure; the suffix vowel is /ɛ/ in both; *Osadi* would be an irregular form | Only 2 data points for the suffix pattern; *-esh* and *-ekh* differ significantly in their coda consonant; no way to confirm the root-suffix boundary |
| **H3: *-sh* imperative suffix** | *Kadesh* = *Kade-* + *-sh* and *Satekh* = *Sate-* + *-kh*; the final consonant (*-sh* / *-kh*) is an imperative mood marker while *Kade-* and *Sate-* are verb stems | Final consonant distinguishes the two action types: *-sh* = offensive action (swing), *-kh* = defensive action (block); /ʃ/ and /x/ are both voiceless fricatives (potential allomorphs) | Only 2 data points; *Osadi* lacks a final fricative (if *-sh*/*-kh* are imperative markers, *Osadi* should also end in a fricative); the semantic opposition (offensive vs. defensive) is speculative |
| **H4: Template pattern with vocalic ablaut** | All three commands share a CV-dominant template with penultimate /ɛ/ or /ɑ/; the vowel grade may encode grammatical information (e.g., /ɛ/ = singular action, /ɑ/ = repeated action) | *Kadesh* (/ɛ/) = single swing, *Osadi* (/ɑ/) = repeated swing, *Satekh* (/ɛ/) = single block | Only 3 data points; the vowel distribution could be coincidental; *Osadi* is trisyllabic while the others are disyllabic, complicating the template analysis |

**Assessment**: H1 (unrelated verb roots) is the most parsimonious analysis given the extremely limited data. The structural similarity between *Kadesh* and *Satekh* (both CV.CVC with /ɛ/ in the penultimate syllable) is notable but could reflect a general Tol'vir phonological word-shape preference rather than a verb-specific template. H3 (imperative suffix) is the most interesting if confirmed — a final-fricative imperative marker would be typologically unusual and would provide the first confirmed verbal morphology in any titan-forged language.

**Confidence: Very Low–Low** for any morphological analysis. Three words with partial glosses is insufficient to establish a paradigm. The analysis is offered as a testable framework: if additional Tol'vir verb forms are discovered, they can be evaluated against these four hypotheses.
