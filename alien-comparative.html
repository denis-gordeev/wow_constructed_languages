Alien Languages of Azeroth

A comparative analysis of the five “alien” languages of World of Warcraft: Shath’Yar (Old Gods and their servants), Nerubian (spider-people of Azj-Kahet), Qiraji (insectoid servants of C’Thun in Silithus), Ethereal / Broker (energy beings from K’aresh), and Hara'ni (Haranir, ancient subterranean bat-folk of Khaz Algar). These languages share no confirmed genetic relationship to the elven or titan-forged language families (with the notable exception of Hara'ni, now confirmed as a deeply divergent branch of the elven/troll language family), but exhibit striking parallels in orthographic conventions (especially apostrophe-marked morpheme boundaries) and, in the case of nerubian and qiraji, a potential shared Aqir substrate.

Methodological caveat: The attested corpus for all five languages is extremely small. None has any attested sentences — the entire corpus for each consists of proper names and place names. Shath’Yar has a few opaque phrases without translations. All morpheme decompositions and reconstructed forms are provisional. Forms marked in purple are inferred; forms marked in gray italics have uncertain glosses. Confidence ratings: High, Low–Moderate, Very Low.

Key distinction: Shath'Yar, nerubian, and qiraji are connected through the Old God → Aqir chain. The ethereal language is genetically unrelated — ethereals originate from K'aresh, a separate world — but shares surface features (apostrophe-marked compounding, sibilant preference) that may reflect Blizzard design aesthetics or convergent typological development. Hara'ni is now confirmed as a deeply divergent branch of the elven/troll language family — the Haranir are described as "not really elves, not really trolls" but are now known to be related to the elven/troll lineage rather than being an independent language isolate — and shares the medial apostrophe compounding pattern with nerubian, qiraji, and ethereal. These are the first confirmed semantic glosses for any Hara'ni morphemes.

Apostrophe Patterns

The most striking shared feature across all five languages is the use of apostrophes to mark morpheme boundaries. However, the structural position and function of the apostrophe differ significantly between Shath'Yar and the other four languages.

Pattern 1: Initial Apostrophe (Shath'Yar dominant)

In Shath'Yar, the apostrophe typically separates a single initial consonant from the remainder of the name. This suggests the initial consonant is itself a morpheme (possibly a prefix or determiner).

Key insight: The two apostrophe patterns are structurally different and may have different origins. Shath'Yar uses apostrophes to mark prefix boundaries (single initial consonant + root), while nerubian/qiraji/ethereal use them to mark compound boundaries (two content morphemes). If the Aqir inherited apostrophe-marking from Shath'Yar (via Old God creation), the function shifted from prefix-marking to compound-marking during the transmission.

Midnight additions: The Midnight expansion (2026) introduced two further apostrophe-marked compounds outside the five core languages: Hash'ey (Zandali title meaning "unifier") and Mor'duun (leader of Twilight's Blade). Both follow the medial apostrophe compound pattern, reinforcing that Blizzard treats the apostrophe as a general alien/exotic marker across multiple language families.

Key Phonological Observations

  1. The /ks/ (x) phoneme is diagnostic of Aqir-descended languages (nerubian, qiraji) but absent from both Shath'Yar and ethereal. Its high frequency in nerubian names may reflect the chitinous clicking sounds of arachnid vocalization, or a Blizzard aesthetic choice for harsh insectoid names.
  2. Ethereal phonology is distinctly softer than the other three languages. The absence of /ks/, /θ/, and complex initial clusters, combined with high /ʃ/ frequency and simple CV/CVC syllable structure, creates a whispery, shimmering quality appropriate for energy beings.
  3. Geminate patterns diverge: qiraji retains Aqir geminates (-naxx), nerubian simplifies them (-nax), and ethereal may have independently developed geminates (-ff- in Shaffar). Shath'Yar geminates are unclear due to the small corpus.
  4. The /θ/ (dental fricative) appears in Shath'Yar, nerubian, and qiraji but not in ethereal. It also appears in several titan-forged languages (Vrykul, Earthen) and Darnassian, suggesting it may be a deep Azerothian areal feature rather than inherited from any single source. Its absence from ethereal is consistent with ethereal being native to K'aresh, not Azeroth.

Transmission Hypotheses

The shared apostrophe convention across all four languages requires explanation. Three broad hypotheses are evaluated:

Hypothesis 1: Blizzard Design Aesthetic

Apostrophe-marked names are a standard fantasy convention for "alien" or "ancient" names. Blizzard applied this convention consistently to all non-titan-forged, non-elven species without implying in-universe linguistic connections. Notably, Hara'ni — now confirmed as an elven/troll branch — still uses the convention, suggesting Blizzard treats apostrophes as a genre marker for "ancient/exotic" regardless of genetic classification. Assessment: High plausibility as at least a contributing factor.

Hypothesis 2: Old God → Aqir → Nerubian/Qiraji Transmission

Shath'Yar (Old God language; initial apostrophe = prefix boundary)
    ↓ (Old Gods created the Aqir)
Aqir (inherited apostrophe convention; shifted to medial = compound boundary)
    ↓ (Aqir diverged)
    │├– Nerubian (retained medial apostrophe compounding)
    │├– Qiraji (retained medial apostrophe compounding)
    └– [Substrate in Nazja via naga/Old God contact]

Assessment: Medium–High plausibility for the Shath'Yar → Aqir → nerubian/qiraji chain. The ethereal language sits outside this chain.

Hypothesis 3: Convergent Development

Multiple alien languages independently developed apostrophe-marked compounding because their phonological systems all feature morpheme boundaries at points of phonotactic transition. Assessment: Medium plausibility. Most likely, the truth combines Hypothesis 1 (Blizzard aesthetic) and Hypothesis 2 (in-universe transmission for Aqir-descended languages).

Nerubian Scroll Text: First Attested Sentence

The only attested nerubian-language text — writing on a nerubian scroll — provides the first and only constructed-language sentence from any of the four "alien" languages:

"Unhhh-tikk-a-shisss. Kka shir. Na-l bkah a-shisss. Kreesh kik ik ta. Sohk-a tik hiss a-k-k-k."
Key finding: The scroll text uses hyphens to mark grammatical morpheme boundaries, while proper names use apostrophes for compound boundaries. This dual orthographic system is unique among the four alien languages and suggests nerubian writing distinguishes between lexical compounding and inflectional morphology.

Key Observations

  1. Repeated *-shisss* / *a-shisss*: The element *-shisss* appears twice, suggesting grammatical agreement, parallel structure, or a refrain pattern.
  2. Sibilant dominance confirmed: The text is dominated by sibilants (*shisss*, *Kreesh*, *hiss*, *shir*), confirming that sibilant-heavy phonology is genuine, not merely a naming convention.
  3. Linking vowel *-a-*: The sequences *tikk-a-shisss* and *Sohk-a* suggest a linking vowel /a/ that connects morphemes within words — a feature not visible in proper names.
  4. Short phrases: Five short phrases separated by periods; may reflect incantation, ritual text, or formal register rather than everyday speech.

Confidence: Very Low for any specific gloss; High that this is genuine nerubian-language text with regular morphological structure.

The *-ar* Suffix: Nerubian Faction Designation

The suffix *-ar* appears in five nerubian faction/group names and one major place name, suggesting it may be a productive collective/faction-designation morpheme meaning "brood" or "swarm."

The TWW attestation Nerub-ar Palace confirms native (non-Scourge) usage, ruling out the hypothesis that *-ar* is merely a Scourge-era designation.

Confidence: Low–Moderate for *-ar* as a productive faction suffix.

Haranir cross-reference: The Hara'ni place name Ul'ar (*Ul'* + *-ar*) contains an identical *-ar* suffix. Wildcamp Ul'ar is near the City of Threads in Azj-Kahet, placing Haranir and nerubians in direct geographic proximity. If *-ar* were a nerubian loanword into Hara'ni, the semantic shift from "brood/collective" to "camp/settlement" would be natural. However, *-ar* is a common syllable across WoW languages, and no other Haranir–nerubian shared morphemes exist. Confidence: Very Low for *-ar* as a confirmed loanword. See haranir/vocabulary.html for full analysis.

The *En-* Prefix: *En'kilah*

The WotLK place name En'kilah (Temple City in Borean Tundra) contains the element *En-*, which would be a third nerubian toponymic prefix alongside *Azj-* and *Ahn-*. However, the building's original architects are not definitively identified as nerubian, and *-kilah* strongly resembles Arabic *qila* / *kil'a* ("fortress/citadel"), suggesting a possible Blizzard design borrowing with no in-universe morphological significance.

Summary of Key Findings

  1. Two distinct apostrophe patterns: Shath'Yar uses initial apostrophes (prefix boundaries: C'+root), while nerubian, qiraji, ethereal, and Hara'ni use medial apostrophes (compound boundaries: stem+'stem). This structural difference suggests independent development or a functional shift during the Old God → Aqir transmission.
  2. Aqir substrate morphemes emerging: At least two morphemes (*Ahn-* = "old/sacred" and *Azj-* = "deep/realm") appear to be shared Aqir heritage between nerubian and qiraji/naga. A third (*-naxx*/*-nax*) is possible but very low confidence.
  3. Potential first regular sound correspondence: If the geminate simplification hypothesis is correct (Aqir *-naxx* → nerubian *-nax*), this would be the first established sound correspondence between Aqir-descended languages.
  4. Ethereal is independent: Despite sharing the apostrophe convention, the ethereal language shows no confirmed morpheme sharing with the Aqir-descended languages. Its apostrophe usage and soft phonological profile suggest either independent development or Blizzard aesthetic choice.
  5. Hara'ni reclassified: Hara'ni is now confirmed as a deeply divergent branch of the elven/troll language family, not an independent isolate. It retains the medial apostrophe compounding pattern shared with nerubian, qiraji, and ethereal, but its genetic relationship to elven/troll languages makes it the only "alien" language with confirmed ties to another WoW language family. Three confirmed morphemes (*Har'* = village, *Shul'* = shadow, *-ka* = agentive) represent the first confirmed semantic glosses for any of the five languages.
  6. Blizzard design aesthetic is a major factor: The apostrophe convention is a standard fantasy trope that Blizzard applied consistently to "alien" species. Any in-universe explanation must account for the fact that the convention extends to the ethereal language, which has no Azeroth origin, and the Hara'ni language, which is elven/troll-related yet still uses the convention.
