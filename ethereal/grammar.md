# Ethereal Grammar

Grammar rules below are **inferred from attested examples only**. The Ethereal language has no attested sentences — the entire corpus consists of proper names, place names, and faction designations spanning *The Burning Crusade* (2007), *Shadowlands* (2020), and *The War Within* (2024–2026). Patterns are noted where multiple examples support a rule. Speculative extensions are marked [speculative].

## Phonology

### Sound Inventory (Observed from Attested Names)

**Consonants**: b, d, f, h, j, k, l, m, n, r, s, sh (/ʃ/), t, v, z, zh (/ʒ/)

**Vowels**: a, e, i, o, u

### Phonological Patterns

1. **Apostrophe as morpheme boundary**: The names *K'aresh* and *Ve'nari* use apostrophes to mark morpheme boundaries, consistent with nerubian, qiraji, and Shath'Yar conventions. However, not all ethereal names use apostrophes (*Salhadaar*, *Shaffar*, *Haramad*, *Azir*, *Ameer*), suggesting the convention is less consistent than in nerubian.

2. **Sibilant preference**: High frequency of /ʃ/ (sh) and /s/ in ethereal names: *K'aresh*, *Tazavesh*, *Shaffar*. This may reflect:
   - The ethereals' "whispery" nature as energy beings
   - A Blizzard aesthetic choice to make ethereal names sound "shimmering" or "insubstantial"
   - A genuine phonological tendency of the language

3. **Syllable simplicity**: Ethereal names tend toward simple CV or CVC syllables, without the complex consonant clusters found in nerubian (*Krix'vizk*, *Vrrix*) or Vrykul. This is a notable phonological difference from other "alien" WoW languages.

4. **Long vowels**: *Salhadaar* contains the sequence *-aa-* (/ɑː/), which may represent a long vowel or geminate vowel. If this is a phonologically significant feature, it could mark stress, morpheme boundaries, or semantic distinctions.

5. **Geminate consonants**: *Shaffar* contains *-ff-*, which may represent a geminate /f/. If the ethereal language has geminate consonants (like qiraji *-naxx*), this would be a feature shared with qiraji but not nerubian.

6. **Vowel distribution**: Names favor /a/ and /e/ heavily, with /i/ and /u/ less common. /o/ is rare. This pattern partially overlaps with nerubian and Nazja vowel distributions.

## Morphology

### Compounding with Apostrophe Boundary

The primary morphological pattern observable in ethereal names is the apostrophe-marked compound:

| Pattern | Examples |
|---------|----------|
| *K'* + suffix | *K'aresh* |
| *Ve'* + suffix | *Ve'nari* |

Unlike nerubian (which has dozens of apostrophe-marked compounds), ethereal has only two clear examples. This makes it difficult to determine whether apostrophe-marking is a productive morphological process or a feature of only certain name types.

[speculative]: If the compounding pattern is [modifier/prefix] + [head/suffix], then *K'aresh* = "[K']-realm" and *Ve'nari* = "[Ve']-[nari]" — a modifier-head order consistent with right-headed compounding in the titan-forged and Aqir-descended language families. However, with only two data points, this inference is extremely weak.

### The *-esh* / *-aresh* Suffix

The element *-esh* appears in two place names:

| Name | Decomposition | Proposed Gloss |
|------|---------------|----------------|
| *K'aresh* | *K'ar-* + *-esh* (or *K'* + *-aresh*) | "[K'ar]-realm" |
| *Tazavesh* | *Tazav-* + *-esh* | "[Tazav]-realm" |

If *-esh* = "world" / "realm" / "home," this would be one of the most securely attested ethereal morphemes, with two independent attestations and consistent semantics.

The artifact name *Reshii* may provide the full root form: *Resh-* = "realm/world," with *-esh* being a reduced form (loss of initial /r/ after consonant-final stems) and *-aresh* being an extended form with linking vowel. See vocabulary.md §14 for full analysis.

[speculative]: The *-aresh* form in *K'aresh* could be an extended variant: *-a-resh* with a linking vowel, or *-ar-esh* with an additional consonant. If the base suffix is *-esh*, then *-aresh* = *-a-* + *-esh*, with the linking vowel possibly marking a specific semantic distinction (e.g., "homeworld" vs. "city/realm").

**Confidence: Moderate** for *-esh* as a genuine locative suffix (upgraded from Low–Moderate based on *Reshii* analysis).

### The *-daar* Suffix

The element *-daar* appears in *Salhadaar*, the name of the Nexus-King (former ruler of K'aresh). If *-daar* = "king" or "ruler," this would parallel other WoW language systems where name suffixes encode social rank (cf. nerubian *-arak* "king/ruler").

However, with only one attestation, this remains highly speculative.

**Confidence: Very Low.**

## Relationship to Other WoW Languages

### No Confirmed Genetic Relationships

The ethereal language shows no confirmed genetic relationship to any other WoW language family:

- **Not titan-forged**: Ethereals are not titan-forged beings; they are native to K'aresh, a separate world
- **Not elven**: No shared morphemes with Darnassian/Thalassian/Nazja
- **Not Aqir-descended**: No confirmed shared morphemes with nerubian/qiraji (the *Az-* in *Azir* and *Azj-* is likely coincidental)
- **Not Shath'Yar**: Despite some ethereals having Void connections, the language is phonologically distinct from Shath'Yar

### Possible Areal Features

The shared apostrophe convention across ethereal, nerubian, qiraji, and Shath'Yar may reflect an areal feature of "alien" WoW naming conventions rather than genuine linguistic contact. However, the structural differences between Shath'Yar (initial apostrophe marking prefix boundaries) and the other three (medial apostrophe marking compound boundaries) suggest independent development or different transmission chains. See vocabulary.md §15 for full cross-reference analysis.

### The *Reshii* Connection

The artifact name *Reshii Ribbons* contains the element *Resh-*, which may be the full root form of the *-esh* locative suffix. If *resh* = "realm/world," then:
- *Reshii* = *Resh-* + *-ii* = "realms" or "of the realm" (full root form)
- *-esh* in *Tazavesh* = reduced form of *resh-* with /r/ loss after consonant cluster
- *-aresh* in *K'aresh* = *-a-* + *resh* = "realm" with linking vowel

This analysis strengthens the *-esh* suffix hypothesis by providing the full root form. See vocabulary.md §14 for full analysis.

**Confidence: Low–Moderate** for *Resh-* as the full root form of *-esh*.

### The Broker–Ethereal Dialect Hypothesis

Brokers and ethereals are closely related species from K'aresh. The shared *-esh* element in *K'aresh* and *Tazavesh* suggests they speak dialects of the same language. Key differences:

| Feature | Ethereal Dialect | Broker Dialect |
|---------|-----------------|----------------|
| Name length | Longer (*Salhadaar*, *Haramad*) | Shorter (*Ve'nari*) |
| Apostrophe usage | Less consistent | More consistent (*Ve'nari*) |
| Cultural focus | Political/military | Mercantile |

**Confidence: Low–Moderate** for a shared language with dialectal variation.

## Limitations

The ethereal corpus is extremely small:
- ~6 personal names
- ~4 place names with potential ethereal etymologies
- **0 attested phrases or sentences**

No verb forms, particles, or syntactic structures are known. The only confirmed morphological patterns are:
1. Apostrophe-marked compounding (very low confidence — only 2 examples)
2. *-esh* / *-aresh* suffix (moderate confidence — upgraded from Low–Moderate based on *Reshii* analysis; see §14 in vocabulary.md)
3. Nexus-* title hierarchy (Common-language, not ethereal)
