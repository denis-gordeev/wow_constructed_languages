# Nazja Grammar

Grammar rules below are **inferred from attested examples only**. Nazja is not a fully constructed language; Blizzard provided limited phrases and names. Patterns are noted where multiple examples support a rule. Speculative extensions are marked [speculative]. RPG content is marked [RPG, non-canon].

## Morphology

### Compounding

Like Darnassian and Thalassian, Nazja makes heavy use of compounding. Morphemes are concatenated to form complex words, particularly in names and titles.

**Pattern: Tribe/Clan Names with Apostrophe Boundary**

- Sira + 'kess = Sira'kess
- Idra + 'kess = Idra'kess
- Naz + 'jar = Naz'jar
- Skar + 'this = Skar'this
- Sriss + 'tiz = Sriss'tiz
- Vashj + 'ir = Vashj'ir
- Zanj + 'ir = Zanj'ir

The apostrophe consistently marks morpheme boundaries, inherited from the Darnassian/Elven compounding tradition.

**Pattern: Place Names with Hyphen Boundary**

- Zin-Azshari = Zin ("glory") + Azshari ("of Azshara") — this is Darnassian, but the pattern may persist in Nazja

**Pattern: The -tar Suffix in Place/Domain Names**

- Nazjatar = Nazj- + -atar
- Zin'jatar = Zin'- + -atar
- Kaz'jatar = Kaz'- + -atar

The element *-tar* (or *-atar*) appears in three attested names. If *Zin-Azshari* means "Glory of Azshara" and *Zin'jatar* is a separate name containing *Zin*, then *-jatar* may be a distinct morpheme from *-Azshari*. The suffix *-tar* could mean "city," "domain," "throne," or "capital."

[speculative]: Nazjatar may decompose as *Nazj-* (root meaning "deep" or "naga") + *-atar* (suffix meaning "city/domain"), yielding "City of the Deep" or "Domain of the Naga." This would align with the speculation that "Nazjatar" derives from "Nazja" (the language name).

### The -kess Suffix

Three tribe names share the element *-kess*:

- Sira'kess
- Idra'kess  
- Shirakess (possibly Shira + kess with assimilation)

This could be a tribal/clan suffix, possibly indicating a female-led group (all three are associated with female leaders: Lady Sira'kess, Overseer Idra'kess). Alternatively, it may relate to the sea or tides.

### The -'ir / -'jir Suffix

Three tribe names share the element *-'ir*:

- Azsh'ir
- Vashj'ir
- Zanj'ir

This may mean "people of" or "tribe of" or "domain of." Compare with Darnassian *-dorei* ("children of"), which serves a similar ethnonym-forming function.

### Apostrophe Usage

Apostrophes mark morpheme boundaries within compound words, consistent with the elven language family:

- Vashj'ir = Vashj + ir
- Naz'jar = Naz + jar
- Sira'kess = Sira + kess
- Zanj'ir = Zanj + ir

Unlike Darnassian, where some compounds lack apostrophes (*Kaldorei*, *Teldrassil*), nearly all multi-morpheme Naga names use apostrophes. This may reflect:
- A more consistent orthographic convention in Nazja
- The greater phonological complexity of Nazja morpheme boundaries
- A deliberate visual distinction from Darnassian naming conventions

## Syntax

### Imperative / Battle Cry Constructions

Several attested phrases appear to be imperative constructions:

- **Be'lemer an-delei!** — Lady Vashj's battle cry. Possibly: *Be'* (imperative marker?) + *lemer* + *an* (particle?) + *delei*.
- **Bal, lamer zhita!** — High Warlord Naj'entus. Possibly: *Bal* (command?) + *lamer* + *zhita*.
- **Fara dena'lay!** — Zhahara Darksquall. Possibly: *Fara* (command/verb?) + *dena'lay* (target/effect).

[speculative]: The pattern of short initial element + longer complement resembles the Darnassian imperative pattern (*Andu-falah-dor*, *Tor ilisar'thera'nal*), suggesting a shared syntactic structure for jussive/imperative constructions.

### Vocative / Invocation Pattern

- **Zin-Azshari adore.** — Illidari naga NPCs. This appears to invoke *Zin-Azshari* (the city/queen's glory) + *adore* ("be with you" / "bless"), structurally parallel to Darnassian *Elune-adore*.
- **Therazjasha!** — Sea Witch cry. Possibly an invocation of Azshara: *Thera* + *Azshara* → *Therazjasha* (compressed invocation).
- **For Nazjatar!** — Simple dedicatory battle cry (likely a Common-language exclamation, not Nazja).

### Phrase-Internal Boundaries

- **Ash-thero-sauguine!** — Hyphens separate three elements: *Ash* + *thero* + *sauguine*. This parallels the Darnassian multi-morpheme phrase pattern (*Tor ilisar'thera'nal*).
- **Osh'bala-tharei** — Apostrophe separates *Osh* from *bala*, hyphen separates *bala* from *tharei*.

The use of both hyphens and apostrophes as boundary markers mirrors Darnassian convention:
- **Apostrophe** = word-internal morpheme boundary
- **Hyphen** = phrase-level syntactic boundary

### Word Order

The extremely limited sample prevents firm conclusions. Observed patterns:

- **Initial imperative particle + complement**: Be'lemer an-delei!, Fara dena'lay!
- **Initial vocative + blessing**: Zin-Azshari adore
- **Modifier + Head in compounds**: Nazj-atar (root + suffix)

## Phonology

### Sound Inventory (Observed)

**Consonants**: b, d, f, h, j (/dʒ/), k, l, m, n, p, r, s, t, th (/θ/), v, z, sh (/ʃ/), zh (/ʒ/)

**Vowels**: a, e, i, o, u

### Phonological Patterns

1. **Sibilant density**: /s/, /z/, /ʃ/, /ʒ/ are the most distinctive feature. Names and phrases average 2-3 sibilants per word, far exceeding Darnassian or Thalassian.

2. **/ʒ/ (zh) as marker of Nazja identity**: This sound, rare in other elven languages, is extremely common in Nazja. It may have developed from:
   - A Darnassian /j/ that shifted under water-distorted articulation
   - A Darnassian /ʃ/ that lenited in the naga vocal tract
   - An entirely new phoneme enabled by the transformed vocal apparatus

3. **/dʒ/ (j) frequency**: The affricate /dʒ/ is uncommon in Darnassian but very frequent in Nazja names. It appears in medial and final positions: *Naz'jar*, *Zin'jatar*, *Vashj*, *Sashj'tar*.

4. **Consonant cluster tolerance**: Nazja tolerates clusters that would be unusual in Darnassian:
   - Word-initial: /sr/ (*Sriss'tiz*), /sk/ (*Skar'this*)
   - Word-medial: /ssz/ (*Sassz'ine*), /lθ/ (*Kalithresh*)
   - Word-final: /ʃ/ (*Vashj*, *Nazesh*)

5. **Vowel reduction**: Compared to Darnassian's balanced vowel distribution, Nazja strongly favors /a/ and /e/ in stressed syllables, with /i/ common in unstressed positions. /o/ and /u/ are rare.

6. **Syllable structure**: Tends toward (C)V(C) but allows more complex onsets and codas than Darnassian. Common patterns:
   - CV: *Na, ze, shi*
   - CVC: *Vashj, Nazj, kess*
   - CCV: *Sras, shj'tar* (in rapid speech)

### Phonological Evolution from Darnassian (Speculative)

The following shifts from Darnassian to Nazja are hypothesized based on the comparative evidence:

| Darnassian | Nazja | Example |
|-----------|-------|---------|
| /j/ | /dʒ/ or /ʒ/ | *Azshari* → *Azsh'ari*; possible /j/ → /dʒ/ shift in *Vashj*, *Naz'jar* |
| /ʃ/ | /ʒ/ | Possible lenition in underwater environment |
| /l/ + /r/ | Retained but less frequent | Reduced liquid frequency compared to Darnassian |
| Vowel /o/, /u/ | Shifted toward /a/, /e/ | Possible underwater acoustic adaptation |
| Simple onsets | Complex clusters | /sr/, /sk/ onset clusters may reflect naga vocal tract capabilities |

## Limitations

The corpus is extremely small (~15-20 attested phrases, ~50+ proper names). Many "words" are isolated from single contexts, making it impossible to determine:

- Whether word order is free or fixed
- How case, number, or tense are marked (if at all)
- The full range of pronominal forms
- Whether *Be'* and *Fara* are truly imperative markers or carry different nuances
- The relationship between *thero* (in *Ash-thero-sauguine*) and Darnassian *thera* (in *Tor ilisar'thera'nal*) — whether cognate or coincidental
- The meaning of most attested phrases beyond "battle cry" or "invocation"
- Whether the tribal suffixes *-kess*, *-'ir*, *-tar* are productive morphemes or fossilized name elements
