Earthen Vocabulary

All entries are attested in World of Warcraft canon (NPC dialogue, quest text, in-game text, official Blizzard sources) unless marked. Inferences are marked with [inferred].

The Earthen are the titan-forged stone progenitors of Dwarves, introduced as a major faction in The War Within (2024). Their naming patterns preserve older titan-forged conventions that Dwarves simplified after the Curse of Flesh.

6. Key Linguistic Observations

  1. Khaz Algar confirms *Khaz-* as a productive Titan-derived toponymic prefix. With *Khaz Modan* (Dwarven kingdom) and *Khaz Algar* (subterranean continent), the *Khaz-* prefix now appears in three distinct place names, strengthening the inference that *Khaz-* = "forge/craft/shape" in the Titan-forged language family.
  2. Earthen personal names differ from Dwarven personal names. Dwarven names (Magni, Muradin, Brann, Moira) follow a Celtic/Norse short-name pattern. Earthen names (Baelgrim, Merrix, Adelgonn, Brinthe, Eirich, Lufsela) show a different pattern with longer, more complex names. This may reflect:
    • Earthen retaining older titan-forged naming conventions while Dwarves developed simpler names after the Curse of Flesh
    • Regional variation: Khaz Algar Earthen were isolated for millennia from Eastern Kingdoms Dwarves
    • Blizzard design choice for TWW aesthetics
  3. The *Bael-* element appears in both Earthen and Dwarven naming. Earthen *Baelgrim* (Stormward) and Dwarven *Bael'dun* (dig site) share the *Bael-* root. If *Bael-* = "stone/earth," this demonstrates a shared morpheme between the two groups, consistent with their common Earthen ancestry.
  4. The *Eir-* element may connect Earthen and Vrykul. Earthen *Eirich* and Vrykul *Eyir* (Val'kyr goddess) both contain the *Eir-* element. In Norse mythology, *Eir* is a goddess of healing. If both languages preserve this as a Titan morpheme, it would demonstrate deep vocabulary retention across the titan-forged family. See Section 7 below.
  5. The *-dar* suffix in Hallowfall place names may reflect a Titan *-dor* variant. Both *Beledar* and *Mereldar* end in *-dar*, which phonologically parallels Titan *-dor* (as in *Kalimdor* = "Land of Eternal Starlight"). However, these are Arathi settlements, not Earthen ones — the *-dar* suffix may have been borrowed from Earthen into Arathi/Common, or it may be an independent Arathi development.
  6. Skardyn as a void-corrupted Earthen term. The *Skar-* element in *Skardyn* parallels Vrykul *Skar-* in *Skarvald* and *Voldskar*. If *Skar-* = "dark/corrupt" or "shadow" in the titan-forged family, this would be a meaningful connection, though the sample size is too small to confirm.

7. Investigation: The *Eir-* Connection Between Earthen and Vrykul

The Earthen name *Eirich* and the Vrykul Val'kyr goddess *Eyir* share the element *Eir-* /ɛɪr/. This section evaluates whether this shared element reflects a common Titan morpheme or is coincidental.

Arguments for a connection

  1. Phonological identity: Both *Eirich* and *Eyir* begin with the sequence /ɛɪr/. The phonological match is exact for the first syllable.
  2. Semantic plausibility: In Norse mythology, *Eir* is a goddess associated with healing, medicine, and medical skill. If the Warcraft *Eir-* morpheme has a similar meaning ("healer," "guardian," "one who tends"), then:
    • *Eyir* as a Val'kyr goddess could be "the Healer" or "the Guardian" — fitting for a goddess who chooses and tends to the slain
    • *Eirich* as a Machine Speaker leader could be "Healer-[suffix]" or "Guardian of [uncertain]" — fitting for one who maintains (heals) ancient machinery
  3. Titan morpheme distribution: If *Eir-* is a Titan morpheme, it would be expected to appear in multiple titan-forged languages. The appearance in both Earthen (via Khaz'goroth's creations) and Vrykul (via Odyn's Val'kyr) is consistent with a shared Titan source that was preserved in different cultural contexts.
  4. Parallel to *Val-* distribution: The *Val-* morpheme appears in both Titan (*Val'anyr*) and Vrykul (*Valhalas*, *Val'kyr*), demonstrating that Titan morphemes can survive in multiple descendant languages. *Eir-* could follow the same pattern.

Arguments against a connection

  1. Blizzard design convergence: Both *Eirich* and *Eyir* are Norse-inspired names. Blizzard routinely draws on Norse mythology for titan-forged naming. The shared *Eir-* could simply reflect independent borrowing from the same real-world source (Old Norse *Eir*), not an in-universe linguistic connection.
  2. Semantic mismatch: *Eyir* is a Val'kyr — a being who chooses the slain and carries them to the Halls of Valor. *Eirich* is a Machine Speaker — a being who maintains ancient Titan machinery. The semantic domains (death/valor vs. machinery/craft) are very different, making a shared meaning for *Eir-* less likely.
  3. Phonological variation: *Eirich* /ˈaɪrɪk/ and *Eyir* /ˈɛɪjɪɾ/ have different vowel qualities in the first syllable (/aɪ/ vs. /ɛɪ/) and different second elements (/ɪk/ vs. /ɪɾ/). The match is not as close as it initially appears.
  4. No other *Eir-* names exist: If *Eir-* were a productive Titan morpheme, one might expect it in other titan-forged languages (Dwarven, Mogu, Tol'vir, Gnomish). It does not appear in any of these.

8. The *-dar* Suffix and the Arathi Connection

The *-dar* suffix appears in two Hallowfall place names (*Beledar*, *Mereldar*) and may reflect a variant of Titan *-dor* (land/realm). However, Hallowfall is an Arathi settlement, not an Earthen one. This section evaluates whether *-dar* is Earthen, Arathi, or shared.

Analysis: Earthen *-dar* vs. Arathi *-dar*

There are three possible explanations for the *-dar* suffix in Hallowfall place names:

1. Earthen substrate: The Hallowfall area was originally Earthen territory (part of Khaz Algar). The Arathi arrived later (expedition from the Arathi Empire 15 years prior). If the Arathi adopted existing Earthen place names, then *Beledar* and *Mereldar* would be Earthen toponyms borrowed into Arathi/Common. This would make *-dar* = *-dor* (Titan "land/realm"), with regular vowel raising /ɔ/ → /ɑ/.

2. Arathi independent innovation: The Arathi, as descendants of humans (who descend from Vrykul, who descend from Titan), may have preserved the *-dor* morpheme through their Vrykul ancestry, developing it independently into *-dar*. This would represent a parallel sound change in two branches of the titan-forged family: Earthen *-dor* → *-dar* and Common/Arathi *-dor* → *-dar*.

3. Contact borrowing: The Arathi, living in close proximity to Earthen in Khaz Algar, borrowed the *-dar* toponymic suffix from Earthen for their own place names. This would be consistent with the known contact between the two groups.

10. Kobyss and the Murloc/Jinyu/Ankoan Language Family

The kobyss are vicious amphibious creatures native to the Undersea of Khaz Algar, resembling large humanoid anglerfish. They attack the Arathi settlements whenever Beledar shifts into its Void phase, suggesting a connection to Void energy. The full murloc → jinyu → ankoan → kobyss evolutionary chain and all associated linguistic data have been moved to a dedicated directory: nerglish/.

This language family is entirely separate from both the titan-forged family and the Aqir-descended family. Key linguistic findings:

  • The family shows a rise and fall of linguistic complexity: murloc → jinyu (expansion), jinyu → ankoan (simplification), ankoan → kobyss (near-total language loss)
  • The kobyss represent the only known case in WoW of linguistic regression in a descended race
  • The Gutter kobyss tribe has documented contact with nerubians (the alchemist Al'kubian studied them)
