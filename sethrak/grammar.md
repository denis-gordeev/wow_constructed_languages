# Sethrak Grammar

This document presents grammatical inferences drawn from attested Sethrak proper names in World of Warcraft. Sethrak is an extremely poorly attested language; all rules below are highly speculative and based on a handful of proper names. No native grammar has been published by Blizzard.

## Morphology

### Compounding

Sethrak appears to form words through compounding, similar to other WoW constructed languages (Draenei, Zandali):

- *Seth-rak*: *seth* ("serpent"? (inferred)) + *rak* ("people"? (inferred)) = "Serpent People"
- *Seth-raliss*: *seth* ("serpent"? (inferred)) + *raliss* ("lightning"? / "divine"? (inferred)) = "Serpent Lightning" or "Divine Serpent"
- *Jakr'a-zet*: *jakra* ("faithless"? (inferred)) + *zet* ("ruler"? (inferred)) = "Faithless Ruler"

Compounding may be right-headed: the final element carries the core semantic category (*rak* → people, *zet* → title). This is uncertain given the extremely limited data.

### Apostrophe Usage

The apostrophe in *Jakra'zet* appears to mark a morpheme boundary, consistent with the Zandali orthographic convention (cf. Zandali *Zul'jin*, *Tal'farrak*). This may indicate:

1. A morpheme boundary between two compounding roots (*jakra* + *zet*).
2. Zandali orthographic influence on Sethrak writing, since Sethrak and Zandalari trolls coexist on Zandalar.
3. A phonological feature such as a glottal stop /ʔ/ or vowel elision at the morpheme juncture.

The single attested apostrophe makes generalization impossible.

### Suffixation

**-rak** ("people" / "ones" (inferred)): A collective or nominalizing suffix indicating a group of people.

- *Sethrak* = "Serpent People" (*seth* + *rak*) (inferred)

This parallels similar suffixes in other WoW languages:
- Draenei *-ei* ("ones/people")
- Vulpera *-era* ("people/folk") (inferred)

All three are speculative; the similarity may be coincidental or may reflect a shared WoW naming convention rather than a linguistic relationship.

### Potential Name Structure

Sethrak proper names observed fall into patterns:

- **CVC-CVC**: *Korthek*, *Korgok* — possible compound names with two roots.
- **CVCV**: *Aspix* — simplex or unanalyzable.
- **CVCV-CV**: *Jakra'zet* — compound with apostrophe boundary.
- **CV-CVC-CV**: *Merektha* — possibly three morphemes; possibly simplex.

The small sample size (n ≈ 8) makes any structural claims very uncertain.

## Phonology

### Consonant Inventory

Based on attested proper names, Sethrak consonants likely include:

- **Stops**: /k/, /g/, /t/, /p/, /b/ (the latter from *Aspix* /b/ uncertain)
- **Fricatives**: /s/, /θ/ (th in *Sethrak*, *Korthek*, *Merektha*), /v/, /z/
- **Nasals**: /m/, /n/
- **Liquids**: /r/, /l/
- **Affricates**: /dʒ/ (in *Jakra'zet*)

The fricative /θ/ is notably frequent, appearing in three of eight personal names (*Sethrak*, *Korthek*, *Merektha*) and in the ethnonym and loa name (*Sethraliss*). This may reflect a phonological preference or a semantic association with serpent-related vocabulary.

### Consonant Clusters

Sethrak permits some consonant clusters:

- *Korthek*: /rk.θ/ across syllable boundary
- *Merektha*: /k.θ/ across syllable boundary
- *Teskris*: /skr/ onset cluster

Clusters are less complex than in Draenei. The most common cluster involves /θ/ following a velar stop (/kθ/, /gθ/), which may be a characteristic phonotactic pattern.

### Vowel Inventory

Observed vowels include /a/, /e/, /i/, /o/. The vowel /u/ is absent from attested forms but may exist in unattested vocabulary. Vowel quality in stressed vs. unstressed positions is unknown.

### Stress

No data available. Names with heavy penultimate syllables (*Merektha*, *Sethraliss*) may carry penultimate stress, but this is pure speculation.

### Phonotactic Notes

- The sequence /sɛθ/ ("seth") appears in the two most important cultural terms (*Sethrak*, *Sethraliss*), suggesting it is a phonologically stable and semantically central root.
- Rhotic /r/ is frequent, appearing in nearly every name (Sethrak, Sethraliss, Vorrik, Merektha, Korthek, Korgok).
- Names tend to be 2–3 syllables, with CVC roots appearing common.
