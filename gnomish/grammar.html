Gnomish Grammar

This document presents grammatical inferences drawn from attested Gnomish words and names in World of Warcraft. Gnomish is an extremely poorly attested language; all rules below are highly speculative. No native grammar has been published by Blizzard. Most gnomish NPCs speak Common in-game, and the Gnomish language is referenced but rarely demonstrated.

Notable Phonological Features

  • Initial clusters: Gnomish names show a preference for initial clusters: Mekk-, Gnom-, Spr- (Sprysprocket), Br- (Brassbolts). However, many of these are Common-language surnames, not Gnomish-language words.
  • Preference for /k/ and /g/: The sounds /k/ and /g/ are overrepresented in gnomish names, consistent with the cultural association of gnomes with mechanical devices (clicking, clanking, gear-work).
  • Reduplication: The name Mimiron (Titan Keeper who created mechagnomes) shows a reduplicative pattern Mi-mi-ron. Whether this reflects a Titan morphological process or is simply an aesthetic name choice is unknown.

Morphology

Compounding

Gnomish appears to form words through compounding, consistent with other Titan-forged languages:

  • Gnomeregan = Gnom- + -eregan (?) — "Gnome-[home/city]" (speculative)
  • Mechagnome = Mecha- + gnome — "Machine-gnome" (transparent in Common)
  • Mechagon = Mech- + -agon (?) — "Machine-[place]" (speculative)

The compounding direction is likely right-headed (inherited from Titan), though the corpus is too small to confirm.

The *Mech-* / *Mekka-* Root

The root Mech- / Mekka- appears in multiple terms associated with gnomish and mechagnome culture:

  • Mekkatorque — surname of the High Tinker
  • Mechagnome — the titan-forged progenitors
  • Mechagon — the mechagnome kingdom/island
  • Mimiron — Titan Keeper who created the mechagnomes (shares initial Mi-)

The consistent appearance of Mech- / Mekka- in terms related to mechanical creation suggests this may be a genuine Gnomish (or Titan-derived) morpheme meaning "machine," "mechanism," or "crafted thing." The phonological similarity to Mimiron raises the possibility that Mim- and Mech- are phonologically related Titan roots for "creator" and "creation" respectively, but this is highly speculative.

The *Gnom-* Root

The root Gnom- appears in:

  • Gnome — the race name (self-designation)
  • Gnomeregan — the capital city
  • Gnomercy — a pun name (not linguistic evidence)
  • Mechagnome — the titan-forged progenitors

The etymology of Gnom- is uncertain. In real-world linguistics, "gnome" derives from Latin gnoma / Greek gnōmē ("thought/intelligence") via Paracelsus's 16th-century coinage. Whether Blizzard's "gnome" has an in-universe etymology is unknown. The presence of Gnom- in Gnomeregan suggests it functions as a morpheme in the Gnomish language, but no translation is confirmed.

Potential Suffix *-regan* / *-agon*

If Gnomeregan = Gnom- + -eregan and Mechagon = Mech- + -agon, the suffixal elements might be related:

  • -eregan and -agon share a velar consonant /ɡ/ followed by a vowel and nasal (/æn/ and /ɒn/ respectively)
  • Both names designate places/cities
  • The phonological difference (-eregan vs. -agon) could reflect: (a) different suffixes, (b) the same suffix with phonological variation, or (c) coincidental similarity
This remains highly speculative. Only two data points exist.

Titan-Forged Structural Inheritance

As a Titan-forged descendant language, Gnomish likely inherited:

  1. Right-headed compounding: modifier + head noun (consistent with Titan, Vrykul, Dwarven)
  2. Apostrophe morpheme boundaries: not clearly attested in Gnomish, but the small corpus may simply not include compound names with boundaries
  3. Place-naming conventions: if Gnomeregan and Mechagon are Gnomish-language place names, they may use a suffix pattern (root + place suffix) rather than the Titan Uld- prefix pattern

Writing System

Gnomes use a runic/technological writing system. In-game, gnomish writing appears on signage in Gnomeregan and Mechagon, typically rendered as stylized runic characters combined with mechanical diagrams. No complete transcription or decipherment of the Gnomish script has been published.

The mechagnomes, as titan-forged beings, likely originally used Titan script. Over time, the gnomes may have developed their own orthographic system, though this is not confirmed.

Relationship to Titan Language Family

Gnomish is a daughter language of Titan, via the Mechagnome lineage:

Titan
└── Mechagnome (titan-forged by Mimiron)
    └── Gnomish (after Curse of Flesh)

Key structural features shared with other Titan-forged descendants:

  • Right-headed compounding
  • Initial stress (probable)
  • Preference for complex onsets in names
  • Potential shared morphemes (-ir animate suffix is not clearly attested in Gnomish)

Key divergences from other Titan-forged languages:

  • Gnomish names show less compound complexity than Vrykul or Titan
  • The Mech- root is unique to Gnomish/Mechagnome vocabulary
  • Gnomish has been heavily influenced by Common through centuries of bilingualism

Limitations

  • No Gnomish-language sentences are attested. All in-game gnomish dialogue is in Common.
  • The Gnomish language is mentioned in lore (e.g., the language filter for gnomish players) but no examples of the language are provided.
  • Gnomish proper names may preserve Gnomish-language morphemes, but the vast majority of gnomish NPC names are Common-language compounds or puns.
  • The Mechagnome connection is the strongest link to the Titan language family, but mechagnome names in-game are also mostly Common-language.
  • Morpheme decompositions are entirely speculative. The Gnom- and Mech- roots are inferred from patterns, not confirmed by any canon source.
  • The relationship between Gnomish and other Titan-forged languages is based on the canon statement that gnomes descend from mechagnomes (titan-forged), but no direct linguistic comparison (e.g., shared vocabulary with translations) has been provided by Blizzard.
