# Mogu Grammar

This document presents grammatical inferences drawn from attested Mogu words and names in World of Warcraft. Mogu is a poorly attested language; all rules below are speculative and based on limited data. No native grammar has been published by Blizzard.

## Morphology

### Compounding

Mogu appears to form words through compounding, similar to other Titan-influenced languages in Warcraft. Place names and titles are transparently compound:

- *Mogu-shan*: *Mogu* ("the people/stone") + *shan* ("palace/mountain"?) = "Mogu Palace" or "Palace of the Mogu" (inferred)
- *Lei Shen*: *lei* ("thunder"?) + *shen* ("king"?) = "Thunder King" (inferred)

The apostrophe in *Mogu'shan* marks a morpheme boundary between the two compounding roots, similar to the Draenei apostrophe convention (*Sha'tar*, *Man'ari*).

### Potential Suffixation

**-shen** (inferred: "king" / "ruler"): Possibly a title suffix indicating rulership or authority.

- *Lei Shen* = "Thunder King" (*lei* + *shen*)

If *shen* is a productive suffix, it could theoretically combine with other roots to form titles. Only one attestation exists, so this remains highly speculative.

**-shan** (inferred: "palace" / "mountain"): A toponymic element appearing in place names.

- *Mogu'shan Palace* = "Mogu Palace" (*Mogu* + *shan*)
- *Mogu'shan Vaults* = same *shan* element

Whether *-shan* is a suffix or an independent noun is unclear. Its appearance in both compounds (and its potential overlap with Pandaren vocabulary) complicates analysis.

### Name-Epithet Pattern

Several Mogu names follow a pattern of a short Mogu word followed by an English epithet:

- *Qiang* the Merciless
- *Zian* of the Endless Shadow
- *Meng* the Demented
- *Xin* the Weaponmaster

The Mogu element in each case may be a given name that semantically echoes the English epithet. If so, this would suggest that Mogu personal names carry lexical meaning (a common cross-linguistic pattern), and that the English epithets are translations or glosses of the Mogu names:

- *Qiang* ≈ "strong/ruthless" → "the Merciless"
- *Zian* ≈ "shadow/dark" → "of the Endless Shadow"
- *Meng* ≈ "mad/fierce" → "the Demented"
- *Xin* ≈ "weapon/blade" → "the Weaponmaster"

This pattern is (inferred) and not confirmed by Blizzard.

### Apostrophe Usage

The apostrophe in Mogu orthography appears to mark a morpheme boundary:

- *Mogu'shan*: *Mogu* + *shan* — the apostrophe separates two compounding roots.

Only one attestation of the apostrophe exists in Mogu. Whether it also indicates a phonological feature (e.g., glottal stop) is unknown.

## Phonology

### Consonant Inventory

Based on attested forms, Mogu consonants include:

- **Stops**: /m/, /k/, /g/, /t/, /b/, /d/
- **Affricates**: /tʃ/ (Qiang), /dʒ/ (possible in some names)
- **Fricatives**: /ʃ/ (Shen, Shan), /z/ (Zian), /θ/ (Molthor)
- **Nasals**: /m/, /n/
- **Liquids**: /r/, /l/
- **Glottal**: /ʔ/ (possible, suggested by apostrophe in Mogu'shan)

### Consonant Clusters

Mogu permits some consonant clusters, though they are less complex than those in Draenei:

- *Molthor*: /lθ/
- *Ogudei*: no complex clusters

Observed syllable structures tend toward (C)V(C) — simple open or lightly closed syllables.

### Vowel Inventory

Observed vowels include /a/, /e/, /i/, /o/, /u/. Possible diphthongs:

- *Lei*: /eɪ/
- *Subetai*: /aɪ/

### Stress

No data available. Based on the romanization, stress may fall on the penultimate syllable in multisyllabic words (e.g., *Mo-gu*, *Ge-kkan*), but this is pure speculation.

### Phonotactic Notes

- The language has a perceived harsh, commanding quality, consistent with Mogu martial culture. Guttural and back consonants (/g/, /k/) are prominent.
- The sound /ʃ/ (written *sh*) appears in multiple important words (*Shen*, *Shan*), suggesting it is a phonologically stable and semantically significant consonant.
- Monosyllabic and disyllabic words predominate in the attested corpus, consistent with an isolating or lightly affixing language typology (inferred).
- The phonological profile is consistent with a Titan-created language, sharing some features with other Titan-influenced languages (e.g., Draenei's use of apostrophe for morpheme boundaries).
