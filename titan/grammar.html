Titan Grammar

This document presents grammatical inferences drawn from attested Titan words and names in World of Warcraft. Titan is a poorly attested language; all rules below are speculative and based on limited data. No native grammar has been published by Blizzard.

Morphology

Compounding

Titan extensively forms words through compounding. Proper names, weapon names, and facility names are transparently compound:

  • Aesir = Aes ("storm") + -ir ("giant") = "Storm Giants"
  • Vanir = Van ("earth") + -ir ("giant") = "Earth Giants"
  • Taeshalach = Tael/Taes ("flame") + shalach ("rend") = "Flame Rend" (RPG)
  • Gorshalach = Gor ("dark") + shalach ("render") = "Dark Render" (RPG)
  • Gorribal = Gor ("dark") + ribal ("seether") = "Dark Seether" (RPG)
  • Kalimdor = Kalim ("starlight"?) + -dor ("land"?) = "Land of Eternal Starlight"

The compound structure is right-headed: the final element appears to carry the core semantic category, while the initial element modifies it. This pattern is clearest in:

  • Aesir: "storm-GIANT" (not "giant-STORM")
  • Gorshalach: "dark-RENDER" (not "render-DARK")

The Suffix -ir

The suffix -ir appears on multiple words designating types of animate beings:

  • Aesir = "Storm Giants"
  • Vanir = "Earth Giants"
  • Tol'vir = the stone cat-people (titan-forged)

The consistent appearance of -ir on names for types of animate titan-forged beings suggests it is a productive suffix meaning "giant," "being," or "one of [type]." If this analysis is correct, Tol'vir decomposes as Tol + -vir (a phonologically conditioned variant of -ir), though the meaning of Tol is unknown.

The variant -nir appears in Midnir = "Vessel." Whether -nir is related to -ir or a separate morpheme is unclear. If Midnir = Mid + -nir, -nir could be an agentive or instrumental suffix meaning "that which [functions as]." Alternatively, Midnir may be monomorphemic.

The Prefix Uld-

The prefix Uld- appears in every known titan facility name:

  • Ulduar — largest titan complex (fortress/prison)
  • Uldum — superweapon zone
  • Uldaman — vault for Earthen and Discs of Norgannon
  • Uldir — research/quarantine facility
  • Uldis — facility beneath the Temple of Storms
  • Uldorus — facility (Tyrhold) in Thaldraszus
  • Uldaz — location mentioned by the Maiden of Vitality
  • Ulderoth — alternate timeline version of Azeroth

Brann Bronzebeard proposed Uld = "city" (Lands of Mystery, RPG). The sheer number of Uld- facilities supports the inference that this is a productive morpheme meaning "city," "facility," "construction," or "place of titan work."

The suffixal elements following Uld- may encode the facility's function:

  • -uar in Ulduar (fortress; the largest complex)
  • -um in Uldum (superweapon; origination device)
  • -aman in Uldaman (vault; storage)
  • -ir in Uldir (research facility)
  • -is in Uldis (unknown function)
  • -orus in Uldorus (Tyrhold; keeper facility)
  • -az in Uldaz (unknown function)

Whether these suffixes are meaningful morphemes or simply phonologically distinct endings is unknown.

The Root Khaz-

The root Khaz- appears in multiple contexts related to shaping and forging:

  • Khaz'goroth — the titan "Shaper and Forger of Worlds"
  • Khaz'mul — a chamber in Uldaman containing its secrets
  • Khaz Algar — a region of Earthen on the Dragon Isles

This distribution suggests Khaz- may mean "forge," "craft," "shape," or "creation." The apostrophe in Khaz'goroth and Khaz'mul marks a morpheme boundary between Khaz and the following element.

The Root Val-

The root Val- appears in several terms associated with Odyn and the Halls of Valor:

  • Val'anyr — "Hammer of Ancient Kings"
  • Val'kyr — "choosers of the slain" (shieldmaidens of Odyn)
  • Valarjar — Odyn's elite army of stormforged vrykul warriors

The semantic thread connecting these terms is "chosen," "valor," or "elevated/exalted." Val'kyr is a clear Norse calque on Valkyrie ("chooser of the slain"), and Valarjar echoes Norse Einherjar (the chosen slain of Valhalla). Whether Val- is a genuine Titan morpheme or a Common-language rendering of an Odyn-specific tradition is unclear.

The Root Gor- "Dark"

The root Gor- appears consistently in weapon names associated with Sargeras and destruction:

  • Gorshalach = "Dark Render" (RPG)
  • Gorribal = "Dark Seether" (RPG)
  • Gorridar = "Darkblade of the Sunderer"

The consistent glossing of Gor- as "dark" across three independent weapon names (all from the RPG, but internally consistent) supports this analysis at medium confidence.

The Roots Tael/Taes- "Flame" and -shalach "Rend/Render"

  • Taeshalach = "Flame Rend" (RPG) — Aggramar's sword
  • Gorshalach = "Dark Render" (RPG) — Sargeras's original sword

The shared element shalach means "rend" or "render." The initial elements Tael/Taes ("flame") and Gor ("dark") distinguish the two fragments of the original sword Gorshalach. The alternation between Tael and Taes may reflect:

  • Phonetic variation (assimilation to following sh)
  • Morphological conditioning (different allomorphs in different compound contexts)
  • Simple orthographic variation in the source

Reduplication or Echo Words

The name Echomok (a cavern in Uldaman) may contain a reduplicative element echo-, though this is highly speculative. No other examples of reduplication are attested.

The Particle Kol- in Kol-tharish

The exclamation Kol-tharish, yelled by Kologarn when killing a player, may decompose as Kol + tharish, but no glosses are available for either element. It may be a battle cry, a curse, or a ritual phrase. The hyphen suggests a compound.

Syntax

Word Order

Insufficient data. Only one multi-word phrase is attested:

  • Revola ruk maez amir — spoken by Magni Bronzebeard to open the Primordial Observatory door. Meaning unknown.

Without a translation, no syntactic analysis is possible. If we hypothesize that the phrase is a command or formulaic utterance (a "password" to open a door), possible structures include:

  • VSO: Revola (verb) + ruk (subject) + maez amir (object) — "Open the door of the observatory"
  • SOV: Revola (noun) + ruk (modifier) + maez (verb) + amir (object) — "The vessel opens the way"
  • Formulaic: The phrase may be a fixed ritual formula that does not reflect productive syntax.
This is pure speculation. No conclusions about Titan word order are justified.

Definiteness, Case, and Agreement

No data available.

The Conflation of "Created" and "Ordered"

The Grimoire of the Shadowlands and Beyond (pg. 154) confirms that Titan uses the same word for "created" as it does for "Ordered." This is a grammatical/lexical fact about the language, not a syntactic rule, but it has implications:

  • The language does not lexically distinguish between the act of bringing something into existence and the act of imposing Order upon it. This suggests that in the Titan worldview (and in the grammar encoding it), creation is ordering — there is no concept of "creation" that is not also "ordering."
  • This conflation may extend to related concepts: "build," "forge," "shape," and "organize" could all fall under the same lexical umbrella.

Phonology

Consonant Inventory

Based on attested forms, Titan consonants include:

  • Stops: /p/, /b/, /t/, /d/, /k/, /g/
  • Fricatives: /f/, /v/, /θ/, /ð/, /s/, /z/, /ʃ/, /h/
  • Affricates: /tʃ/ (possible in some names)
  • Nasals: /m/, /n/
  • Liquids: /r/, /l/
  • Glottal: /ʔ/ (possible, suggested by apostrophe in many names)

Consonant Clusters

Titan permits complex consonant clusters, more so than most mortal languages on Azeroth:

  • Khaz'goroth: /zɡ/ cluster across morpheme boundary
  • Golganneth: /lɡ/ cluster
  • Taeshalach: /ʃ/ cluster boundary
  • M'thorus: syllabic consonant or initial cluster

The complexity of these clusters is consistent with a language designed for beings whose vocal apparatus is not humanoid — the titans are planet-sized entities, and the language may reflect articulatory possibilities that mortal speakers cannot fully replicate.

Vowel Inventory

Full cardinal vowel set is attested: /a/, /e/, /i/, /o/, /u/. Possible additional vowels:

  • /æ/ (in names like Aggramar, Kalimdor)
  • /ʌ/ (in Uldaman, Uldum)

Syllable Structure

Observed syllable structures range from simple to complex:

  • Simple: (C)V — Ra (in Ra-den)
  • Moderate: (C)V(C) — Khaz, Gor, Tsol
  • Complex: CCV(C) — M'thorus (if initial cluster)

The predominant pattern in attested names is disyllabic or trisyllabic compounds of (C)V(C) roots.

Stress

No data available. The apostrophe may mark a stress boundary in some names:

  • Aman'Thul: stress likely on Thul
  • Khaz'goroth: stress likely on goroth
  • Ra-den: stress likely on den

If the apostrophe marks morpheme boundaries, stress may fall on the head (final) element of compounds, consistent with right-headed compound structure.

Phonotactic Notes

  • The language has a monumental, resonant quality — long sonorant sequences (Golganneth, Norgannon), back vowels, and heavy clusters give Titan names their characteristic weight.
  • Norse-inspired names (Odyn, Thorim, Hodir, Freya, Loken) are likely Common-language approximations of Titan names. The original Titan forms may have been phonologically quite different. These Keeper names are drawn from Norse mythology and may not reflect Titan phonology at all.
  • Apostrophe usage is widespread and appears to consistently mark morpheme boundaries (Aman'Thul, Khaz'goroth, Ra-den, Val'anyr, Gni'kiv, M'thorus, Tol'vir, Val'kyr, Akma'hat). In some cases (M'thorus), the apostrophe may also indicate a syllable break or reduced vowel.
  • The distinction between -ir and -vir in Aesir/Vanir vs. Tol'vir suggests that vowel insertion before the suffix may occur under certain phonological conditions (e.g., after certain consonants or in certain stress patterns).
