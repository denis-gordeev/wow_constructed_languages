# WoW Constructed Languages

Grammatical descriptions and vocabulary compilations for the constructed languages of World of Warcraft. All entries are based on attested canon from in-game dialogue, NPC speech, quest text, and official Blizzard sources. No invented words are included. Grammar rules are inferred from observed examples, not assumed from real-world languages.

## Methodology

1. **Attestation first**: Only words and phrases that appear in WoW canon are included.
2. **Inference from examples**: Grammar rules are derived from observed patterns in the attested corpus. Where the corpus is too small to support a rule, this is noted.
3. **Uncertainty marked**: All inferences and uncertain glosses are clearly labeled.
4. **No invention**: No words, phrases, or grammatical features are invented to fill gaps.

## Relationships

Several languages are genetically related:

- **Darnassian** is ancestral to **Thalassian**, **Shalassian**, and **Nazja** (all share the `-dorei` ethnonym pattern and core vocabulary; see [elven-comparative.html](elven-comparative.html) for full comparative analysis)
- **Draenei** and **Eredun** are the same language with cultural divergence (see [eredun-draenei-comparative.html](eredun-draenei-comparative.html) for comparative analysis)
- **Zandali** dialects vary across troll tribes but share core grammar
- **Shath'Yar** has no known relationship to any mortal language; it may be the ultimate source of apostrophe-marked compounding in Aqir-descended languages (see [alien-comparative.html](alien-comparative.html))
- **Pandaren** and **Mogu** share contact vocabulary due to millennia of domination (see [pandaren/vocabulary.md](pandaren/vocabulary.md#cross-reference-with-mogu-language) for cross-reference)
- **Sethrak** names show Zandali orthographic influence (apostrophe usage)
- **Titan** is the ancestral language of **Vrykul**, **Dwarven** (via Earthen), **Gnomish** (via Mechagnomes), **Mogu**, **Tol'vir**, and **Common** (via Vrykul→Humans) — all titan-forged races descend from Titan-speaking creations
- **Vrykul** is a direct descendant of Titan; **Common** (Human language) descends from Vrykul
- **Gnomish** descends from Titan via Mechagnomes, clockwork beings created by Keeper Mimiron; the *Mech-* / *Mekka-* root may be Titan-derived
- **Drust** is a daughter language of Vrykul, spoken by vrykul who settled Kul Tiras after the Sundering; it uses a unique *Gol-* prefix for place names (vs. Vrykul suffixes like *-heim*, *-garde*) and may preserve archaic features through its druidic Thornspeaker tradition
- **Earthen** is the direct ancestor of Dwarven, spoken by the titan-forged stone beings created by Khaz'goroth; it preserves more Titan morphemes and phonological features than Dwarven (including the /θ/ phoneme and longer compound names)
- **Nerubian** is spoken by the spider-people of Azj-Kahet; it has no confirmed genetic relationship to any other WoW language family but may preserve Aqir substrate vocabulary (the *Azj-* prefix) shared with naga *Nazj-*, and the *Ahn-* prefix shared with qiraji *Ahn'qiraj* (see [alien-comparative.html](alien-comparative.html))
- **Qiraji** is spoken by the insectoid servants of C'Thun in Silithus; it descends from the Aqir alongside nerubian, sharing the *Ahn-* morpheme ("old/ancient/sacred") and possibly the *-naxx* suffix pattern; qiraji retains geminate consonants (*-naxx*) where nerubian simplifies (*-nax*) (see [alien-comparative.html](alien-comparative.html))
- **Nazja** (Naga) is derived from Darnassian but has diverged significantly due to 10,000 years of underwater isolation and physiological transformation
- **Tol'vir** is likely derived from Titan, sharing this ancestry with **Mogu** — both are titan-forged languages from stone construct races; Tol'vir shows strong Egyptian-inspired naming conventions
- **Ethereal / Broker** is spoken by ethereals and brokers from the shattered world of K'aresh; it has no confirmed genetic relationship to any other WoW language family. The potential *-esh* locative suffix appears in both *K'aresh* (homeworld) and *Tazavesh* (broker capital). Ethereals and brokers likely speak dialects of the same language, with broker names tending to be shorter. Despite sharing the apostrophe convention with Shath'Yar, nerubian, and qiraji, this is likely due to Blizzard design aesthetics rather than in-universe contact (see [alien-comparative.html](alien-comparative.html))
- **Murloc/Jinyu/Ankoan/Kobyss** form a language family entirely separate from both the titan-forged and Aqir-descended families. Murlocs speak **Nerglish** (gurgling phonology, syllabic consonants); **Jinyu** evolved from murlocs with dramatically increased phonological complexity (full vowel inventory, CV/CVC syllable structure); **Ankoan** descend from jinyu with moderate simplification; **Kobyss** are ankoan-descended but show near-total language loss, retaining only 3–5 Common words ("Animate bones", "Devour", "Violence") — the only known case of linguistic regression in WoW (see [nerglish/](nerglish/))
- **Hara'ni** is spoken by the Haranir, an ancient race now confirmed to descend from **dark trolls/night elves** (*The Art of Midnight*, pg. 125): "those who would eventually become the haranir split from them and journeyed deep below the ground." This origin is further confirmed **in-plot** during the "Curse of Ula'tek" campaign (patch 12.1.0), where Hagar reveals that "elves and trolls share common ancestry, having descended from the haranir that chose to remain on the surface." This makes Hara'ni a **deeply divergent branch of the proto-troll/elven language family**, separated by ~16,000 years of independent development — and potentially the **ancestral stock** from which both elven and troll languages diverged. No cognates with attested elven or troll languages are confirmed due to extreme time depth. The Haranir use the **same Xhosa-accented English as the Zandalari trolls**, suggesting inherited phonological features from proto-troll ancestry. The Haranir worship **Aln'hara** (the Worldsoul of Azeroth) as their goddess — confirmed by warcraft.wiki.gg. The **first confirmed Hara'ni translations** come from the wiki: *Shul'ka* = "Hunters of Shadows", *Har'alnor* = "Village of Twilight", *Har'athir* = "Village of Memory", *Har'kuai* = "Village of Rain", *Har'mara* = "Village of Balance". The morpheme **Har' = "village"** is confirmed by four independent attestations. The *Aln-* root has four attestations (*Aln'hara*, *Alndust*, *Alnscorned*, *Rift of Aln*) and likely means "deep/source/rift/essence"; **Alndust is confirmed to be Azerite** in particle form (warcraft.wiki.gg), and *Aln* is used as a verb in the quest title "The Greater They Aln." The *Hara-* root appears in five attestations (ethnonym, language name, capital, faction, concept) and likely means "people/we." The Shul'ka are Haranir warriors, healers, and guides who sever their connection to Aln'hara to patrol the Rift of Aln. The Zur'ashar are painter-storytellers. (see [haranir/](haranir/))
- **Zandali–Elven *-da* kinship suffix**: Zandali *Fa'da* = "Father" and *Ma'da* = "Mother" share the *-da* suffix with Darnassian *An'da*/*Min'da* and Thalassian *Ann'da*/*Minn'da*. This is potentially the **first confirmed grammatical morpheme shared between the troll and elven language families**, supporting the dark troll → night elf origin theory. See Darnassian grammar.md Investigation 6 and Zandali grammar.md Investigation 6 for full cross-family analysis.
- **Hara'ni–Elven cognates**: Four candidate cognate pairs at Low–Moderate to Moderate confidence support deep genetic relationship between Hara'ni and the elven/troll family: (1) *-nir* collective suffix (Hara'ni *Haranir* ↔ Darnassian *Shala'nir*, *Alash'anir*), (2) *Har'* "village" ↔ *Aran*/*-naar* "settlement" (requires /h/ → ∅ sound change; independently supported by /h/ distribution gradient across 5 elven-family languages: Hara'ni ~20% > Nazja ~4% > Darnassian <1% > Thalassian/Shalassian 0%; potential /h/-retention cognate: Hara'ni *Hagar* ↔ Nazja *Haghazed*, Very Low confidence), (3) *Shul'* "shadow" ↔ *Shal* "shadow/twilight" (identical /ʃ_l/ consonantal skeleton; no unconfirmed sound change needed), and (4) *-athir* "memory" ↔ Darnassian *-athir*/*-ethir* in Nazjatar spire names (*Alathir*, *Lemor'athra*, *Kal'methir*; confirmed semantics in Hara'ni; *-athir*/*-athra* vowel alternation analyzed under four hypotheses — case marking, number, phonological conditioning, or different morpheme). Cross-family *-thir* parallels in nerubian (*Krik'thir*) and Shath'Yar (*K'thir*) assessed as independent /θ/-heavy phonological aesthetics (Very Low–Low confidence for cross-family connection). A systematic morphological comparison between Hara'ni and Zandali reveals structural parallels in village-naming strategy and agentive suffixes, but also a key phylogenetic difference: Hara'ni's head-initial compounding (*Har'* + qualifier) aligns with the elven pattern, while Zandali's modifier-first pattern is distinct. The *-mara* correspondence (Hara'ni *-mara* = "balance" ↔ Zandali *-mara* in *Shaol'mara*, *Kul'amara*) is upgraded to Low–Moderate confidence based on identical structural position (post-apostrophe second element) in both languages. See Darnassian grammar.md Investigation 13 for full analysis.

### Cross-Language Phonological Comparison

**Stress patterns** group WoW languages into three families:

| Stress Pattern | Languages | Description |
|---------------|-----------|-------------|
| **Initial (trochaic)** | Vrykul, Dwarven, Earthen, Eredun | Consistent word-initial stress; heavy-syllable attraction in Dwarven (*Adelgonn*); shared by titan-forged languages and Eredun |
| **Penultimate** | Darnassian, Thalassian, Shalassian, Nazja, Tol'vir | Penultimate stress in trisyllabic+ words; trochaic in disyllabics; inherited Proto-Elven feature; Tol'vir independently shares this pattern |
| **Variable/compound-dependent** | Zandali, Orcish, Nerubian, Qiraji, Ethereal, Hara'ni, Kalimag | Stress varies by word class or morphological structure; Zandali shows modifier-initial stress; nerubian shows rightward stress in compounds; Kalimag stress correlates with word class (CV grammar vs. CVC ritual) |

**Word-final sound preferences** reveal a fundamental phonological divide:

| Pattern | Languages | Examples |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| **Sonorant/vowel finals** | Darnassian, Thalassian, Shalassian, Nazja (-ei, -an, -ra, -a), Hara'ni, Ethereal | Elven family: -ei (*dorei*), -an (*Aran*), -ra (*Thalyssra*); Hara'ni: ~80%+ open-syllable final; Ethereal: -aar, -aan, -aal |
| **Voiceless obstruent finals** | Nerubian, Earthen | Nerubian: -k, -ks, -θ, -ʃ (*Nizrek*, *Ulgrax*, *Krik'thir*); Earthen: -m, -k, -θ (*Baelgrim*, *Brinthe*) |
| **Mixed** | Vrykul, Dwarven, Qiraji, Zandali, Orcish, Eredun, Kalimag | No strong final-sound constraint |

**Shalassian–Darnassian phonological conservatism**: Shalassian is the most phonologically conservative elven daughter language, retaining all core Darnassian features (/θ/, balanced vowel distribution, moderate sibilant density, simple onset clusters) with minimal innovation. Quantitatively: of 9 diagnostic phonological features compared across the four elven languages (see Shalassian grammar.md), Shalassian matches Darnassian on 8/9 features — the sole difference being marginal /k/, /g/ limited to names. By comparison, Thalassian matches on 8/9 (marginal /k/, /g/ as well), and Nazja matches on only 4/9 (/ʒ/, /dʒ/, complex onset clusters, reduced vowel distribution are all innovations). This conservatism is consistent with 10,000 years of magical isolation under the Nightwell, which prevented the contact-driven change seen in Thalassian and the physiological transformation seen in Nazja.
