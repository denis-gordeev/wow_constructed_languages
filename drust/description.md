# Drust — Description

## Overview

The Drust language is a daughter language of Vrykul, spoken by the vrykul who settled Kul Tiras after the Great Sundering. Separated from mainland Vrykul by ocean and ~10,000 years of independent development, Drust developed distinctive phonological innovations (/ʒ/, /θ/) and a unique toponymic system using the *Gol-* prefix (vs. Vrykul suffixes like *-heim*, *-garde*). The language is now effectively dead — the last living speaker is Ulfar, the last Thornspeaker — but survives in spirit form, ritual incantations, and the place names of Drustvar.

## Speakers

The Drust were a population of vrykul who settled the island of Kul Tiras before the first humans arrived there. They are the ancestral culture of the Drustvar region. The Drust language (also called Drust) is a divergent variety of Vrykul, separated from the mainland Vrykul language for millennia after the Great Sundering. It is spoken — or was spoken — by the Drust people, their surviving spirits, the Heartsbane Coven, the Thornspeakers, and the wicker/stone constructs animated by Drust magic.

In the modern era, the last truly living Drust speaker is Ulfar, the last Thornspeaker, who dwells in bear form at Ulfar's Den in Drustvar. The Heartsbane Coven (human witches taught by Gorak Tul) wield Drust magic but likely speak Common; their incantations may preserve fragments of the Drust language. Drust spirits in Thros and Ardenweald also presumably retain the language.

## History

The Drust descend from seafaring vrykul who settled Kul Tiras after the Great Sundering, approximately 10,000 years ago. Their language thus diverged from mainland Vrykul over the same period.

Key historical events affecting the language:

- **Post-Sundering isolation**: Separated from other vrykul populations, the Drust language developed independently for ~7,000+ years before human contact.
- **Druidic period**: Before Gorak Tul, the Drust practiced druidism. This period likely preserved the oldest stratum of the language, including nature-related vocabulary shared with other titan-forged druidic traditions.
- **Gorak Tul's corruption**: The sorcerer king perverted druidic rituals into death magic, likely introducing new ritual vocabulary and shifting the semantic range of existing terms (e.g., words for "growth" or "life" may have been inverted to mean "undead growth" or "corruption").
- **War against humans (~2,700 years ago)**: Contact with Gilnean humans introduced Common-language loanwords and possibly influenced Drust syntax.
- **The Thornspeaker schism**: The druidic Thornspeakers left Drust society and joined the humans, preserving an older, uncorrupted register of the language.
- **The fall of Gol Osigr**: Gorak Tul's defeat and retreat to Thros. The living Drust were wiped out; only spirits and constructs remained, potentially fossilizing the language.
- **The Heartsbane Coven (Fourth War)**: Lady Meredith Waycrest was taught Drust death magic by Gorak Tul. The Coven's rituals may contain Drust-language incantations, though this is unconfirmed.
- **Shadowlands incursion**: The Drust, led by Gorak Zhar, invaded Ardenweald from Thros. Their interactions with the Night Fae may have introduced some fae vocabulary.

## Cultural Context

- The Drust were originally a druidic vrykul culture, deeply connected to the wilds. Their language likely had extensive nature, tree, and forest vocabulary.
- Gorak Tul transformed their druidism into death magic, creating a duality in the language: the "old ways" (Thornspeaker druidism) versus the "dark ways" (death magic, Thros).
- The Drust built stone constructs and wicker constructs, suggesting specialized vocabulary for creation rituals.
- The Heartsbane Coven represents a human appropriation of Drust death magic. Their terminology (Heartsbane, hexthralled, etc.) is Common-language, not Drust.
- Drust Steles preserve carved narratives of their history, indicating a written tradition using pictographic or runic symbols.

## Phonological Features

Drust phonology is similar to Vrykul with several distinctive innovations resulting from ~10,000 years of isolation:

- **Innovative /ʒ/**: The voiced postalveolar fricative /ʒ/ in *Zhar* is not attested in mainland Vrykul. It likely developed from an earlier /j/ or /ʃ/: /j/ → /ʃ/ → /ʒ/ (paralleling Vrykul's *Gj* → /ʃ/ sound change, pushed one step further)
- **Innovative /θ/**: The voiceless dental fricative /θ/ in *Thros* is rare in mainland Vrykul. It may reflect a Drust sound change from earlier /t/ or /s/ before /ɾ/
- **Unusual onset cluster**: *Thros* /θɾɔs/ shows a /θɾ-/ onset not found in mainland Vrykul, which prefers simpler onsets before /ɾ/
- **Second-syllable stress**: *Drustvar* is pronounced "druhst-VAHR" (canon), diverging from Vrykul's typical first-syllable stress. This may reflect a genuine Drust sound change, Common-language reanalysis, or morphological stress attraction by the head *-var*
- **Gol- prefix**: The /ɡɔl-/ prefix is unique to Drust toponyms. The vowel /ɔ/ followed by /l/ is consistent with Vrykul phonotactics, but the prefix-based toponymic strategy is a structural innovation
- **Celtic/Norse blend**: Drust names blend Norse phonology (inherited from Vrykul) with Celtic-inspired elements (*Thros*, *Gol-*, *Kivarr*), reflecting the Drust's unique cultural development in Kul Tiras

See [grammar.md](grammar.md#phonology) for full consonant/vowel IPA charts.

## Writing System

The Drust possessed a runic writing system, preserved on the Drust Steles found throughout Drustvar. These standing stones carry carved pictographic or runic symbols depicting Drust history — conflict, nature worship, the Circle, and the Ritual. The script is related to the Vrykul runic tradition but appears to have diverged visually over millennia of isolation. The Thornspeaker druidic tradition likely preserved the oldest form of the script, while the corrupted death-magic register under Gorak Tul may have introduced new symbols for necromantic purposes. None of the Stele inscriptions have been translated; they are described in Common-language quest text rather than rendered in the native script.

## Dialects and Variants

- **Classical Drust (Thornspeaker Drust)**: The uncorrupted, druidic register preserved by the Thornspeakers. Ulfar is the last speaker. This likely represents the older stratum of the language, closer to mainland Vrykul.
- **Corrupted Drust (Thros Drust)**: The death-magic register developed under Gorak Tul. This includes ritual vocabulary for necromancy, binding, and construct animation. The spirits of Thros speak this variant.
- **Heartsbane Drust**: A possible third register used by the Heartsbane Coven, heavily influenced by Common and containing corrupted ritual phrases taught by Gorak Tul to Lady Waycrest. This is speculative; the Coven may use Common with Drust incantations.

## Relationship to Other Languages

- **Vrykul**: Drust is a daughter language of Vrykul, separated by geography and ~10,000 years of independent development. The Drust are explicitly identified as vrykul (canon: warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Drust). Shared features likely include basic phonological structure (Celtic/Norse-inspired phonotactics), compounding patterns, and core vocabulary. The Drust language probably preserves some archaic Vrykul features lost in Northrend/Stormheim dialects.
- **Titan**: Drust is a grand-daughter of Titan via Vrykul. The druidic connection may preserve Titan roots related to nature and creation.
- **Common (Human)**: Humans are descendants of vrykul. The Kul Tiran humans of Drustvar may have borrowed Drust words (especially place names). The extensive Drust place names in Drustvar (Gol Osigr, Gol Inath, Gol Koval, Gol Var) are likely Drust-language toponyms adopted into Common.
- **Dwarven/Gnomish**: Fellow titan-forged descendant languages; remote cognates through Titan.
- **Drustvar naming**: Many place names in Drustvar use the "Gol-" prefix (Gol Osigr, Gol Inath, Gol Koval, Gol Var). This is likely a Drust morpheme meaning "settlement," "place," or "stone/fortress." The prefix is not found in mainland Vrykul, suggesting it is a Drust innovation.

## Pronunciation Notes

- **Drustvar** is pronounced "druhst-VAHR" (canon: warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Drustvar). The stress falls on the second syllable, which is unusual compared to Vrykul's typical first-syllable stress. This may reflect a Drust sound change, or the Common-language reanalysis of a Drust word.
- The "Gol-" prefix in place names likely uses a hard /g/ (as in "go"), consistent with Vrykul phonology.
- Drust names contain phonemes not typical of Vrykul (e.g., the "th" in "Thros," the "zh" in "Zhar"), suggesting sound changes from the parent language.

## Reliability Notes

- **Extremely low attestation for the Drust language proper.** No complete Drust-language phrases with translations have been identified in canon. The Drust speak Common in-game (for gameplay reasons), and any Drust-specific dialogue is rendered in Common.
- The Drust Steles contain narrative descriptions in Common (e.g., "The carving shows a massive tree, bigger than the others marked onto the stone. It is surrounded by worshipful Drust."). These are Common-language descriptions of Drust carvings, not Drust-language texts.
- **All Drust proper names** (Gorak Tul, Gorak Zhar, Gol Osigr, Gol Inath, Gol Koval, Gol Var, Thros, Sef Iwen, Ulfar, Kivarr) are attested in canon but **none have confirmed translations**.
- The **Gol-** prefix is a strong candidate for a Drust morpheme based on the consistent pattern in place names, but its meaning is inferred, not canon-confirmed.
- The **Gorak-** element in personal names (Gorak Tul, Gorak Zhar) may be a title or name element, but this is speculative.
- The **-var** in "Drustvar" may mean "land" or "home" (parallel to Vrykul -heim), but this is inferred from the Common suffix pattern, not confirmed.
- The Heartsbane Coven's terminology (Heartsbane, Hexthralled, etc.) is Common-language, not Drust.
