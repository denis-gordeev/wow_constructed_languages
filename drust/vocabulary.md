## 6. Relationship to Vrykul Naming Patterns

### Shared Features with Vrykul

| Feature | Drust Example | Vrykul Parallel | Notes |
|---------|---------------|-----------------|-------|
| Compound personal names | Gorak Tul, Gorak Zhar | Ingvar the Plunderer, Skadi the Ruthless | Drust uses two-element names without Common-language epithets |
| Place-name suffixes | -var (Drustvar) | -heim (Stormheim, Jotunheim) | Both form "land/home" compounds |
| Title + name pattern | Gorak Tul | Drottinn Hrothgar | Vrykul uses "Drottinn + name"; Drust may use "Gorak + name" |

### Divergences from Vrykul

| Feature | Drust | Vrykul | Notes |
|---------|-------|--------|-------|
| Place-name prefix | Gol- (Gol Osigr, Gol Inath) | No equivalent | Drust innovation; not found in any Vrykul dialect |
| /ʒ/ phoneme | Zhar (/ʒɑɾ/) | Not attested | Drust may have developed the voiced postalveolar fricative |
| /θ/ phoneme | Thros (/θɾɔs/) | Rare in Vrykul | Drust may have developed /θ/ more extensively |
| Place suffix | -var | -heim | Different morpheme; may be cognate or independent development |
| Stress pattern | Second-syllable stress in "Drustvar" | First-syllable stress dominant | May reflect Common-language reanalysis, not native Drust phonology |

## 7. Phonological Observations

### Consonant Distribution in Drust Names

- **Initial clusters**: Dr- (Drust, Drustvar), Th- (Thros), G- (Gorak, Gol)
- **Notable phonemes**: /ʒ/ (Zhar), /θ/ (Thros), /v/ (var, Vadekius)
- **Vowel patterns**: Predominantly /ɑ/, /ɔ/, /ʊ/ — consistent with Vrykul phonology

### Comparison with Vrykul Phonology

| Drust Feature | Vrykul Equivalent | Analysis |
|---------------|-------------------|----------|
| Gorak /ˈɡɔɾɑk/ | Similar to Magnar, Skarvald | Consistent with Vrykul stress and consonant patterns |
| Gol /ɡɔl/ | No direct parallel | Unique Drust prefix |
| Thros /θɾɔs/ | No direct parallel | /θ/ + /ɾ/ cluster is unusual; may be a Drust innovation |
| Zhar /ʒɑɾ/ | No direct parallel | /ʒ/ is not attested in mainland Vrykul |
| -var /vɑɾ/ | -heim /haɪm/ | Both form place names; different phonological development |
| Osigr /ˈoːsɪɡɾ/ | Similar to Ymirheim structure | Compound-like structure consistent with Vrykul patterns |
