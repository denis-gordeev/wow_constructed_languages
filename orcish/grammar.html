Orcish Grammar

The grammatical rules below are inferred solely from attested canon phrases. Orcish is an under-documented constructed language with very few surviving examples, so all grammatical claims are tentative.

Morphology

Compounding

Orcish relies heavily on compounding. Every multi-morpheme attested word is a compound of two shorter elements:

CompoundElement 1Element 2Inferred Meaning
Lok'tarLok (victory)tar (uncertain)“Victory”
Lok'tar ogarLok'tar (victory)ogar (death)“Victory or death”
Mak'goraMak (duel/combat)gora (honor)“Duel of honor”
Aka'MagoshAka (blessing)Magosh (upon you/ancestors)“Blessing upon you”
Kosh'hargKosh (gathering)Harg (festival)“Gathering-festival”
Zug zugZug (work)Zug (work)“Work work” → agreement

The compounding pattern is consistently head-initial: the primary semantic element precedes the modifying or extending element.

Reduplication

Zug zug demonstrates full reduplication. The duplicated form shifts meaning from the literal (“work work”) to a pragmatic function (“agreement / yes / okay”). It is unclear whether reduplication is a productive grammatical process or limited to this single attested example.

Morpheme Boundary Marking

Two orthographic markers separate morphemes:

  1. Apostrophe (’) — the most common boundary marker: Lok'tar, Mak'gora, Aka'Magosh, Kosh'harg, Gul'dan, Lok'osh
  2. Hyphen (-) — appears in Throm-ka and Nok-Karosh

The functional difference between apostrophe and hyphen boundaries, if any, cannot be determined from available data. It may be purely orthographic.

The Lok'tar ogar Problem

The phrase Lok'tar ogar (“Victory or death”) presents an analytical difficulty:

  • Analysis A: Lok' = victory, -tar = or, ogar = death → “Victory-or death”
  • Analysis B: Lok'tar = victory (unanalyzable), ogar = “or death” → “Victory or-death”
  • Analysis C: Lok'tar = victory, ogar = death, with an unexpressed conjunction → “Victory [or] death”
Analysis A is the most common fan analysis but requires -tar to function as a conjunction, which finds no other attestation. Analysis B requires ogar to be a compound of an unknown conjunction + “death.” Analysis C assumes zero-marking for disjunction. None is confirmable with available data.

Syntax

Word Order

No full sentences are attested, making syntactic analysis impossible. All surviving examples are:

  • Interjections / formulae: Zug zug, Lok'tar ogar, Throm-ka
  • Compound nouns: Mak'gora, Kosh'harg

If Lok'tar ogar reflects underlying syntax, the order is V V (victory death), but this is more likely a formulaic pair than evidence of verb-final or verb-initial order.

Consonant Clusters

Orcish permits heavy onset and coda clusters:

  • Onset: /gr/ (Grom), /θr/ (Throm, Thrall), /sk/ (none attested but likely)
  • Coda: /ʃ/ (Grommash), /rg/ (harg)

Vowel Inventory

Vowels are typically short and open:

  • /a/ — the most frequent vowel
  • /o/ — common in stressed syllables
  • /u/ — appears in zug, bur
  • /i/ — rare; appears in Orgrim

Diphthongs are not clearly attested.

Phonotactic Patterns

  1. Guttural preference: A preponderance of velar and glottal consonants (/g/, /k/, /h/) gives Orcish its characteristic harsh, guttural quality.
  2. Consonant-heavy syllables: Most attested words are CVC or CVC.CVC structures. Open syllables (CV) are less common.
  3. Prominent /r/: The alveolar trill /r/ appears frequently, often in clusters (Grom, Throm, Garrosh, Saurfang).
  4. Stress pattern: Cannot be determined from written attestations alone.

Sound Symbolism

The harsh, percussive phonology aligns with orcish cultural values — strength, directness, combat readiness. The prevalence of stops and gutturals over fricatives and approximants reflects this aesthetic.

Summary of Limitations

  • No full sentences are attested; syntax is entirely unknown.
  • Morphemic analysis of compounds rests on very few data points and is circular.
  • The cross-faction language filter generates many “Orcish” strings that are not genuine vocabulary.
  • Blizzard has not published any official grammar or lexicon.
  • All rules above are hypotheses, not confirmed features of the language.
