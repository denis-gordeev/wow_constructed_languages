# Common (Human Language) — Overview

## Overview

Common is the native language of the humans of Azeroth and serves as the primary trade language of the Alliance. It descends from Vrykul, which descends from Titan, representing a lineage of simplification from the Titan-forged precursor through the Vrykul warrior culture to the human trade lingua franca. Common presents a unique attestation challenge: it is the most frequently heard language in WoW (rendered as English), but as a linguistic object within the Warcraft universe, it has almost no distinct attestation beyond the confirmed Vrykul etymology of *Tirisfal* = "Tyr's Fall" and the *-garde* suffix in *Stromgarde*. Whether Common IS English or is simply TRANSLATED as English for gameplay is unresolved.

## Speakers

Common is the native language of the humans of Azeroth and serves as the primary trade language of the Alliance. It is spoken by humans across the Eastern Kingdoms, Kalimdor, and beyond. As the lingua franca of Azeroth, Common is also understood (to varying degrees) by members of other Alliance races and some neutral parties.

Common descends from Vrykul, which descends from Titan. Humans are the "runt" offspring of vrykul who were affected by the Curse of Flesh — they grew smaller and weaker but more numerous. The human language underwent significant simplification from its Vrykul ancestor, developing more analytic syntax, reduced morphology, and simplified phonology.

## History

The Common language traces its origins to the vrykul who were affected by the Curse of Flesh and abandoned their titan-forged heritage. According to *Chronicle Volume 1*, these "runt" vrykul were cast out by King Ymiron and eventually became the humans of the Eastern Kingdoms.

Key historical events affecting the language:

- **The Curse of Flesh and the exodus from Northrend**: The earliest humans were vrykul who were born small and weak due to the Curse of Flesh. King Ymiron ordered these "runts" to be killed, but many were saved and sent south by sympathetic parents. These proto-humans would have spoken a form of Vrykul that began to diverge immediately.
- **The Arathi Empire (~2,800 years ago)**: Thoradin unified the human tribes and established the Arathi Empire with its capital at Strom. This period likely saw the standardization of Common as a distinct language from Vrykul. The *Tirisfal* place name (confirmed as "Tyr's Fall" in Vrykul) was adopted into Common during this period.
- **The Seven Kingdoms**: After the fall of the Arathi Empire, humans split into seven kingdoms (Lordaeron, Stormwind, Gilneas, Alterac, Kul Tiras, Stromgarde, Dalaran). Dialectal divergence may have occurred, but Common remained mutually intelligible across all kingdoms.
- **The First and Second Wars**: Contact with orcs introduced Orcish loanwords. The wars also reinforced Common as a lingua franca for the Alliance.
- **Modern era**: Common is the dominant language of Azeroth, used for diplomacy, trade, and daily communication by most Alliance races.

## Cultural Context

- Common is both a native language (for humans) and a trade language (for all Alliance races). This dual function has likely contributed to its simplification — trade languages tend to reduce morphological complexity for easier learning by non-native speakers.
- Human naming conventions show a mix of Vrykul-influenced names (Thoradin, Arthas) and Common innovations (Stormwind, Proudmoore). The shift from Vrykul-style compound names to English-style given + surname pairs reflects the linguistic divergence.
- The *Tirisfal* place name is a key piece of evidence for the Vrykul→Common transition: it shows that Common adopted Vrykul place names but no longer parses their morphological structure.

## Dialects and Variants

- **Standard Common**: The prestige dialect used in Stormwind and for official Alliance communication. Rendered as English in-game.
- **Lordaeron Common**: The dialect of the northern human kingdoms (Lordaeron, Stromgarde, Alterac, Dalaran). Now largely extinct due to the Scourge, but preserved by refugees and the Argent Crusade.
- **Kul Tiran Common**: The dialect of Kul Tiras, influenced by Drust place names and vocabulary. May preserve more Vrykul features due to geographic proximity to Drustvar.
- **Gilnean Common**: The dialect of Gilneas, isolated behind the Greymane Wall for years. May have developed unique features.
- **Trade Common**: A simplified register used for commerce and diplomacy. May be even more analytic than Standard Common.

## Relationship to Other Languages

- **Vrykul**: Common's direct ancestor. The transition from Vrykul to Common involved simplification of consonant clusters, loss of productive compounding, development of analytic syntax, and reduction of the vowel system (loss of /y/). Key evidence: *Tirisfal* (Vrykul compound fossilized in Common), *-garde* (Vrykul suffix surviving in *Stromgarde*).
- **Titan**: Common's grandparent language. Titan morphemes reached Common through Vrykul: *Tyr* → *Tiris-* (in *Tirisfal*), *-garde* (enclosure, from Vrykul *-garde*, replacing Titan *Uld-*).
- **Drust**: A parallel Vrykul offshoot. Common and Drust are "cousin" languages through Vrykul. The Kul Tiran dialect of Common may contain Drust loanwords (especially place names: *Gol Osigr*, *Gol Inath*, *Drustvar*).
- **Dwarven**: Fellow Titan-forged descendant (via Earthen). Common and Dwarven share deep structural features from Titan but diverged through different cultural paths. Extensive borrowing has occurred through centuries of cohabitation in Ironforge.
- **Gnomish**: Fellow Titan-forged descendant (via Mechagnome). Similar relationship to Dwarven.
- **Orcish**: Contact language since the First War. Some mutual borrowing likely occurred, though documentation is limited.
- **Thalassian / Darnassian**: Elven languages in contact with Common. Some elven loanwords may exist in Common (e.g., terms for magic, nature).

## Key Phonological Features

- **Simplified consonant clusters**: Compared to Vrykul, Common has fewer complex initial clusters. Vrykul *Vr-*, *Sk-*, *Hr-* are simplified or lost.
- **Loss of /y/**: The Vrykul front rounded vowel /y/ (as in *Ymiron*) would have been unrounded to /i/ in Common.
- **Variable stress**: Unlike Vrykul's consistent initial-syllable stress, Common has English-style variable stress.
- **Analytic tendency**: Common prefers separate words over compound formations, consistent with the simplification from Vrykul.

## Writing System

Common uses the same alphabet as English (the Latin script), as depicted in all in-game texts, books, signs, and NPC dialogue throughout the Alliance zones. This is a game-mechanic representation — whether Common within the Warcraft universe uses a script identical to the Latin alphabet, or whether this is a translation convention for the player's benefit, is unresolved. The most likely interpretation is that Common has its own alphabetic script that Blizzard renders as English for gameplay purposes.

Key observations about Common writing in lore:

- **Vrykul runic inheritance**: As Common descends from Vrykul, its earliest written form likely used Vrykul runes or a simplified derivative. The *Tirisfal* inscription ("Tyr's Fall") would originally have been carved in Vrykul runes
- **Arathi standardization**: The Arathi Empire (~2,800 years ago) likely standardized a Common script for administrative purposes, potentially simplifying Vrykul runes into a more alphabetic system
- **Dwarven influence**: Centuries of cohabitation with Dwarves in Ironforge may have introduced Dwarven runic conventions into Common writing, particularly for technical and architectural inscriptions
- **Dalaran scholarship**: The mages of Dalaran produce extensive written works in Common, including spell formulae that may blend Common and arcane notation

## Attestation and Reliability

Common presents a unique attestation challenge. It is the most frequently heard language in WoW (rendered as English), but as a linguistic object within the Warcraft universe, it has almost no distinct attestation. The question of whether Common IS English or is simply TRANSLATED as English for gameplay is unresolved.

### What is reliable

- Humans descend from vrykul (confirmed by *Chronicle Volume 1*).
- *Tirisfal* = "Tyr's Fall" (confirmed Vrykul etymology).
- *-garde* in *Stromgarde* is a Vrykul suffix.
- Common has undergone simplification from Vrykul (inferred from structural comparison).
- Common serves as a trade language for the Alliance (confirmed by in-game mechanics).

### What is uncertain

- Whether Common has a distinct grammar from English or is simply English within the Warcraft universe.
- The degree of mutual intelligibility between Common and Vrykul.
- The extent of Vrykul vocabulary preservation in Common.
- Whether human names contain Vrykul morphemes or are Common innovations.

### What is unknown

- Common's distinct phonology (as separate from English).
- Common's distinct syntax (as separate from English).
- The timeline of Vrykul→Common divergence.
- The specific sound changes from Vrykul to Common.
