# Common (Human) Vocabulary

All entries are attested in World of Warcraft canon sources (in-game dialogue, NPC speech, quest text, or official Blizzard publications). No invented words are included. IPA transcriptions are approximate.

**Note on attestation:** Common is the universal trade language of Azeroth, spoken by humans and used as a lingua franca between Alliance races. In-game, Common is rendered as English for gameplay. As a constructed language within the Warcraft universe, Common has extremely low linguistic attestation — most "Common" dialogue is simply English, and no distinct Common-language grammar or vocabulary has been published by Blizzard. This file documents the few proper names and place names that may preserve Common-language morphemes traceable to the Vrykul→Titan lineage.

## Relationship to Other Languages

### Vrykul (Direct Ancestor)

Common descends directly from Vrykul. Humans are the offspring of vrykul who were affected by the Curse of Flesh and grew smaller. The linguistic relationship is confirmed by:

- The wiki notes that Common shares many similar words with Vrykul, Dwarven, and Gnomish
- The place name *Tirisfal* is confirmed as a Vrykul word adopted into Common
- The suffix *-garde* in *Stromgarde* is directly inherited from Vrykul *-garde* (enclosure)
- Historical human names (*Thoradin*, *Lothar*) may contain Vrykul morpheme elements

### Titan (Grandparent Language)

Common is a grand-daughter of Titan via Vrykul. Any Titan morphemes in Common would have been inherited through Vrykul. The evidence for direct Titan→Common borrowing is limited to what Vrykul preserved:

- *Tirisfal* (Tyr's Fall) contains *Tiris-* (Tyr), which traces to Titan *Tyr* (the Keeper)
- *-garde* traces to Vrykul *-garde*, which replaced Titan *Uld-* naming

### Dwarven and Gnomish (Parallel Descendants)

Common, Dwarven, and Gnomish all descend from Titan through different titan-forged lineages (Vrykul, Earthen, Mechagnome). They are parallel descendants, not ancestor-descendant. Shared features include:

- Right-headed compounding (inherited from Titan)
- Some shared vocabulary for basic concepts
- Similar phonological inventories (simplified from Titan)

### Orcish (Contact Language)

Common and Orcish have been in contact since the First War. Extensive borrowing likely occurred in both directions, though no specific Common→Orcish or Orcish→Common loanwords have been documented in lore.

## Key Phonological Features

- **Simplified consonant clusters**: Compared to Vrykul, Common likely has fewer complex initial clusters. Names like *Thoradin* and *Arthas* show simpler onsets than Vrykul names like *Vrykul* or *Skarvald*.
- **Loss of /y/**: The Vrykul front rounded vowel /y/ (as in *Ymiron*, *Hyldnir*) would likely have been unrounded to /i/ in Common, as English-type languages do not maintain front rounded vowels.
- **Analytic syntax**: Common likely developed more analytic syntax (relying on word order rather than inflection) as part of the simplification from Vrykul.
- **English as proxy**: In-game, Common is rendered as English. Whether this means Common IS English-like (analytic, SVO, low inflection) or is simply translated into English for gameplay is unclear. The structural simplification from Vrykul is consistent with Common being English-like in typology.

## Attestation and Reliability

Common is a **paradoxically attested** language. It is the most frequently heard language in WoW (rendered as English), but as a linguistic object within the Warcraft universe, it has almost no distinct attestation. Everything said in "Common" is simply English, and no Blizzard publication has treated Common as a distinct constructed language with its own grammar or vocabulary.

### What is reliable

- *Tirisfal* = "Tyr's Fall" (confirmed by *Chronicle Volume 1*).
- Humans descend from vrykul (confirmed by *Chronicle Volume 1*).
- Common shares many similar words with Vrykul, Dwarven, and Gnomish (noted by the wiki).
- *-garde* in *Stromgarde* is a Vrykul suffix (structural inheritance).
- Common has undergone significant simplification from Vrykul (inferred from the "runt" vrykul narrative and observed structural differences).

### What is uncertain

- Whether human names (*Thoradin*, *Arthas*, *Lothar*) contain Vrykul morphemes or are simply Common names.
- The extent of Vrykul vocabulary preservation in Common.
- Whether Common has a distinct grammar from English or is simply English within the Warcraft universe.
- The sound changes from Vrykul to Common.

### What is unknown

- Common grammar and syntax (as distinct from English).
- Common vocabulary (as distinct from English).
- The degree of mutual intelligibility between Common and Vrykul.
- The timeline of Vrykul→Common divergence.
