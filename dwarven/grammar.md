# Dwarven Grammar

## Phonology

- The language is described as guttural, influenced by the Titan language (inferred from lore descriptions)
- Consonant clusters are common, particularly initial clusters (inferred) from Khaz, Grim, Brann
- The phonology suggests a preference for stops and fricatives over fricatives alone (inferred)
- Proper names show both Germanic-style roots (Magni, Muradin, Brann) and more exotic forms (Thaurissan)

## Morphology

### Compounding

Place names suggest a compounding pattern where two elements are joined to form a descriptive name (inferred):

- **Dun Morogh** = Dun + Morogh → hill/fortress + frost/cold → "Frost Fortress" (uncertain)
- **Grim Batol** = Grim + Batol → dark + hold → "Dark Hold" (uncertain)
- **Khaz Modan** = Khaz + Modan → Khaz's + mountain → "Mountain of Khaz" (inferred)
- **Bael'dun** = Bael + dun → stone/earth + fortress → "Stone Fortress" (uncertain)

Unlike Taur-ahe, compounding does not appear to use apostrophes as regular markers. Bael'dun is an exception that may reflect a Titan influence (inferred).

### Prefix "Khaz-"

- Appears to be a possessive or honorific construction referencing Khaz'goroth (inferred)
- "Khaz Modan" may mean "Khaz's Mountain" with Khaz as possessor (inferred)

### Suffix "-issan"

- Appears in "Thaurissan"; may denote a person or ruler (uncertain)
- Too few examples to determine if this is a productive suffix

### The "Uld-" Prefix

- Ulduar, Uldum, Uldaman all share the "Uld-" element
- This is Titan language, not Dwarven, but Dwarves may use it as a loan (inferred)
- "Uld-" may mean "city of the makers" or "Titan place" (uncertain)

## Syntax

- No sentence-level attestation exists
- Word order cannot be determined
- The language may follow a modifier-head structure in compounds (inferred) from Dun Morogh (fortress + descriptor)

## Titan-Dwarven Linguistic Connection

Dwarves are descended from the Earthen, titan-forged stone beings created by Khaz'goroth. The Dwarven language preserves several morphemes of Titan origin:

### Confirmed Titan Loans

- **Uld-** — The prefix meaning "city" or "facility" appears in Dwarven-associated place names (*Ulduar*, *Uldum*, *Uldaman*). This is a direct Titan loanword, not a native Dwarven morpheme.
- **Khaz** — The root meaning "forge" or "shape" appears in both the Titan creator's name (*Khaz'goroth* = "Shaper of Worlds") and the Dwarven kingdom (*Khaz Modan* = "Mountain of Khaz"). Dwarves likely preserved this as a proper-name element rather than a productive morpheme.
- **Earthen** — The name of the titan-forged progenitors is a Titan designation, not a native Dwarven word.

### Possible Sound Correspondences

| Dwarven | Titan | Proposed Correspondence | Confidence |
|---------|-------|------------------------|-----------|
| -modan | -dor (in *Kalimdor*) | Both relate to land/mountain; *-dor* = "land" in Titan, *-modan* = "mountain" in Dwarven | Low |
| -dun | -den (in *Ra-den*) | Both appear in fortification/authority contexts | Very Low |

These correspondences are highly speculative. The sample size is too small to confirm regular sound changes between Titan and Dwarven.

## Limitations

Dwarven grammar is almost entirely unattested beyond place name decomposition. The compounding patterns observed in toponyms may not reflect everyday syntax. Many "Dwarven" place names could be Common renderings of original Dwarven terms. The Titan-Dwarven connection is inferential; no Blizzard source explicitly confirms linguistic descent.

## Investigation: Dwarven *Dun-* / *Durn-* and Common *Durnholde*

The Dwarven element *Dun* (attested in *Dun Morogh*, *Dun Modr*, *Bael'dun*) is inferred to mean "hill" or "fortress." The Common place name *Durnholde* (a keep in Hillsbrad Foothills) contains the element *Durn-*, which may be related.

### Arguments for a connection

1. **Phonological similarity:** Dwarven *Dun* /dʌn/ and Common *Durn* /dɜːrn/ differ by a single epenthetic /r/. The insertion of /r/ between a vowel and a following nasal or consonant is a known phenomenon in several language families (cf. the "linking r" in non-rhotic English dialects; Latin *honorem* → French *honneur* with /r/ retained only in certain environments).

2. **Semantic consistency:** If *Dun* = "fortress/hill" in Dwarven and *Durn-* = the same morpheme in Common, then *Durnholde* = "Hill-hold" or "Fortress-keep" — a tautological compound parallel to *Ironforge* (iron + forge) and *Dun Morogh* (fortress + frost). Tautological compounds are extremely common in place names cross-linguistically (cf. *Sahara Desert* = "desert desert"; *Mount Fujiyama* = "mountain Fuji-mountain").

3. **Dwarven–Common contact:** Dwarves and humans have been in close alliance since the War of the Three Hammers and the founding of Stromgarde. Borrowing of Dwarven place-name elements into Common is entirely plausible, especially for fortifications (the Dwarves are renowned builders). *Durnholde* is in the Hillsbrad Foothills, directly south of the Dwarven homeland — a region of intense Dwarven–Human contact.

4. **Proposed sound change: Dwarven *Dun* → Common *Durn*.**

   The phonological development could proceed as follows:
   - Dwarven *Dun* /dʌn/ was borrowed into Common
   - In the compound *Dun-holde*, the sequence /n.h/ developed an epenthetic /r/ through a dissimilation process: /n/ → /nr/ before /h/
   - This produced *Durn-holde* /dɜːrnhoʊld/
   - The epenthetic /r/ may have been reinforced by the Common tendency to develop rhotic consonants in stressed syllables before nasals (cf. *Stormgarde* where /r/ appears medially in *Storm*)

   Alternative phonological pathway: If the original Dwarven form was *Durn* rather than *Dun* (i.e., the Dwarven dialect that lent the word had an /r/ that the main Dwarven dialect lost), then *Durnholde* would be a direct loan with no sound change needed. This is possible but requires assuming dialectal variation in Dwarven that is not independently attested.

5. **Titan chain: *-den* → *Dun* / *Durn*.**

   If the Dwarven *Dun* and the Titan element *-den* (as in *Ra-den*) share an origin, the full chain would be:
   - Titan *-den* (honorific/authority) → Dwarven *Dun* (fortress/hill) → Common *Durn* (via Dwarven loan)
   - The semantic shift from "authority/honorific" (Titan) to "fortress/hill" (Dwarven) is consistent with the general pattern of Titan vocabulary narrowing in meaning among the titan-forged descendants (cf. *Khaz* = "forge/shape" in Titan → "forge" specifically in Dwarven).

### Arguments against a connection

1. **Coincidental similarity:** *Durn-* in *Durnholde* could be an independent Common root unrelated to Dwarven *Dun*. The Old English word *dyrne* means "hidden, secret," and *Durnholde* could mean "hidden hold" — a fitting name for a mountain keep. This would require no borrowing and no sound changes.

2. **No other *Durn-* names exist:** If *Durn-* were a productive Common morpheme borrowed from Dwarven, one might expect other *Durn-* place names. *Durnholde* is the only attested example.

3. **The *Dun* → *Durn* sound change is not attested elsewhere:** No other Dwarven→Common borrowings show epenthetic /r/ insertion. This makes the proposed sound change ad hoc.

4. **Dwarven *Dun* itself is uncertain:** The inference that *Dun* = "fortress/hill" is based on three place names (Dun Morogh, Dun Modr, Bael'dun), none of which have confirmed translations. *Dun* could simply be a proper-name element with no independent meaning.

### Assessment

| Analysis | Plausibility | Notes |
|----------|-------------|-------|
| *Durn-* = Dwarven *Dun* (borrowed with epenthetic /r/) | Low–Medium | Phonologically possible; supported by Dwarven–Human contact; but the /r/ epenthesis is ad hoc |
| *Durn-* = Common root meaning "hidden" (cf. OE *dyrne*) | Medium | Simpler; no borrowing required; but the semantic fit is less specific |
| *Durn-* and *Dun* share a common Titan origin (*-den*) | Low | Requires assuming both Dwarven and Common preserved a Titan morpheme independently; possible but unconfirmed |
| Coincidence: *Durn-* is unrelated to *Dun* | Medium | Always a possibility with a single data point |

**Confidence: Low–Medium** for a Dwarven *Dun* → Common *Durn* connection. The geographic and cultural context (Dwarven–Human alliance, proximity of Durnholde to Dwarven lands) supports borrowing, but the phonological mechanism is not well-attested and a coincidental or independent Common etymology is equally plausible.
