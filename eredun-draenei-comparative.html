Comparative Eredar Linguistics

A comparative analysis of the two attested branches of the Eredar language family: Draenei (the language of the exiles who fled Argus) and Man'ari Eredun (the language of the eredar who accepted Sargeras's offer and became the Burning Legion's architects). Both descend from a common ancestor — Proto-Eredar — spoken on Argus approximately 25,000 years before the First War.

Methodological caveat: The attested corpus for each branch is extremely small (~15–25 items total across both languages). All reconstructions and sound correspondences are highly provisional. Forms marked in brown are reconstructed; forms marked in gray italics have uncertain glosses. With ~25,000 years of separation — more than three times the time depth separating the elven languages — the potential for unrecognizable divergence is enormous. Many cognates may simply no longer be identifiable.

Cognate Sets

The following sets identify words across Draenei and Eredun that derive from a common Proto-Eredar root. Shared roots are highlighted in rose. Given the extreme time depth, only morphemes embedded in proper names are available for comparison.

Set 1: Exile/Refuge draen

draen- Draenei, Draenor (not securely attested) exile / refuge High

Analysis: The morpheme draen appears in the Draenei self-designation Draenei ("exiled ones") and the toponym Draenor ("exile world" or "refuge land"). This is the most securely attested root in the Draenei branch. Its absence from the attested Man'ari Eredun corpus likely reflects the small sample size — the Man'ari would have had no reason to preserve a term for "exile," since they did not flee. The root may survive in unattested Eredun forms, or it may have been semantically shifted or lost entirely in the Man'ari branch.

Set 2: Collective suffix -ei

-ei Draenei (possibly in unattested forms) ones / people (collective) High

Analysis: The suffix -ei in Draenei is interpreted as a collective suffix meaning "ones" or "people," yielding Draen-ei = "exiled ones." This suffix is almost certainly inherited from Proto-Eredar, as it parallels the -dorei pattern in the elven languages. Its absence from the Man'ari corpus may again reflect the small sample — the Man'ari refer to themselves as Eredar, where the collective formation uses -ar rather than -ei. If both -ei and -ar were Proto-Eredar collective suffixes with different semantic nuances, this would represent a rare case of differential survival.

Analysis: The suffix -or in Draenor is interpreted as a toponymic suffix meaning "world" or "land," yielding Draen-or = "exile world" or "refuge land." This is consistent with the pattern of world-naming in Warcraft (cf. Azeroth, Argus). The suffix is confidently reconstructed for Proto-Eredar because of its semantic specificity and its appearance in the only Draenei toponym whose morphology can be analyzed. Whether Argus contains the same suffix is unclear — if Argus = Arg-us, then -us may be a related (or unrelated) toponymic suffix in the Eredun branch, possibly reflecting a sound change or > us.

Analysis: The morpheme man is attested in Man'ari, the Draenei term for the corrupted eredar (and, by extension, for all demons). Velen's Draenei use Man'ari as a pejorative — "those who are wrong/corrupt." The fact that this term is used by the Draenei about the Man'ari suggests it may be a Draenei semantic innovation (applying an existing word for "wrong" to the corrupt), or it may preserve an older Eredar root that the Man'ari themselves have abandoned. If the Man'ari do not use Man'ari as a self-designation (preferring Eredar), this root may have been lost or semantically shifted in the Man'ari branch.

If sha means "light," it is plausibly a Draenei innovation — a vocabulary item developed after the Draenei's encounter with the naaru and the Holy Light. There is no attested cognate in Man'ari Eredun, and the Man'ari's rejection of the Light makes it unlikely they would preserve a root for "light" in the same spiritual sense. However, it is also possible that sha is an inherited Proto-Eredar root for "light/brightness" that was abandoned by the Man'ari and semantically elevated by the Draenei under naaru influence.

Analysis: Karabor (the Draenei name for the Black Temple) can be segmented as Kara-bor, where kara may mean "temple" or "holy place." The Eredun toponym Mac'Aree (a region of Argus) has been speculatively linked to the same root: if Mac'Aree contains a reflex of kara, it might reflect a sound change such as apheresis (kara > k'a) or a different morphological segmentation. However, this is extremely speculative. The phonological distance between kara and the relevant portion of Mac'Aree is significant, and Mac'Aree may contain an entirely different root. This cognate set should be treated as a hypothesis for future investigation rather than an established correspondence.

Sound Correspondences

The following sound correspondences are hypothesized between Draenei and Man'ari Eredun. With only ~15–25 attested forms across both branches and ~25,000 years of separation, these are highly speculative hypotheses, not confirmed regular sound changes.

Correspondence 1: Rhotic stability

Proto-EredarDraenei reflexEredun reflexEvidence
r r r Draenor, Eredar, Karabor

Inference: The rhotic /r/ appears in both branches in similar positions. While the attested forms are too few to establish a regular correspondence, rhotic stability is cross-linguistically common and would be unsurprising over any time depth. The prominence of /r/ in both branches (Eredar, Draenor, Karabor, Sargeras) suggests it was phonemically robust in Proto-Eredar.

Correspondence 2: Possible apheresis in Eredun

Proto-EredarDraenei reflexEredun reflexEvidence
kara- Karabor Mac'Aree (if k'a < kara) Single, uncertain pair

Inference: If both Karabor and Mac'Aree contain a reflex of kara "temple/holy place," then Man'ari Eredun may have undergone apheresis (loss of initial syllable): kara > k'a. However, the phonological relationship is not clean — the proposed reflex appears as 'Aree or k'aree in Mac'Aree, which involves vowel changes and possible consonant mutation as well. An alternative analysis is that Mac'Aree contains a different root entirely. This is very speculative with such limited data.

Correspondence 3: Consonant cluster development

Inference: The Man'ari Eredun names Kil'jaeden and Archimonde show more complex consonant clusters and heavier syllable codas than the attested Draenei forms. If this pattern is representative, Man'ari Eredun may have developed harsher phonotactics — possibly driven by phonological drift, but also possibly reflecting the influence of demonic/fel energy on the Man'ari's vocal apparatus over millennia. However, this hypothesis rests on only two proper names and may simply reflect individual name etymologies rather than a systematic sound change.

Correspondence 4: The -ari / -ar correspondence

Inference: If -ari and -ar are cognate, the most parsimonious sound change is apocope of the final vowel in Man'ari Eredun: ari > ar / _#. This would parallel common cross-linguistic tendencies for final vowel loss. However, -ar and -ari could also represent distinct suffixes that happened to be semantically similar, or -ar could be a truncated borrowing. The evidence is insufficient to confirm a regular sound change.

Correspondence 5: Possible -or / -us correspondence

Proto-EredarDraenei reflexEredun reflexEvidence
-or Draenor Argus (if -us < -or) Single, uncertain pair

Inference: If the -us in Argus is cognate with the -or in Draenor, this would imply a sound change or > us in Man'ari Eredun. However, this would require both a vowel shift (o > u) and a consonant shift (r > s), which is phonetically unusual as a single change. It is more likely that Argus and Draenor use different toponymic formations, or that Argus is a monomorphemic proper name. This correspondence should be considered very weak.

The Eredar Etymology

The self-designation Eredar is used by both the Draenei (as a historical/ethnic term) and the Man'ari (as their primary self-designation). Its etymology is unclear:

The fact that both branches preserve Eredar as a term confirms it is inherited from Proto-Eredar. However, without knowing the root ered's original meaning, the full etymology remains elusive. Limitations

Critical limitations of this analysis:
  1. Extremely small corpus: The total attested vocabulary across both branches is approximately 15–25 items, almost exclusively proper names and ethnonyms. No sentences, verb forms, or grammatical paradigms are attested. This makes it impossible to confirm any regular sound change or grammatical reconstruction.
  2. Extreme time depth: With ~25,000 years of separation, the two branches have had far more time to diverge than any real-world language family we can reconstruct. (Proto-Indo-European is dated to ~6,000 years ago; Proto-Afroasiatic to ~10,000–15,000.) By analogy, many cognates may have diverged beyond recognition, and the shared forms we can identify likely represent a small, conservative subset of the vocabulary (ethnonyms, toponymic suffixes) that are resistant to change.
  3. Proper-name bias: Almost all attested forms are proper names. Proper names are subject to special linguistic processes — archaization, folk etymology, morphological reanalysis — that may obscure genuine historical relationships.
  4. No native speaker testimony: We have no grammatical descriptions, dictionaries, or native-speaker intuitions for either language. All glosses are inferred from context, English translations, or in-game dialogue.
  5. Possible contact effects: The Man'ari Eredun have been immersed in the multilingual environment of the Burning Legion for 25,000 years. Loanwords from Eredun into other demonic languages (and vice versa) may contaminate the comparative picture. Similarly, the Draenei have been in contact with numerous species during their long exile.
  6. Magical influence on language: The transformation of the eredar into Man'ari through fel energy may have caused physical changes to their vocal apparatus, potentially introducing sound changes with no natural phonetic motivation. This is a factor with no real-world analogue and complicates the application of the comparative method.
  7. Circular reasoning risk: Many reconstructions are supported by only a single form in a single branch. The "reconstruction" in these cases is simply the attested form projected backward, with no corroborating evidence from the other branch. These should be regarded as hypotheses about Proto-Eredar, not confirmed reconstructions.
