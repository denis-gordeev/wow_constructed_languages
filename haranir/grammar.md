# Hara'ni Grammar

Grammar rules below are **inferred from attested examples only**. The Hara'ni language has no attested sentences — the entire corpus consists of proper names, place names, and cultural designations from *The War Within* (2024) and *Midnight* (2026). Patterns are noted where multiple examples support a rule. Speculative extensions are marked [speculative].

### Notable Phonological Features

1. **High /h/ frequency**: Six of ~15 attested Hara'ni names contain /h/ (*Hagar*, *Hannan*, *Harandar*, *Hara'ni*, *On'hiea*, *Ran'hune*). This is unusually high compared to other WoW languages. /h/ may be a phonologically salient consonant in Hara'ni, possibly reflecting a connection to breath/spirit vocabulary (the Haranir are associated with bat-like shapeshifting and the "call from deep within").

2. **Apostrophe as morpheme boundary**: The apostrophe in Hara'ni names consistently marks compound boundaries between two content morphemes (e.g., *Ter'* + *-kova*, *Nye'* + *-rangi*). This is identical to the medial apostrophe pattern in nerubian and qiraji, and distinct from the initial apostrophe pattern in Shath'Yar.

3. **Geminate consonants**: *Hannan* (*-nn-*) and *Amarakk* (*-kk-*) contain geminate consonants. If these are phonologically significant (not merely orthographic), Hara'ni would share this feature with qiraji (*-naxx*).

4. **Syllable simplicity**: Hara'ni names tend toward simple CV or CVC syllables. The most complex name in the corpus is *Na'layro* (/nɑːˈleɪroʊ/), which still follows a straightforward CVC.CV.CV pattern after the apostrophe boundary.

5. **Palato-alveolar consonants**: *Shul'ka* (/ʃ/), *Chonon* (/tʃ/), *Or'jan* (/dʒ/), *Zhakir* (/ʒ/) introduce a new series of palato-alveolar consonants not previously attested. These may reflect a phonological subsystem used primarily in compound-forming elements or religious vocabulary.

6. **Dental fricative /θ/**: *Har'athir* and *Dreth'amar* introduce /θ/ (th-sound), a relatively rare phoneme in WoW constructed languages. In Hara'ni, /θ/ appears in compound suffixes.

Vowel distribution skews heavily toward /a/ and /i/, with /e/ moderate and /o/, /u/ less common.

The sequence /kw/ in *Har'kuai* suggests that /w/ may function as a semivowel in consonant clusters, or that *kuai* represents a diphthong /kwaɪ/.

### Stress Pattern

No canon pronunciation guide exists for Hara'ni names. Stress is inferred from the morphological structure:

- *Harandar* — likely stress on second syllable: ha-RAN-dar (paralleling Earthen *Dornogal* = dor-NO-gal)
- *Hara'ni* — likely stress on first syllable: HA-ra-ni (language name typically stresses the root)
- Compound names with apostrophe — stress likely falls on the second element: ter-KO-va, ran-HU-ne

These are entirely speculative.

## Morphology

### The *Hara-* Root

The *Hara-* root is the most securely attested morpheme in Hara'ni, appearing in five independent attestations:

| Name | *Hara-* Context | Proposed Function |
|------|-----------------|-------------------|
| *Haranir* | *Hara-* + *-nir* | Ethnonym: "Hara-people" |
| *Hara'ni* | *Hara'* + *-ni* | Language name: "Hara-tongue" |
| *Harandar* | *Haran-* + *-dar* | Toponym: "Hara-land" |
| *Hara'ti* | *Hara'* + *-ti* | Faction: "Hara-group" |
| *Aln'hara* | *Aln'* + *-hara* | Concept: "[Aln]-call" |

The *Hara-* root may have two related meanings:
1. **"People/we"** — as the ethnonymic root in *Haranir*, *Hara'ni*, *Harandar*, *Hara'ti*
2. **"Call/song"** — as the reversed form *-hara* in *Aln'hara* ("the call from deep within")

If both meanings derive from a single root, the semantic connection would be: "those who hear the call" → "the called people" → "we, the people." This is semantically coherent but entirely speculative.

[speculative]: The *Hara-* root may be related to the Haranir's ability to hear "Aln'hara" — the call from deep within the world. If *hara* originally meant "call" or "song," the ethnonym *Haranir* would mean "the called ones" or "those who hear the call," with *Hara'ni* = "language of the called" and *Harandar* = "land of the called." This analysis would unify all five attestations under a single semantic root.

**Confidence: Moderate** for *Hara-* as a genuine Hara'ni morpheme.

Note: *Har'* = "village" (confirmed) may be related to *Hara-* or may be a separate morpheme. The semantic ambiguity remains: if *Har'* derives from *Hara-*, then *Har'alnor* = "village-of-the-people twilight" rather than just "village of twilight." Most parsimonious analysis: *Har'* and *Hara-* are related but distinct forms, with *Har'* being a reduced compound prefix meaning "village" and *Hara-* being a full root meaning "people/we/call."

### The *Har'* Root: "Village/Settlement" (CONFIRMED)

The most significant morphological discovery from the warcraft.wiki.gg Hara'ni language page is the **confirmed translation** of *Har'* = "village/settlement" in four village names:

| Name | Decomposition | Confirmed Translation |
|------|---------------|----------------------|
| *Har'alnor* | *Har'* + *-alnor* | Village of Twilight |
| *Har'athir* | *Har'* + *-thir* | Village of Memory |
| *Har'kuai* | *Har'* + *-kuai* | Village of Rain |
| *Har'mara* | *Har'* + *-mara* | Village of Balance |

This is the **most securely attested morpheme in Hara'ni** — four independent attestations, all with confirmed official translations.

**Relationship to *Hara-* root**: *Har'* (village) may be related to *Hara-* (the ethnonymic root in *Haranir*, *Hara'ni*, *Hara'ti*). If *Hara-* = "people/we" and *Har'* = "village", the semantic connection would be: "village" = "place of the people." However, the apostrophe in *Har'* (marking it as a compound prefix) vs. its absence in *Hara-* (a free root) suggests different morphological status. They may instead be homophonous morphemes with unrelated meanings.

**Confidence: High** for *Har'* = "village/settlement" (four confirmed translations).

### The *Shul'* Root: "Shadow" (Speculative)

If *Shul'ka* = "Hunters of Shadows" (confirmed translation), the decomposition *Shul'* + *-ka* yields *Shul'* = "shadow" and *-ka* = "hunter(s)" or agentive suffix. This is the most natural morpheme break, but the word could also be indivisible.

**Confidence: Low–Moderate** for *Shul'* = "shadow" and *-ka* = "hunter(s)/agentive".

### The *Aln-* Root

The *Aln-* root has four attestations, making it one of the more securely attested morphemes in Hara'ni. *Aln* is a place — the Rift of Aln in Harandar and the Emerald Dream, formed when the Worldsoul was taken from the Cradle. This means *Aln* likely means "the deep/source/rift" rather than just "deep/sacred." The attestations:

| Name | *Aln-* Context | Proposed Function |
|------|----------------|-------------------|
| *Aln'hara* | *Aln'* + *-hara* | The Worldsoul of Azeroth — "song from the deep" or "call from the source" |
| *Alndust* | *Aln-* + *-dust* | Material: "dust from the deep/source" |
| *Alnscorned* | *Aln-* + *-scorned* | Mixed Hara'ni-English: "scorned/corrupted by the deep" |
| *Rift of Aln* | *Aln* | The physical location — the source |

The *Aln-* root likely means "deep/source/rift," consistent with all four attestations. The absence of apostrophe in *Alndust* and *Alnscorned* may reflect phonological conditioning: the following consonant eliminates the need for a glottal stop boundary marker, whereas *Aln'hara* requires one before /h/.

[speculative]: If *Aln-* = "deep/source/rift," it may be connected to the Haranir's subterranean existence and their goddess Aln'hara (the Worldsoul). The root could originally have meant "depth" (physical depth of Harandar) and acquired a sacred connotation through association with the Worldsoul and the World Tree roots.

**Confidence: Moderate** for *Aln* = "deep/source/rift" (four attestations, confirmed in-game geography).

### The *-nir* / *-ni* / *-ti* Suffix Set

Three suffixes appear to form related nominal categories:

| Suffix | Attestation | Proposed Meaning |
|--------|-------------|------------------|
| *-nir* | *Haranir* | "people" / collective ethnonym |
| *-ni* | *Hara'ni* | "language" / "tongue" |
| *-ti* | *Hara'ti* | "group" / "faction" / "those who [do]" |

These three suffixes may reflect a systematic nominal derivation pattern:

| Base | + *-nir* | + *-ni* | + *-ti* |
|------|----------|---------|---------|
| *Hara-* | *Haranir* (the people) | *Hara'ni* (the language) | *Hara'ti* (the faction) |

If this pattern is productive, it would imply:
- *-nir* = collective noun ("people of X")
- *-ni* = language noun ("language/tongue of X")
- *-ti* = agentive/collective ("those who X" or "X-group")

The phonological similarity of *-nir*, *-ni*, and *-ti* (all contain alveolar nasal or stop + high vowel + possibly alveolar consonant) may reflect a common origin or a systematic suffix set.

**Confidence: Low–Moderate** for this suffix set as a systematic nominal derivation pattern.

Note: *Ka'dani* (order of elite hunters from Har'athir) has the *-dani* ending which does not match *-nir*/*-ni*/*-ti*. However, if *-dani* = *-da-* + *-ni*, the *-ni* suffix would appear in a non-Hara-root context, supporting its productivity. Alternatively, *-dani* may be an unrelated suffix. **Confidence: Very Low** for *-ni* in *Ka'dani*.

### The *-dar* Toponymic Suffix

*Harandar* contains the suffix *-dar*, which also appears in Earthen (*Dornogal*) and Arathi (*Beledar*, *Mereldar*) toponyms within Khaz Algar. See earthen/vocabulary.md §8 for full cross-reference analysis.

If *-dar* = *-dor* (Titan "land/realm"), then *Harandar* = "Hara-land" — a toponym formed using a borrowed Titan-derived suffix. This would place Hara'ni toponymic conventions within the Khaz Algar areal sphere.

[speculative]: The *-dar* suffix may have been borrowed from Earthen into Hara'ni during the Haranir's long residence in Khaz Algar, similar to how English borrowed Norman French toponymic elements.

**Confidence: Low–Moderate** for *-dar* as a borrowed toponymic suffix in Hara'ni.

### The *-ia* / *-a* Feminine Suffix (Speculative)

Three confirmed female Haranir names end in *-ia* or phonologically conditioned variants: *Ruia* (-ia), *Ney'leia* (-eia), *On'hiea* (-iea). The surface variation may reflect a single underlying suffix *-ia* /jə/ with phonological conditioning by the preceding vowel:

| Preceding Environment | Surface Form | Example |
|-----------------------|--------------|---------|
| After back vowel (/uː/) | *-ia* /i.ə/ | *Ruia* |
| After mid front vowel (/eɪ/) | *-eia* /eɪ.ə/ | *Ney'leia* |
| After high front vowel (/iː/) | *-iea* /iː.ə/ | *On'hiea* |

Alternatively, the common element may be final *-a* (a feminine suffix), with the preceding *i/e* belonging to the stem: *Rui-* + *-a*, *Ney'lei-* + *-a*, *On'hie-* + *-a*.

No male Haranir names end in *-a* (*Amarakk*, *Hannan*, *Hagar*), but other female names (*Orweyna*, *Gnarla*) also lack *-ia*. The *-a* feminine ending is a widespread Blizzard design pattern across multiple WoW languages (Draenei, Nazja), making it difficult to distinguish genuine morphology from naming aesthetics.

**Confidence: Very Low–Low** for *-ia* / *-a* as a genuine Haranir feminine suffix.

### The *-ar* Element in *Ul'ar*: Potential Nerubian Loanword

The place name *Ul'ar* (*Ul'* + *-ar*) contains an *-ar* suffix that parallels the nerubian *-ar* faction suffix (Anub'ar, Nerub'ar, Hath'ar). Wildcamp Ul'ar is located near the City of Threads in Azj-Kahet, making cultural contact between Haranir and nerubians plausible. However, *-ar* is a common syllable across WoW languages, and no other Haranir-nerubian shared morphemes exist.

If *-ar* were a nerubian loanword, the semantic shift from "brood/collective" (nerubian faction suffix) to "camp/settlement" (Hara'ni place-name element) would be natural. Na'layro's confirmed contact with outsiders provides a social mechanism for loanword transmission.

**Confidence: Very Low** for *-ar* as a nerubian loanword. See vocabulary.md §6 for full analysis.

### Apostrophe-Marked Compounding

The dominant morphological process in attested Hara'ni names is apostrophe-marked compounding:

| Pattern | Examples |
|---------|----------|
| CV'CV.CV | *Ter'kova*, *Nye'rangi* |
| CVC'CV.CV | *Ran'hune* |
| CV.CV'CVC | *Na'layro* |
| CV.CV'CV.CV | *Ney'leia*, *On'hiea* |
| CVC'CV | *Ul'ar*, *Or'lay* |
| CV.CV'CVC | *Aln'hara* |
| CV.CV'CV | *Hara'ni*, *Hara'ti* |
| CV'CVC | *Shul'ka*, *Or'jan* |
| CVC'VCVC | *Dreth'amar* |
| CVC'VCC | *Zur'ashar* |
| CV'CVCCV | *Ka'dani* |
| CVC'VC | *En'liahn* |
| CV.CV'VCV | *Na'bala* |
| CVC'VVCV | *Nai'theren* |
| CVC'VCV | *Ahl'ua* |

All apostrophe positions are medial (between two content morphemes), consistent with nerubian and qiraji patterns. No initial apostrophes (Shath'Yar pattern) are attested.

The compounding pattern appears to be [modifier/prefix] + [head/suffix], consistent with right-headed compounding in other WoW language families. However, the semantic content of most compound elements is unknown, so the headedness cannot be confirmed.

## Relationship to Other WoW Languages

### Confirmed Genetic Relationship to Elven/Troll Family

*The Art of Midnight* (pg. 125) confirms: the Haranir split from dark trolls/night elves approximately 16,000 BDP and journeyed underground. This means Hara'ni is a **deeply divergent branch of the proto-troll/elven language family**.

- Hara'ni diverged from proto-elven before Darnassian, making it potentially the oldest attested branch
- 16,000+ years of independent development have erased obvious cognate recognition
- No shared morphemes are confirmed with Darnassian, Thalassian, Nazja, or Zandali
- The Haranir use Xhosa-accented English like Zandalari trolls, suggesting possible phonological substrate

**Confidence: High** for genetic relationship (confirmed by lore); **Very Low** for specific cognate identification.

### Areal Features

Hara'ni shares three areal features with neighboring Khaz Algar languages:

1. **The *-dar* toponymic suffix** — shared with Earthen and Arathi, likely borrowed
2. **Medial apostrophe compounding** — shared with nerubian, but this is a widespread WoW convention
3. **The *Har'* village-naming convention** — *Har'* = "village" (confirmed) followed by an attribute suffix (*Har'alnor*, *Har'athir*, *Har'kuai*, *Har'mara*) has no parallel in other WoW language families, making it a distinctive Hara'ni feature

### The Apostrophe Convention: Comparison

| Feature | Hara'ni | Nerubian | Qiraji | Ethereal | Shath'Yar |
|---------|---------|----------|--------|----------|-----------|
| Apostrophe position | Medial | Medial | Medial | Both | Initial |
| Apostrophe frequency | Very high | Very high | High | Low | Very high |
| Apostrophe function | Compound boundary | Compound boundary | Compound boundary | Compound boundary | Prefix boundary |
| /h/ frequency | Very high | Low | Low | Low | Low |

The Hara'ni apostrophe pattern is structurally identical to nerubian and qiraji (medial compound boundary), but the languages share no confirmed morphemes. The most likely explanation is that the apostrophe convention is a widespread Blizzard design aesthetic for "alien" names, not evidence of in-universe linguistic contact.

## Limitations

- **No complete Hara'ni sentences are attested.** All Haranir NPCs speak Common in-game.
- **Only ~15 proper names provide linguistic evidence**, plus the Midnight-era terms *Ruutani* and *Alndust*. The entire corpus consists of personal names, place names, and cultural designations.
- **The confirmed translations from warcraft.wiki.gg significantly expand the reliable corpus**, but the total number of confirmed glosses remains small (6 words).
- **The Haranir goddess is confirmed to be Aln'hara** (the Azeroth Worldsoul), resolving a major open question.
- **The Haranir are confirmed to descend from dark trolls/night elves** (*The Art of Midnight*), meaning Hara'ni is not an independent language family but a deeply divergent branch of the elven/troll family.
- **Morpheme decompositions are inferred from patterns**, not confirmed by any canon source (except where noted as confirmed above).
- **The *Har'* root** is now the most attested morpheme (4 confirmed attestations); the *Hara-* root has 5 attestations; the *Aln-* root has 4 attestations; all other morphemes have 1–2 attestations.
- **The Haranir's unique biological origin** ("not really elves, not really trolls") means there are no obvious cognate languages to compare with.
- **The Haranir lived on Mount Hyjal before migrating to Harandar** after 16,000 BDP — this extreme antiquity means any related languages may have diverged beyond recognition.
