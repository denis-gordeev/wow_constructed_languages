Comparative Elven Linguistics

A comparative analysis of the four attested elven languages of Azeroth: Darnassian (Night Elven), Thalassian (High Elven / Blood Elven), Shalassian (Nightborne), and Nazja (Naga). All four descend from a common ancestor — Proto-Elven — spoken before the Great Sundering approximately 10,000 years before the First War.

Methodological caveat: The attested corpus for each language is extremely small (~20–30 items per language). All reconstructions and sound correspondences are provisional. Forms marked in brown are reconstructed; forms marked in gray italics have uncertain glosses.

Cognate Sets

The following sets identify words across the three elven languages that derive from a common Proto-Elven root. Shared roots are highlighted in green.

Analysis: The -dorei suffix is the most securely attested cognate across all four languages. It appears with identical form and meaning, suggesting it has been resistant to sound change — possibly because it is a bound morpheme in high-frequency compound formations. The Nazja attestation Ba'anthalso-dorei! is particularly significant: it confirms that the naga retain this elven morpheme despite 10,000 years of divergent evolution and physiological transformation.

Set 3: Solar/luminous root *belor- / *belore

Proto-ElvenDarnassianThalassianShalassianNazjaGloss
*belore belore (in Fandu-dath-belore) belore(not attested)(not attested)sun / light

Analysis: Belore appears in Darnassian within the sentry challenge Fandu-dath-belore ("Who goes there?") and in Thalassian as "sun" (Anu belore dela'na = "The sun will guide us"). The shared form confirms Proto-Elven *belore. In Darnassian, belore may have shifted semantically from "sun" to a more general "light/revelation" sense (in the sentry challenge, possibly "reveal yourself by the light"), while Thalassian retains the primary meaning "sun." This semantic split parallels the cultural divergence: Night Elves venerate the moon, High/Blood Elves venerate the sun. Nazja's absence of belore is expected given the naga's deep-sea isolation from sunlight.

Analysis: The apostrophe/hyphen distinction is consistent across all four elven languages, strongly suggesting it is an inherited orthographic convention from Proto-Elven, not an independent development. Nazja is especially notable: nearly all multi-morpheme naga names use apostrophes (more consistently than Darnassian), and the hyphen appears in battle cries (Ash-thero-sauguine) mirroring Darnassian phrase-level boundaries (Andu-falah-dor). This is a shared graphological cognate — a feature of the writing system preserved across all daughter languages.

Analysis: The morpheme shal appears in Darnassian only in the World Tree name Shaladrassil, while it is productive in Shalassian as the defining ethnic/cultural morpheme. If Shaladrassil in Darnassian contains the same root, it would mean "twilight" or "shadow" was already a Darnassian lexical item before the Nightborne's isolation. The World Tree in Val'sharah stands in a region of twilight and corruption, which is semantically consistent. Nazja's absence of shal- is unsurprising: the naga abandoned their elven heritage in favor of Azshara-centered and deep-sea vocabulary.

Set 7: Glorification / invocation root *zin-

Proto-ElvenDarnassianThalassianShalassianNazjaGloss
*zin Zin-Azshari(not attested)(not attested)Zin-Azshari adoreglory

Analysis: Zin meaning "glory" is confirmed in Darnassian by the translation of Zin-Azshari as "Glory of Azshara." The Nazja phrase Zin-Azshari adore is a direct retention of this Darnassian formula — naga NPCs invoke the glory of their queen using the same morpheme. This is a clear case of Nazja preserving a Darnassian lexical item in a ritual/religious context.

Set 8: Blessing / benediction *adore

Proto-ElvenDarnassianThalassianShalassianNazjaGloss
*adore Elune-adore(not attested)(not attested)Zin-Azshari adorebe with you / bless

Analysis: The morpheme adore appears in Darnassian as part of Elune-adore ("Elune be with you") and in Nazja as part of Zin-Azshari adore ("Glory of Azshara be with you"). The structural parallel is exact: [deity/glory] + adore. In Nazja, Azshara has replaced Elune as the object of veneration, but the grammatical formula remains identical. This is strong evidence that adore is an inherited Proto-Elven benediction preserved in both languages.

Set 9: Failing/collapse root *sindu

Proto-ElvenDarnassianThalassianShalassianNazjaGloss
*sindu SinduShindu(not attested)(not attested)failing / falling / collapse
Sindu-fallah-norShindu fallah na(both co-occur with fallah/falah)

Analysis: Darnassian Sindu /ˈsɪndu/ (in Sindu-fallah-nor, uncertain gloss — possibly "sleep" or "fallen") and Thalassian Shindu /ˈʃɪndu/ ("failing/breaking" in Shindu fallah na, Shindu sin'dorei) differ only in initial /s/ vs /ʃ/. This is consistent with a regular palatalization *s > ʃ / _i in Thalassian — a typologically common sound change. Both forms co-occur with fallah/falah, strengthening the cognate hypothesis. If confirmed, this would represent the second regular sound change between Darnassian and Thalassian (after *u > e / _# in *andu > ande). However, the Darnassian gloss ("sleep/fallen") and Thalassian gloss ("failing/breaking") show semantic divergence that requires explanation — perhaps a shared root meaning "collapse/fall" with independent semantic specialization. Confidence: Low–Moderate. See Thalassian grammar Investigation 3 for full analysis.

Sound Correspondences

The following regular sound correspondences are observed between the three elven languages. Because the corpus is very small, these are hypotheses, not confirmed rules.

Correspondence 1: Vowel quality before -dorei

EnvironmentDarnassianThalassianShalassianNotes
Before -'dorei Kal'dorei (a) Sin'dorei (i), Quel'dorei (el) Shal'dorei (a) No consistent vowel change; vowels in the modifier element are preserved

Inference: The vowel in the pre-'dorei element is stable across languages. There is no evidence for a systematic vowel shift between Darnassian and Thalassian in this position. The different vowels (a, i, el) reflect different lexical roots, not sound changes.

Correspondence 2: The devoicing hypothesis

Inference: There is no evidence for consonant devoicing between Darnassian and Thalassian. The consonant inventories appear largely identical where comparison is possible.

Correspondence 3: Liquids /l/ and /r/

Proto-ElvenDarnassianThalassianEvidence
*l l l belore, falah / alah, drassil
*r r r dorei (internal), ilisar / thoras

Inference: Liquids are stable. No liquid shifts are attested between Darnassian and Thalassian. This contrasts with many real-world language families where liquid shifts (rhotacism, lambdacism) are common.

Correspondence 4: Semantic divergence despite phonological stability

RootDarnassian senseThalassian senseType of change

Quel noble / high noble / high Stable

Inference: Where phonological form is preserved, semantic shift is the primary mechanism of divergence between Darnassian and Thalassian. This is consistent with a relatively recent separation (~7,300 years) where sound changes have not had time to accumulate significantly, but cultural divergence (lunar vs. solar worship, isolation vs. expansion) has driven lexical-semantic innovation. The sindu/shindu pair may represent a rare case where both sound change and semantic shift co-occur.

Correspondence 5: Palatalization *s > ʃ / _i

Proto-ElvenDarnassianThalassianEvidence
*sindu Sindu /ˈsɪndu/ (in Sindu-fallah-nor) Shindu /ˈʃɪndu/ (in Shindu fallah na) Initial /s/ → /ʃ/ before /i/

Inference: If this correspondence is genuine, it represents a regular palatalization sound change in Thalassian: *s > ʃ / _i. This is a typologically common change (parallel to English ship vs. German Schiff from Proto-Germanic *skip). The palatalization would predict other /s/ → /ʃ/ alternations before front vowels, but no other examples are currently attested. Confidence: Low–Moderate — based on a single cognate pair.

The Andu / Ande Correspondence

A particularly interesting pair is Darnassian Andu (in Andu-falah-dor, "Let balance be restored") and Thalassian Ande (in Ande'thoras-ethil, "May your troubles be diminished").

LanguageFormFunctionPhonological shape
DarnassianAndujussive/imperative particleCV.CV
ThalassianAndeoptative particleCV.CV

The vowel alternation u ~ e may reflect:

  1. A Proto-Elven *andV- with vowel gradation conditioned by the following syllable (ablaut)
  2. A semantic分化: andu = jussive ("let"), ande = optative ("may")
  3. An independent sound change: Proto-Elven *andu > Thalassian ande via u > e / _CV (word-final vowel raising/fronting)

Hypothesis 3 is the most parsimonious. If correct, it represents the only attested regular sound change between Darnassian and Thalassian: word-final *u > e in Thalassian. However, this rests on a single cognate pair and cannot be confirmed.

Conclusion: Darnassian and Thalassian are remarkably conservative in their phonology. The primary divergence is lexical and semantic, not phonological. This is consistent with a relatively recent separation (~7,300 years) and continued cultural contact (the High Elves maintained trade and occasional communication with Kalimdor before the Third War).
