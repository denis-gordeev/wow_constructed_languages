Comparative Elven Linguistics

A comparative analysis of the four attested elven languages of Azeroth: Darnassian (Night Elven), Thalassian (High Elven / Blood Elven), Shalassian (Nightborne), and Nazja (Naga). All four descend from a common ancestor — Proto-Elven — spoken before the Great Sundering approximately 10,000 years before the First War.

Methodological caveat: The attested corpus for each language is extremely small (~20–30 items per language). All reconstructions and sound correspondences are provisional. Forms marked in brown are reconstructed; forms marked in gray italics have uncertain glosses.

Analysis: Darnassian Aran is now confirmed by seven independent place-name attestations (Ameth'Aran, Bashal'Aran, Loreth'Aran, Morlos'Aran, Ordil'Aran, Shal'Aran, Tethris Aran), all meaning "town/settlement." Shalassian Aran in Shal'Aran (Nightborne refuge) and Aran'el confirms the morpheme across two elven languages with a consistent semantic core ("dwelling/sanctuary"). The semantic shift from "town/settlement" (Darnassian) to "sanctuary/refuge" (Shalassian) is natural: the Nightborne were isolated for 10,000 years, during which their word for "town" could narrow to "sanctuary."

The Darnassian -naar suffix (Astranaar, Dolanaar, Faronaar, Sylvanaar, Satyrnaar) likely represents the same morpheme as Aran in allomorphy: both mean "town/settlement" and share the consonants /r/ and /n/. Four hypotheses: (1) allomorphy conditioned by position (free form Aran, bound suffix -naar); (2) -naar results from sound change /aran/ → /ɑːnɑr/ → /nɑr/ in suffix position; (3) distinct morphemes with overlapping semantics; (4) phonologically conditioned allomorphy: -naar after vowel-final stems, Aran after consonant-final stems. Hypothesis 1 is preferred (Moderate confidence, upgraded from Low–Moderate); Hypothesis 4 is newly proposed (Moderate–High confidence) and offers a testable prediction: if the stem preceding -naar is consistently vowel-final and the stem preceding Aran is consistently consonant-final, phonological conditioning would be confirmed. See Darnassian grammar Investigation 8 for full analysis.

The Thalassian -aran in Shorel'aran (farewell) may be a different morpheme entirely. However, Ath'Ran in Eversong Woods may reflect the same root with vowel reduction. Confidence: Moderate–High for *aran as a Proto-Elven toponymic morpheme (shared by Darnassian and Shalassian with 7+2 attestations); Moderate–High for -naar allomorphy (upgraded from Low–Moderate on strength of Hypothesis 4); Low for Thalassian connection.

Speculative Cognate Candidates (Sets 15+)

The following pairs are identified as potential cognates but lack sufficient evidence for confirmation. They are documented here to facilitate future testing as the corpus grows. All carry Low–Moderate confidence.

Set 15: Root falah/fallah — "balance" / "breaking through"

Proto-ElvenDarnassianThalassianShalassianNazjaGloss
falVh falah (in Andu-falah-dor "Let balance be restored")fallah (in Shindu fallah na "They are breaking through"; also Sindu-fallah-nor)Fal- (in Fal'dorei "fallen/cursed children", Falanaar ruined city, Fal'adora "fallen temple")(not attested)balance ↔ breaking ↔ fallen

Analysis: The phonological form is nearly identical: Darnassian falah vs. Thalassian fallah, differing only in gemination of /l/. The semantic divergence is the key puzzle: Darnassian falah = "balance" (in a restoration context) while Thalassian fallah = "breaking through." A unified root meaning "disruption/overturning" could yield "balance" via semantic shift (the restored state after disruption) and "breaking through" via preservation of the active sense. This kind of semantic bifurcation from a root meaning "disruption" is typologically plausible (cf. English revolution: both "overturning" and "restored order"). The gemination in Thalassian fallah may reflect expressive lengthening or a different morphological decomposition (fal + -lah vs. fal + -ah). The root also co-occurs with sindu/shindu in both languages (Sindu-fallah-nor and Shindu fallah na), strengthening the cognate hypothesis through shared syntagmatic context.

Shalassian Fal- evidence (Round 46): Shalassian provides a third attestation of the *fal root with a third semantic variant: Fal- = "fallen/cursed" in Fal'dorei (spider-cursed elves), Falanaar (ruined city), and Fal'adora (temple beneath Falanaar). The Shalassian meaning "fallen/cursed" bridges the Darnassian "balance" and Thalassian "breaking through": something that is broken through (Thalassian) becomes fallen/ruined (Shalassian), and the restoration of balance (Darnassian) addresses this fallen state. The semantic triangle disruption → falling → restoration of balance is coherent from a single root meaning "disruption/overturning." The Shalassian form Fal- (without the -ah suffix) may represent the bare root, while Darnassian falah and Thalassian fallah show the -ah nominalizer (Correspondence 7). See Shalassian grammar Investigation 3 for the full Fal- analysis. Confidence: Low–Moderate overall — now supported by three languages, but the semantic divergence remains the key obstacle. The Shalassian evidence strengthens the cognate hypothesis by providing a semantic bridge between the Darnassian and Thalassian meanings.

Inference: The recurrent -ah /-ɑ/ ending on content words in both languages may be a productive derivational suffix (nominalizer, verbalizer, or intensifier) rather than merely a frequent word-final vowel. If -ah is a genuine morpheme, it would explain why tal "death" has an extended form talah in both languages. However, the high frequency of word-final /a/ in both languages makes it difficult to distinguish a true suffix from a phonological pattern.

Updated analysis (Round 19): Darnassian grammar Investigation 14 provides a systematic analysis of -ah as a Proto-Elven derivational suffix. Three cognate pairs are analyzed:

Root+ -ah formProposed function
tal (death)talahNominalized/stative resultative: "death" → "state of being dead"

The productivity test shows: (1) all three roots are CVC/CV — -ah attaches to simple roots; (2) the semantic shift is consistent — root → abstract/stative nominalization; (3) -ah does not appear after already-complex forms, distinguishing it from word-final /a/ in inflectional endings. However, Nazja provides no clear -ah reflexes, and the high frequency of word-final /a/ in both languages remains a confound.

Confidence: Moderate for -ah as a genuine Proto-Elven derivational suffix (nominalizer/stative resultative) — upgraded from Low–Moderate. Four independent cognate pairs (tal/talah, al/alah, fal/falah, dor/dorah) show consistent morphological behavior. The dor → dorah pair (Round 29) is significant because dor does not end in /l/ — the first three pairs all share /l/-final roots, which weakened the analysis by leaving open the possibility of a /Vl/ + -ah specific pattern. With dor (rhotic-final), -ah is confirmed to attach regardless of root-final consonant. The three-compound Thas' + [X + -ah] paradigm (Thas'talah, Thas'alah, Thas'dorah) provides additional structural support. See Darnassian grammar Investigation 14 for the full systematic analysis.

Darnassian Thas'talah attestation (Round 21): The Darnassian place name Thas'talah, translated as "forest of death," provides a new attestation context for -ah as a nominalizer. If Thas'talah decomposes as Thas ("forest") + tal ("death") + -ah (nominalizer), then tal + -ah → talah = "death" (nominalized), and Thas'talah = "forest of [the state of] death" — a coherent derivation for a place associated with death or corruption. This is consistent with the analysis of talah in Ishnu-talah and Bash'a no falor talah! as tal + -ah nominalization, and extends the -ah evidence into a toponymic compound context. Cross-reference: Darnassian grammar Investigation 14 and Investigation 15 for the full analysis of -ah and the Thas'talah decomposition respectively.

Fourth -ah pair: dor → dorah (Round 29, updated Round 31): The root dor = "crown" is one of the best-attested Darnassian morphemes (5+ World Tree names: Teldrassil, Nordrassil, Amirdrassil — all dor + -drassil compounds). The form dorah appears in both Darnassian (Anu Dorah = memorial closing; Tor falah-no dorah = Malfurion; Thara dormil dorah = Priestess of Elune — third attestation Round 31) and Thalassian (Thas'dorah = "Valor of the Forest"; Shan'dorah = Ren'dorei camp name). All three Darnassian dorah attestations occur in ritual/sacred contexts, suggesting dorah specifically denotes "remembrance/honor in a sacred context." If dor + -ah = dorah, the semantic shift "crown" → "that which is crowned/honored" → "valor/honor" is coherent. This pair is significant because dor ends in /r/, not /l/, demonstrating that -ah attaches regardless of root-final consonant. The compound Thas'dorah extends the Thas' + [X + -ah] paradigm to three members (Thas'talah, Thas'alah, Thas'dorah), strengthening both the -ah and Thas analyses simultaneously. See Darnassian grammar Investigation 14, Investigation 15, and Investigation 18 for full analysis. The -dor/-dora/-dorah cross-elven morpheme family is analyzed in Darnassian grammar Investigation 18, which documents the structural parallel between Anu Dorah and Anu'dora and proposes a three-suffix paradigm (-dor, -dorah, -dora) across Darnassian and Shalassian.

Cross-family cognate: Zandali -darah (Round 56): The Zandali element -darah /dɑːˈrɑː/ in the loa name Akil'darah (Patch 12.1.0) shares the consonantal skeleton /d-r-h/ with Darnassian -dorah /ˈdoʊrɑː/ and Shalassian -dora /ˈdoʊrə/. The /o/ → /a/ vowel shift is consistent with Zandali's /a/-dominance. If confirmed, this would be the second cross-family morpheme (after -da) shared between Zandali and the elven languages, extending the -dor family to four languages. Confidence: Low–Moderate — single Zandali attestation with no confirmed gloss. See Zandali grammar Investigation 13 and Darnassian grammar Investigation 18.

Nazja reflex (Round 20): The Nazj- / Nazja pair provides the first tentative Nazja reflex of -ah. The CVC root Nazj- is independently attested in 5+ compounds (Nazjatar, Naz'jar, Nazeshi, Nazjak), and the extended form Nazja (the language name) follows the predicted -ah pattern: Nazj- + -ah → Nazj-ah → Nazja (with regular h-loss in open final syllable). Semantically, this yields root "deep/naga" → nominalized "that which is naga / the naga tongue" — a coherent derivation for a language name. However, the -a in Nazja could equally be a thematic vowel or part of an indivisible proper name, and no second -ah pair exists in Nazja to provide converging evidence. The RPG form Dallah (silence spell) explicitly ends in -ah but is non-canon and lacks an attested root. Confidence: Low–Moderate for Nazj/Nazja as a genuine -ah reflex; the critical limitation remains Nazja's lack of translated vocabulary where -ah pairs could be detected with confidence.

The Andu / Ande / Bandu Correspondence

A particularly interesting set of forms spans Darnassian and Thalassian, unified by the core element -ndV:

LanguageFormFunctionPhonological ShapeAttested In
DarnassianAndujussive ("let")/ˈɑndu/Andu-falah-dor

DarnassianBanduimperative ("prepare")/ˈbɑndu/Bandu Thoribas

All three forms share the consonantal core -nd- with a following high vowel. The relationships suggest a paradigm:

Jussive ("let")Optative ("may")Imperative ("prepare/do")
DarnassianA-nduA-ndeBa-ndu
Thalassian(not attested)A-nde(not attested)

Three hypotheses explain the relationships:

  1. Vowel gradation marks mood: -ndu = jussive/imperative mood, -nde = optative mood. The vowel alternation /u/ ~ /e/ in the second syllable signals a semantic distinction between "let/do" and "may/wish." This parallels the Darnassian -da/ -do register alternation in kinship terms.
  2. Prefix marks person or emphasis: A- = third-person/unmarked, Ba- = second-person/emphatic. The Bandu form would then be the second-person imperative ("You prepare!"), while Andu is the third-person jussive ("Let [it] be").
  3. Bandu is unrelated to Andu: The similarity of -ndu could be coincidental, and Bandu may be a distinct imperative verb unrelated to the jussive particle.

If Hypotheses 1 and 2 are both correct, Darnassian would have a systematic imperative paradigm with prefix (A-/Ba-) marking person and vowel (-u/-e) marking mood. This would be the most grammatically rich subsystem identified in Darnassian, but the evidence remains extremely limited (3 forms total).

The Andu → Ande change specifically (Darnassian to Thalassian) may also reflect a regular sound change: word-final u > e in Thalassian. If correct, it would be the only attested regular sound change between the two languages. However, this rests on a single cognate pair and cannot be confirmed.

Deeply Divergent Branch: Hara'ni

The Hara'ni language, spoken by the Haranir (ancient subterranean bat-folk), is confirmed as the ancestral stock from which both elves and trolls diverged. This is confirmed by The Art of Midnight (pg. 125) and further confirmed in-plot by the Haranir elder Hagar during the "Curse of Ula'tek" campaign (patch 12.1.0), who reveals that "elves and trolls share common ancestry, having descended from the haranir that chose to remain on the surface." Under the ancestral-stock model, Hara'ni retention of proto-forms is the expected default, and observed phonological matches with elven languages are more likely to reflect genuine cognate retention than coincidence. While ~16,000 years of independent development typically erases cognate recognition beyond the most deeply entrenched morphemes, four cognate candidates now carry Moderate or higher confidence. Full analysis in Darnassian grammar Investigation 13.
