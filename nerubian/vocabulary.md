# Nerubian Vocabulary

All entries are attested in World of Warcraft canon (NPC dialogue, quest text, in-game text, official Blizzard sources) unless marked. Inferences are marked with [inferred]. Pre-TWW entries are from Wrath of the Lich King and other expansions.

The Nerubian language is spoken by the nerubians — an ancient arachnid race native to Northrend and, as revealed in *The War Within* (2024), the subterranean realm of Azj-Kahet. Their language has no confirmed relationship to the elven or titan-forged language families.

## 3. Attested Phrases

**No constructed-language nerubian phrases are attested.** All nerubian NPCs speak Common (English) in-game. No nerubian-language dialogue, battle cries, or incantations have been recorded.

This is a notable absence compared to naga (who have ~15 attested phrases in Nazja) and vrykul (who have ~10 attested phrases). The nerubian language is known entirely from proper names.

## 5. The *Azj-* / *Nazj-* Connection

The prefix *Azj-* in nerubian place names (*Azj-Kahet*, *Azjol-Nerub*) and *Nazj-* / *Nazj-* in naga place names (*Nazjatar*, *Nazja* language) share a phonological shape that may not be coincidental.

### Nerubian *Azj-* Attestations

| Name | Expansion | Analysis |
|------|-----------|----------|
| *Azjol-Nerub* | WotLK (2008) | *Azj-* + *-ol-* + *Nerub*. The first element of the ancient nerubian kingdom in Northrend. |
| *Azj-Kahet* | TWW (2024) | *Azj-* + *-Kahet*. The nerubian kingdom in Khaz Algar. |

### Naga *Nazj-* Attestations

| Name | Expansion | Analysis |
|------|-----------|----------|
| *Nazjatar* | BfA (2018) | *Nazj-* + *-atar*. The naga capital. |
| *Nazja* | Lore | The naga language name. |
| *Naz'jar* | Cata (2010) | *Naz-* + *-jar*. A naga tribe. |
| *Nazeshi* | BfA (2018) | *Naz-* + *-eshi*. A naga tribe. |
| *Nazjak* | Classic | *Naz-* + *-jak*. A naga prince. |

### Analysis

**Arguments for a connection:**

1. **Phonological identity:** *Azj-* and *Nazj-* share the sequence /æz(dʒ)/, differing only by the initial /n/ in the naga form. If *Nazj-* = *N-* + *Azj-*, then *Nazjatar* = *N-azj-atar* and *Azj-Kahet* = *Azj-kahet*, with the same root *Azj-* meaning "deep" or "realm below."

2. **Aqir ancestry:** Both nerubians and naga (via the qiraji/n'raqi) descend from the Aqir, the ancient insectoid race created by the Old Gods. If the *Azj-* element was an Aqir word meaning "deep" or "home," it could have been inherited by both descendant species.

3. **Ahn'kahet — *-kahet* shared suffix:** The WotLK dungeon *Ahn'kahet* (the Old Kingdom) shares the *-kahet* suffix with TWW's *Azj-Kahet*. This means the *-kahet* morpheme has been attested in nerubian naming since 2008, and *Azj-Kahet* is not a TWW invention but a continuation of an established naming pattern. The alternation *Ahn-* vs. *Azj-* before *-kahet* may reflect:
   - Two different morphemes (*Ahn-* vs. *Azj-*)
   - A sound change (*Ahn-* → *Azj-* or vice versa)
   - Two different nerubian dialects (Northrend vs. Khaz Algar)

4. **Old God substrate:** Both nerubians and naga have been influenced by Old Gods (N'Zoth for naga; historically C'Thun/Yogg-Saron for nerubians). The *Azj-* element could be a Shath'Yar loanword that entered both languages independently through Old God contact.

**Arguments against a connection:**

1. **Different language families:** Nazja is an elven language (derived from Darnassian). The nerubian language has no confirmed relationship to the elven family. A shared morpheme between unrelated languages requires explanation through borrowing or substrate, not inheritance.

2. **Geographic and temporal separation:** Nerubians are from Northrend/Khaz Algar; naga are from the Maelstrom/Nazjatar. The two species have had no documented contact for millennia.

3. **Blizzard design convergence:** The similarity may be a Blizzard naming convention rather than a linguistic connection. Both *Azj-* and *Nazj-* evoke an "alien/ancient" aesthetic that Blizzard has used for multiple Old God-related species.

### Assessment

The *Azj-* / *Nazj-* connection is **intriguing but unconfirmed**. The discovery that *Azj-* appears in *Azjol-Nerub* (WotLK, 2008) — predating *Nazjatar* (BfA, 2018) — strengthens the case, as it shows Blizzard has used *Azj-* for nerubian naming since Wrath. If the Aqir hypothesis is correct, *Azj-* could be one of the few surviving Aqir morphemes, preserved in both the nerubian and naga descendants.

**Confidence: Low–Medium.** The phonological match is strong, and the Aqir ancestry provides a plausible transmission mechanism, but the lack of any confirmed Aqir vocabulary makes this unconfirmable.

## 6. The *-kahet* Suffix: A Persistent Nerubian Morpheme

The suffix *-kahet* appears in two nerubian place names across two expansions:

| Name | Expansion | Analysis |
|------|-----------|----------|
| *Ahn'kahet* | WotLK | "The Old Kingdom" — a dungeon in Dragonblight. *Ahn-* + *-kahet*. |
| *Azj-Kahet* | TWW | The nerubian kingdom in Khaz Algar. *Azj-* + *-kahet*. |

If *-kahet* = "kingdom" or "realm," then:
- *Ahn'kahet* = "[Ahn]-kingdom" = "The Old Kingdom" (matches the in-game designation)
- *Azj-Kahet* = "[Azj]-kingdom" = "The Deep Kingdom" or "The Domain-Kingdom"

This would make *-kahet* one of the most securely attested nerubian morphemes, with two independent attestations spanning 16 years of game development.

**Confidence: Moderate–High.** The repeated appearance of *-kahet* with the same semantic field (kingdom/realm) in two unrelated expansion contexts is strong evidence for a genuine morpheme.

## 7. The *Anub-* Prefix: A Persistent Nerubian Morpheme

The prefix *Anub-* appears in four nerubian names across multiple expansions:

| Name | Expansion | Role |
|------|-----------|------|
| *Anub'arak* | WotLK | King of Azjol-Nerub |
| *Anub'rekhan* | WotLK (Naxxramas) | Crypt lord |
| *Anub'ess* | Classic | Nerubian in Silithus |
| *Anub'azal* | TWW | Military commander |

The *Anub-* prefix clearly evokes Egyptian *Anubis* (the jackal-headed god of mummification and the afterlife). In WoW, all *Anub-* named nerubians are male and hold positions of military or leadership significance. This suggests:

- *Anub-* may mean "lord," "warrior," or "commander" in the nerubian language
- *Anub-* may be a title prefix rather than a personal name element
- The Egyptian association is deliberate Blizzard design, reflecting the Egyptian-inspired aesthetic of nerubian culture (pyramids, scarabs, underworld themes)

**Confidence: High** for the morpheme's existence; **Low** for the specific meaning.

## 8. Phonological Observations

### Sound Inventory (Observed from Attested Names)

**Consonants**: b, d, f, h, j, k, l, m, n, p, r, s, t, th (/θ/), v, x (/ks/), z, sh (/ʃ/), zh (/ʒ/)

**Vowels**: a, e, i, o, u

### Key Phonological Features

1. **Apostrophe as morpheme boundary**: The most distinctive orthographic feature of nerubian naming. Nearly all multi-morpheme names use apostrophes: *Anub'azal*, *Arak'nai*, *Y'tekhi*, *Ovi'nax*, *Ky'veza*, *Krix'vizk*, *Ix'zek*, *Rasha'nan*. This is more consistent than Darnassian/Nazja apostrophe usage.

2. **Sibilant frequency**: High, though less extreme than Nazja. Names like *Nizrek*, *Krix'vizk*, *Xanventh*, *Rasha'nan* feature prominent sibilants.

3. **The /ks/ (x) phoneme**: Unusually common in nerubian names compared to other WoW languages: *Ulgrax*, *Ovi'nax*, *Krix'vizk*, *Ix'zek*, *Vrrix*, *Hadronox*. This may reflect:
   - The chitinous clicking sound of arachnid vocalization
   - A Blizzard aesthetic choice to make nerubian names sound "hard" and "alien"
   - An Old God substrate influence (cf. Shath'Yar's harsh phonology)

4. **Initial clusters**: Nerubian tolerates complex initial clusters: /vr/ (*Vrrix*), /kr/ (*Krix'vizk*), /ks/ or /ɪks/ (*Ix'zek*). This is similar to Vrykul cluster tolerance.

5. **The /θ/ phoneme**: Appears in *Xanventh* and *Krik'thir*, shared with Titan-forged languages (Vrykul, Earthen) and Darnassian. May reflect a deep Azerothian areal feature rather than genetic inheritance.

6. **Vowel patterns**: Names favor /a/ and /e/ heavily, with /i/ common in unstressed syllables. /o/ and /u/ are relatively rare. This pattern is shared with Nazja, though whether through common ancestry or convergence is unclear.

### Comparison with Nazja Phonology

| Feature | Nerubian | Nazja | Analysis |
|---------|----------|-------|----------|
| Apostrophe usage | Extremely consistent (*Anub'azal*, *Ovi'nax*) | Consistent (*Sira'kess*, *Naz'jar*) | Both use apostrophe as morpheme boundary; nerubian usage is slightly more regular |
| /ʒ/ (zh) frequency | Low (not clearly attested in names) | Very high (diagnostic of Nazja) | Nazja innovated /ʒ/; nerubians did not |
| /dʒ/ (j) frequency | Low | Very high | Nazja has many /dʒ/ names; nerubian does not |
| /ks/ (x) frequency | High (*Ulgrax*, *Ovi'nax*) | Absent | Distinctive nerubian feature not shared with Nazja |
| Sibilant density | Moderate (*Nizrek*, *Krix'vizk*) | Very high (*Sira'kess*, *Sassz'ine*) | Nazja has more extreme sibilant usage |
| Vowel preference | a, e, i dominant | a, e, i dominant | Shared pattern, possibly coincidental |

## 9. Relationship to Other Languages

### Nerubian — Nazja (Naga) Connection

The *Azj-* / *Nazj-* connection is analyzed in §5 above. Beyond this single morpheme, the two languages show significant structural differences (different phoneme inventories, different suffix systems, different compounding patterns). The shared *Azj-* element, if genuine, would be a "fossil word" from the Aqir substrate — not evidence of a genetic relationship between the languages themselves.

### Nerubian — Tol'vir Connection

Both nerubians and Tol'vir share an Egyptian-inspired aesthetic:

| Feature | Nerubian | Tol'vir |
|---------|----------|---------|
| *Anub-* prefix | *Anub'arak*, *Anub'azal* | Not attested |
| *Nefer-* element | *Neferess* | *Neferset* (*Nefer* + *-set*) |
| Egyptian design | Pyramids, underworld, mummification | Pyramids, sun worship, pharaohs |

The *Nefer-* element appears in both: nerubian *Neferess* (former queen) and Tol'vir *Neferset* (tribe name). If *Nefer-* = "beautiful" (Egyptian *nfr*), this would be a shared morpheme. However, both may independently borrow from the same Egyptian inspiration rather than sharing a genetic morpheme.

**Confidence: Very Low** for a genuine linguistic connection; **Medium** for a shared Blizzard design inspiration.

### Nerubian — Shath'Yar (Old God) Connection

Several nerubian names have features consistent with Shath'Yar influence:

- Initial apostrophe (*Y'tekhi*) — paralleled in Shath'Yar (*N'Zoth*, *N'raqi*, *K'thir*)
- Harsh consonant clusters (*Krix'vizk*, *Ix'zek*) — consistent with Shath'Yar phonology
- The *Azj-* element itself may be an Aqir/Old God substrate word

The nerubians were created by the Old Gods (through the Aqir) and some were later corrupted by Yogg-Saron. It is plausible that their language retains some Shath'Yar substrate vocabulary.

**Confidence: Low** for direct Shath'Yar loanwords; **Moderate** for Old God phonological influence.

### Nerubian Language Family: Hypothetical

```
Aqir (unattested; Old God-created insectoid race)
├── Nerubian (via Nerubian spider-people)
│   ├── Northrend dialect (Azjol-Nerub, Ahn'kahet)
│   └── Khaz Algar dialect (Azj-Kahet, City of Threads)
├── Qiraji (via Silithid → Qiraji; unattested as language)
└── [Possible substrate in Nazja]
    └── *Azj-* / *Nazj-* element (if Aqir origin)
```

Unlike the Titan-forged language family (which has confirmed genetic relationships), the Aqir-descended language family is entirely hypothetical. Only the *Azj-* element provides any evidence for shared vocabulary, and even this is uncertain.

## 10. Writing System

Nerubians are known to have a written language. In WotLK, nerubian script appears on various objects and architecture in Azjol-Nerub. The script appears to be glyphic or hieroglyphic, consistent with the Egyptian aesthetic.

Unlike Tol'vir hieroglyphs (which have three confirmed translations: *Kadesh* = "Swing", *Osadi* = "Strike", *Satekh* = "Block"), no nerubian hieroglyphs have been translated in canon.
