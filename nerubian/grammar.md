# Nerubian Grammar

Grammar rules below are **inferred from attested examples only**. The Nerubian language has no attested sentences — the entire corpus consists of proper names and place names spanning *Wrath of the Lich King* (2008) and *The War Within* (2024). Patterns are noted where multiple examples support a rule. Speculative extensions are marked [speculative].

## Phonology

### Sound Inventory (Observed from Attested Names)

**Consonants**: b, d, f, h, j, k, l, m, n, p, r, s, t, th (/θ/), v, x (/ks/), z, sh (/ʃ/), zh (/ʒ/)

**Vowels**: a, e, i, o, u

### Phonological Patterns

1. **Apostrophe as consistent morpheme boundary**: The most distinctive structural feature of nerubian naming. Nearly all multi-morpheme names use apostrophes to mark morpheme boundaries:
   - *Anub'azal*, *Anub'arak*, *Anub'rekhan*, *Anub'ess* (four attestations of *Anub-* + suffix)
   - *Arak'nai*, *Ovi'nax*, *Ky'veza*, *Krix'vizk*, *Ix'zek*, *Rasha'nan* (TWW compounds)
   - *Krik'thir*, *Anok'ra* (WotLK compounds)

   This is more consistent than Darnassian/Nazja, where some compounds lack apostrophes (*Kaldorei*, *Teldrassil*). The nerubian apostrophe convention may reflect a more regular orthographic tradition.

2. **The /ks/ (x) phoneme**: Unusually frequent in nerubian names compared to other WoW languages:
   - *Ulgrax*, *Ovi'nax*, *Hadronox* (word-final /ks/)
   - *Krix'vizk*, *Ix'zek* (medial /ks/ in first element)
   - *Vrrix* (doubled consonant + /ks/)
   
   The /ks/ cluster may reflect the chitinous clicking sounds of arachnid vocalization, or it may be a Blizzard aesthetic choice for "hard" alien names.

3. **Sibilant frequency**: Moderate. Names like *Nizrek*, *Krix'vizk*, *Rasha'nan* feature sibilants but less densely than Nazja (*Sira'kess*, *Sassz'ine*).

4. **Consonant cluster tolerance**: High, especially in initial position: /vr/ (*Vrrix*), /kr/ (*Krix'vizk*), /ɪks/ (*Ix'zek*). This approaches Vrykul cluster complexity.

5. **Dental fricative /θ/**: Appears in *Xanventh* and *Krik'thir*. Also found in Titan-forged languages (Vrykul, Earthen) and Darnassian. May be an areal feature of Azerothian languages rather than genetic inheritance.

6. **Vowel distribution**: Names favor /a/ and /e/ heavily, with /i/ common in unstressed syllables. /o/ and /u/ are relatively rare, similar to Nazja.

7. **Syllable structure**: Tends toward (C)(C)V(C)(C), allowing complex onsets and codas:
   - CV: *Izo*
   - CVC: *Nizrek*
   - CCVCC: *Vrrix*
   - CVC'CVC: *Krix'vizk*

## Morphology

### Compounding with Apostrophe Boundary

The most productive morphological pattern in nerubian is the apostrophe-marked compound:

| Pattern | Examples |
|---------|----------|
| *Anub-* + suffix | *Anub'azal*, *Anub'arak*, *Anub'rekhan*, *Anub'ess* |
| Initial + suffix | *Arak'nai*, *Ovi'nax*, *Ky'veza*, *Rasha'nan* |
| Initial + suffix | *Krik'thir*, *Anok'ra* |
| Initial consonant cluster + suffix | *Krix'vizk*, *Ix'zek* |
| Initial vowel + suffix | *Y'tekhi* |

The pattern is consistently: [modifier/prefix] + [head/suffix], with the apostrophe marking the boundary. This right-headed compounding pattern is shared with the Titan-forged language family, though whether through inheritance or convergence is unknown.

### The *Anub-* Prefix

The *Anub-* prefix is the most securely attested nerubian morpheme, appearing in four names across two expansions:

| Name | Expansion | Role |
|------|-----------|------|
| *Anub'arak* | WotLK | King of Azjol-Nerub |
| *Anub'rekhan* | WotLK | Crypt lord |
| *Anub'ess* | Classic | Nerubian in Silithus |
| *Anub'azal* | TWW | Military commander |

All *Anub-* named nerubians are male and hold positions of military or leadership significance. *Anub-* may therefore mean:
- "lord" or "warrior" (a title prefix)
- "servant of [the Old Gods]" (a religious designation)
- A proper name element with no independent meaning

[speculative]: If *Anub-* is a title prefix (like Vrykul *Drottinn* or Drust *Gorak*), then *Anub'arak* would mean "Lord Arak" or "Warrior-Arak," and *Anub'azal* would mean "Lord Azal" or "Warrior-Azal." This interpretation is consistent with the fact that all *Anub-* bearers are military leaders.

### The *-kahet* Suffix

The suffix *-kahet* appears in two nerubian place names:

| Name | Expansion | Context |
|------|-----------|---------|
| *Ahn'kahet* | WotLK | "The Old Kingdom" — dungeon in Dragonblight |
| *Azj-Kahet* | TWW | The nerubian kingdom in Khaz Algar |

If *-kahet* = "kingdom" or "realm":
- *Ahn'kahet* = "[Ahn]-kingdom" → "The Old Kingdom" (matches in-game designation)
- *Azj-Kahet* = "[Azj]-kingdom" → "The Deep Kingdom" or "The Domain-Kingdom"

This is one of the strongest nerubian morpheme inferences, supported by two independent attestations with consistent semantics.

### The *-rek* / *-urek* Suffix

| Name | Analysis |
|------|----------|
| *Nizrek* | *Niz-* + *-rek* |
| *Ansurek* | *Ansur-* + *-ek* (or *Ans-* + *-urek*) |

The *-rek* / *-urek* element may be a title or rank suffix. If *-rek* is related to *-rekhan* in *Anub'rekhan*, then the suffix has three attestations:
- *Nizrek* (*-rek*)
- *Ansurek* (*-urek* variant)
- *Anub'rekhan* (*-rekhan* extended form)

[speculative]: If *-rek* = "advisor/vizier" and *-urek* = "ruler/queen," then *Nizrek* = "Advisor Niz" and *Ansurek* = "Queen Ansur." The semantic difference between *-rek* and *-urek* could reflect a gender distinction or rank distinction.

### The *-nax* / *-ax* Suffix

| Name | Analysis |
|------|----------|
| *Ovi'nax* | *Ovi-* + *-nax* |
| *Ulgrax* | *Ulgr-* + *-ax* |
| *Hadronox* | *Hadron-* + *-ox* |

Three names end in the /æks/ or /ɒks/ sound. This may be:
- A rank or role suffix (e.g., "broodtwister" or "devourer")
- A coincidence (the sound is common in harsh-sounding names)
- Related to the *-nai* suffix in *Arak'nai* through a phonological alternation

**Confidence: Very Low.**

### The *-zek* / *-rek* Parallel

| Name | Suffix |
|------|--------|
| *Ix'zek* | *-zek* |
| *Nizrek* | *-rek* |

The phonological similarity between *-zek* and *-rek* (voiceless vs. voiced final consonant) may reflect:
- A common suffix with phonologically conditioned voicing
- Two different suffixes that happen to sound similar
- Coincidence

[speculative]: If *-zek* and *-rek* are the same suffix with voicing alternation, the distribution could reflect a grammatical distinction (e.g., nominative *-rek* vs. genitive *-zek*, or singular *-rek* vs. plural *-zek*).

## Syntax

- No sentence-level attestation exists
- Word order cannot be determined
- The compounding pattern (prefix + head, with apostrophe boundary) is the only confirmed syntactic observation
- The language may follow a modifier-head structure in compounds (inferred from *Anub-* + suffix pattern), consistent with the right-headed compounding tradition found in both Titan-forged and Aqir-descended languages

## Relationship to Other WoW Languages

### The Aqir Substrate Hypothesis

The nerubians descend from the Aqir, an ancient insectoid race created by the Old Gods. The Qiraji (of Silithus) and the Nerubians are the two known Aqir-descended civilizations. If the Aqir had a language, both Nerubian and Qiraji would be expected to preserve elements of it.

The *Azj-* element is the primary evidence for an Aqir substrate:
- *Azjol-Nerub* (nerubian, WotLK) — *Azj-* + *-ol-* + *Nerub*
- *Azj-Kahet* (nerubian, TWW) — *Azj-* + *-Kahet*
- *Nazjatar* (naga, BfA) — *Nazj-* + *-atar* (if *Nazj-* = *N-* + *Azj-*)

If *Azj-* is an Aqir word, it would be a "fossil morpheme" — preserved in both descendant languages but with different phonological development (*Azj-* in nerubian, *Nazj-* in naga with initial *N-* prefix).

### The *Ahn-* vs. *Azj-* Alternation

The place names *Ahn'kahet* and *Azj-Kahet* share *-kahet* but differ in the initial element. Possible explanations:

1. **Different morphemes**: *Ahn-* = "old" (if *Ahn'kahet* = "Old Kingdom") and *Azj-* = "deep/domain" (if *Azj-Kahet* = "Deep Kingdom"). Simplest analysis; no sound change needed.

2. **Dialectal variation from shared root**: A proto-nerubian form like *\*Anzj-* could have simplified to *Ahn-* in Northrend and fronted to *Azj-* in Khaz Algar. Speculative but possible.

3. **Ahn- is not nerubian**: *Ahn'kahet* may be an Aqir or qiraji name rather than native nerubian, since the similar element *Ahn-* appears in *Ahn'qiraj* (a qiraji place name).

**Confidence: Low.** The data is insufficient to distinguish between these hypotheses.

### The *Nefer-* Element: Nerubian–Tol'vir Connection

The element *Nefer-* appears in both nerubian (*Neferess*) and Tol'vir (*Neferset*). Both are phonologically identical (/nɛfɛr/) and both reflect Blizzard's Egyptian-inspired naming aesthetic (cf. Egyptian *nfr* = "beautiful/good").

However, this is almost certainly a shared **design inspiration** rather than a genuine in-universe linguistic connection:

- Nerubian has no confirmed relationship to the titan-forged language family (which includes Tol'vir)
- No other shared morphemes exist between nerubian and Tol'vir (*Anub-* is nerubian-only; *-kahet* is nerubian-only; *Vir'-*, *-et* are Tol'vir-only)
- Both species have Egyptian aesthetics, and *Nefer-* is the most recognizable Egyptian morpheme

**Confidence: Very Low** for a genuine linguistic connection; **High** for shared Blizzard design inspiration.

### Comparison with Titan-Forged Compounding

Both nerubian and titan-forged languages use right-headed compounding with apostrophe boundaries:

| Language | Example | Pattern |
|----------|---------|---------|
| Nerubian | *Anub'azal* | Prefix + suffix |
| Titan | *Khaz'goroth* | Prefix + suffix |
| Vrykul | *Val'kyr* | Prefix + suffix |
| Darnassian | *Kaldorei* | Modifier + head |

This shared pattern may reflect:
1. Independent development (apostrophe-marked compounding is a common fantasy convention)
2. A deep Azerothian areal feature (shared through contact, not inheritance)
3. Blizzard's consistent design language across WoW naming

The similarity is suggestive but cannot establish a genetic relationship.

## Limitations

The nerubian corpus is extremely small:
- ~16 personal names (from WotLK and TWW combined)
- ~7 place names with nerubian etymologies
- **0 attested phrases or sentences**

No verb forms, particles, or syntactic structures are known. The only confirmed morphological patterns are:
1. Apostrophe-marked compounding (high confidence)
2. *Anub-* prefix (high confidence)
3. *-kahet* suffix (moderate confidence)
4. *-rek* / *-urek* suffix (low confidence)

All other morpheme identifications are speculative.
