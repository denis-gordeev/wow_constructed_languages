Attested Text

A single attested nerubian-language text survives — writing on a nerubian scroll:
"Unhhh-tikk-a-shisss. Kka shir. Na-l bkah a-shisss. Kreesh kik ik ta. Sohk-a tik hiss a-k-k-k."

This is the only attested nerubian-language sentence (or sequence of phrases). Key observations:

FeatureObservationAnalysis
Repeated *-shisss* / *a-shisss*Appears twice: *tikk-a-shisss*, *bkah a-shisss*If *-shisss* is a suffix/particle, repetition suggests grammatical agreement or parallel structure
Hyphens as morpheme boundaries*tikk-a-shisss*, *Na-l*, *Sohk-a*, *a-k-k-k*Unlike proper names (apostrophes), scroll text uses hyphens for word-internal boundaries; suggests nerubian writing distinguishes compound names from grammatical boundaries
Hissing/sibilant phonology*shisss*, *Kreesh*, *hiss*, *shir*Consistent with arachnid nature; supports inference that sibilant-heavy phonology is genuine, not just a naming convention
Short utterancesFive phrases separated by periodsMay reflect incantation, ritual text, or formal register rather than everyday speech
Potential morphology*Unhhh-* = interjection?; *tikk-a-shisss* = *tikk-* + *-a-* + *-shisss*; *Na-l* = *Na* + *-l* suffix; *a-k-k-k* = trilled/geminate /k/Very speculative

Confidence: Very Low for any specific gloss; High that this is genuine nerubian-language text.

The Azj- / Nazj- Connection

Key finding: The prefix Azj- in nerubian place names and Nazj- in naga place names may reflect a shared Aqir substrate morpheme.
NerubianNagaShared Element
*Azj-Kahet* (TWW)*Nazjatar* (BfA)*Azj-* / *Nazj-*
*Azjol-Nerub* (WotLK)*Nazja* (language)*Azj-* / *Nazj-*
*Azj'Aqir* (Aqir empire)—*Azj-* + *Aqir* self-designation
*Naz'jar* (tribe)*Naz-*
*Nazeshi* (tribe)*Naz-*

If Nazj- = N- + Azj-, then the naga form adds an initial /n/ prefix to the same root. This could reflect an Aqir morpheme meaning "deep" or "realm," preserved in both descendant languages after 10,000+ years of divergence. The discovery of Azj'Aqir — the original Aqir empire name directly containing the Aqir self-designation after the *Azj-* prefix — significantly strengthens the case, as it shows *Azj-* was used by the Aqir themselves before their divergence into nerubians and qiraji. Confidence: Medium.

The *En-* Prefix: *En'kilah*

The WotLK place name En'kilah contains the element *En-*, which would be a third nerubian toponymic prefix alongside *Azj-* and *Ahn-*. However, the building's original architects are not definitively identified as nerubian, and *-kilah* strongly resembles Arabic *qila* / *kil'a* ("fortress/citadel"), suggesting a possible Blizzard design borrowing.

The Nefer- Element: Nerubian ↔ Tol'vir

Assessment: The Nefer- element in nerubian Neferess and Tol'vir Neferset is almost certainly a shared Blizzard design inspiration from Egyptian nfr ("beautiful/good"), not a genuine in-universe linguistic connection. No other morphemes are shared between the two languages. Confidence: Very Low for genuine connection; High for shared design inspiration.
NameLanguageContextDecomposition
NeferessNerubianFormer queen of Azj-Kahet*Nefer-* + *-ess*
NefersetTol'virTol'vir tribe in Uldum*Nefer-* + *-set*

Both forms share the exact sequence /nɛfɛr/. If drawn from Egyptian nfr ("beautiful"): Neferess ≈ "the beautiful one"; Neferset ≈ "beautiful place." However, the completely different suffix systems (*-ess* vs. *-set*) and lack of any other shared morphemes between nerubian and Tol'vir strongly suggest independent Blizzard design choices.
